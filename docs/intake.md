# Intake

Sub-phase 2.6, built by [`docs/porting/maps/2.6.md`](porting/maps/2.6.md) under owner decisions 85 to 99 and 120 to 123 (`docs/HANDOFF.md`), ADR 0006 ("Intake words of sub-phase 2.6") and ADR 0002 ("Consequences": an intake request is not a work item). This page records what 2.6a built (the model, internal forms, Settings → Intake and its form builder, the Intake page with every triage action, the notify list and the two jobs) and what 2.6b added: public forms, their bot defence and limits, and the requester's confirmation and notices (see "Public forms (2.6b)"); and what 2.6c added: inbound email through Resend, follow-ups and blocked senders (see "Inbound email (2.6c)").

## The model

| Table | Holds | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| `ProjectIntake` | Whether a project takes requests | One per project; turned off, never deleted (`app_user` holds no DELETE). From 2.6c: `emailEnabled`, `emailToken` (the address's local part, global and unique), `emailTokenRotatedAt`. |
| `IntakeNotifyMember` | A member on a project's notify list | Leaves with the membership (cascade). |
| `IntakeForm` | A form that creates requests in one project | `definitionJson` (validated by `IntakeFormDefinitionSchema`, sanitised: the intro, the fields, the confirmation text and, from 2.6b, the requester rules), `definitionVersion` (increases on every definition change), `version` (`If-Match`), `access` (`MEMBERS`, or `PUBLIC` for anyone with the link while public forms are open), `publicId` (random, about 128 bits: the public link's only content), `publishedAt`, `archivedAt`, `dailyCap` (counted by the public submit). Archived, never deleted. |
| `IntakeRequest` | One entry in intake | `status` (`PENDING`, `SNOOZED`, `ACCEPTED`, `DECLINED`, `MERGED`), `channel` (`INTERNAL_FORM`, `PUBLIC_FORM`, `EMAIL` from 2.6c), `title` (plaintext), `body`, `answersJson`, `declineExplanation`, `internalRemark` (encrypted by the manifest, sanitised), `requesterUserId` (a member), `requesterName` and `requesterEmail` (a public requester: personal data, encrypted in place by the PII extension, the address matched through `requesterEmailHash`), `updatesRequested`, `updatesConfirmedAt`, `confirmTokenHash` (global, unique), `formTokenNonce` (unique per workspace), the snooze, the decision and its target (`workItemId`, `mergedIntoWorkItemId`, `mergedIntoRequestId`, plain columns: the target may be purged first). |

Every table has `tenantId`, a tenant-leading index, the four RLS policies, its grant and a two-tenant test (`tests/integration/intake-tables-isolation.test.ts`). Migration `20261125120100_intake_core` adds the CHECKs that keep a request's status and its columns in step (decided when and only when accepted, declined or merged; a snooze date when snoozed; a reason when declined; an item when accepted; exactly one merge target when merged, never itself; an internal request names its requester), each with a catalogue test (`tests/integration/intake-sql-constructs.test.ts`). The two bell types are added by `20261125120000_notification_type_intake`, alone. Sub-phase 2.6b's `20261127120100_intake_public` adds the requester's columns and the CHECKs that keep them in step with the channel (a public request names its form; a member's request carries no outsider's details and no form token; an address always has its hash; updates are asked for, and confirmed, only with an address), and `20261127120000_email_type_intake` adds the four requester email types, alone.

## Forms

A form always asks for a title and a description; its own fields follow, of eight kinds (short text, long text, choice, choices, date, number, web address, checkbox), each with a label, optional help, required or not, a choice's options, and optionally the project custom field it fills on accept (`FIELD_KIND_FILLS`). `compileIntakeRequestSchema(definition)` (`src/lib/intake/form-definition.ts`, client-safe) is the one schema of a request: the form page validates with it through react-hook-form's `zodResolver`, and the send's usecase parses with it, so a field is refused at its own input for the reason the server refuses it.

A form takes requests from members while it is published, not archived, for members, in a visible, live, unarchived project whose intake is on (`formIsOpen`). Anyone who sees the project may send a request through it (`projects.view`): the Forms page (`/forms`) lists the open forms and a form's page (`/forms/<id>`) shows it, then its confirmation text and a short reference. A form that is not open is a 404, whatever the reason.

Each answer is stored as a snapshot of its field (`fieldId`, `label`, `kind`, `customFieldId`, `value`), so a later edit of the form never changes what an old request shows.

## Triage

The Intake page (`/intake`, every visible project's; `/projects/<prefix>/intake`, one project's) lists requests by view (New, Snoozed, Spam, Decided) beside the selected one (`?request=<id>`, the bell's link). The actions are the server's `intakeAffordances`:

| Action | From | What it does |
|---|---|---|
| Accept | New, Snoozed | Creates the work item through `createWorkItemInTx` (the create path's core: its rules, audit, automation event and assignment email) in the same transaction as the request's claim, in a Backlog or Unstarted state, in the request's project or another the person may create items in. Answers fill the project's live custom fields when they can (a choice by its option's name); the rest are appended to the description as "Label: value" lines (`planAcceptedFields`). The requester, and the requesters of requests merged into this one, become watchers. The create's bells ring after the commit. |
| Decline | New, Snoozed | A reason (Not planned, Out of scope, Spam, Other), an explanation for a public requester (not kept for spam; emailed to a requester who confirmed their address, see "Public forms (2.6b)") and an internal remark. Spam shows under the Spam view. |
| Merge | New, Snoozed | Into a live work item the person can see (by its key), or into another new or snoozed request (never a merged one, never itself). Requests merged into this one follow it to the new target, so no chain forms. |
| Snooze | New, Snoozed | Until a date after today (`NOTIFICATIONS_TZ`). |
| Move | New, Snoozed | To another visible, live project whose intake is on. |
| Edit | New, Snoozed | The title or the text (API only in 2.6a). |
| Reopen | Snoozed, Declined, Merged | Back to New; an accepted request never reopens (its item exists). |

Every action locks the request row, checks the status (`checkTriage`, `src/app-layer/domain/intake.ts`) and the version the person read (`If-Match`, required: 428 without, 409 `VERSION_CONFLICT` with the current request), then claims it with one `updateMany` naming the statuses it starts from and that version. Two people deciding at once make one decision (`tests/integration/intake.test.ts` races two accepts). A merge into a request locks both rows in id order first.

Audit entries name ids, the channel and codes, never a title, text or answer: `INTAKE_REQUEST_RECEIVED`, `_ACCEPTED`, `_DECLINED`, `_MERGED`, `_SNOOZED`, `_MOVED`, `_UPDATED`, `_REOPENED`, `INTAKE_REQUESTS_WOKEN`, `INTAKE_REQUESTS_PURGED`, `INTAKE_FORM_CREATED`, `_UPDATED`, `_PUBLISHED`, `_UNPUBLISHED`, `_ARCHIVED`, `_RESTORED`, `PROJECT_INTAKE_UPDATED`, and from 2.6c `INTAKE_FOLLOW_UP_RECEIVED`, `INTAKE_ADDRESS_ROTATED`, `INTAKE_SENDER_BLOCKED`, `_UNBLOCKED` and `INTAKE_INBOUND_EMAILS_PURGED`. The accepted item's `WORK_ITEM_CREATED` entry carries `intakeRequestId` and `intakeChannel`. The automation stub receives `INTAKE_REQUEST_RECEIVED` (inside the send's transaction) and `INTAKE_REQUEST_DECIDED` (after a decision's commit).

## Who may do what

The `intake` domain of the `PermissionSet` (owner decision 92): `intake.view` (read requests and forms) and `intake.triage` for Owners, Admins and Members; `intake.manage` (turn intake on, the notify list, forms, the email address and blocked senders) for Owners and Admins; none for Viewers and Guests. Accepting also needs `work_items.create`. A custom role saved before the domain existed reads it from its base role. Every route is a literal `requirePermission` with a rule in `route-permissions.ts`; `tests/integration/intake-api.test.ts` drives every route as each role, and `tests/guards/tenant-crud-authz-coverage.test.ts` pins each key.

## Settings → Intake

`/settings/intake`, behind the Settings page's own gate (`mayOpenSettings`), offering the writes `intakeAffordances().manage` allows: per project, intake on or off (the first switch-on puts its author on the notify list, decision 95), the notify list (active members only), the one recipient list it also rings (2.7e, decision 142: a live list of the workspace or of the project, or none), and the forms, built in `IntakeFormBuilder` (react-hook-form's `useFieldArray`; fields moved up or down by buttons; a preview rendered by the form page's own component), published, unpublished, archived and restored. From 2.6c, per project, the email address (turned on or off, copied, replaced), and below the projects the blocked senders; see "Inbound email (2.6c)".

## Notifications

A new request rings each person the project's notify list names who holds `intake.view`, never its sender (`INTAKE_REQUEST_RECEIVED`): the member rows and, from 2.7e (decision 142), the people of the recipient list the intake names (`ProjectIntake.recipientListId`), each person once, resolved when the bell is sent by `resolveListRecipients` (an active member who sees the project, in a live workspace; an archived list names nobody; see `docs/notifications.md`, "Recipient lists (2.7e)"); a woken request rings whoever snoozed it while they still hold `intake.view` (`INTAKE_REQUEST_WOKEN`, one per wake day). Both writers (`src/app-layer/notifications/intake.ts`) ask the workspace's mute list first and are listed by the settings API. The sidebar's Intake entry shows the count of new requests in the visible projects (`GET /intake/requests/count`, read every minute) to readers of intake only. A member who sent a request is made a watcher of the item it becomes; a public requester is emailed only as "Public forms (2.6b)" describes.

## Public forms (2.6b)

Owner decisions 86, 88, 89 and 98. A form whose access is `PUBLIC` takes requests from anyone with its link, `/f/<publicId>`, with no session.

**The switch.** `INTAKE_PUBLIC_FORMS` (`isPublicIntakeOpen`, `src/lib/intake/public-switch.ts`): `1` opens public forms, `0` closes them; unset, they are closed in production and open elsewhere, as public sign-up is. Closed, every public page, submit and confirmation answers the one 404 below, and no form can be made public (409 `PUBLIC_FORMS_CLOSED`). An operator opens it once the release runs on both images (`deploy/.env.prod.example`).

**Resolution without disclosure.** `resolvePublicIntakeForm` (`src/app-layer/usecases/intake-public.ts`) reads one narrow row by the global unique `publicId` under `runWithoutRls({ reason: 'public-intake-resolve' })`, the surface's only bypass, and answers `null` for an unknown or malformed id, a members' form, an unpublished or archived form, an archived or deleted project, a project with intake off, a deleted workspace, and public forms closed. Every caller turns `null` into the same 404 (`publicFormNotFound`). Everything after the resolution runs in `runInTenantContext` for the workspace it found, as its system user: a request from outside is written for nobody. The page renders only the form's own published text; it names no workspace, project or member, and it never reads the session (`tests/guards/public-intake-boundary.test.ts` holds the public modules to `intake-public.ts` and no session read, and the bypass reason to that one file).

**The routes.** `POST /api/intake/forms/<publicId>` (the submit) and `POST /api/intake/confirm` (the confirmation) are `MACHINE_CALLER_PREFIXES` entries, POST only, each gated by its function in `src/lib/intake/public/http.ts` (`acceptPublicIntakeRequest`, `confirmRequesterAddress`; the contracts are in `tests/guards/machine-caller-paths-self-authenticate.test.ts`). `/f/` is a public page prefix.

**Bot defence and limits** (all in-house, decision 88):

| Layer | What it does |
|---|---|
| Signed form token | The page embeds `v1.<definitionVersion>.<issuedAt>.<nonce>.<mac>` (HMAC under `deriveSubkey('islestone-intake-form-token-v1')`, `src/lib/intake/public/form-token.ts`; the MAC in its one base64url spelling, 43 characters, H2f). The submit refuses one older than a day, younger than three seconds, for another form or altered (400 `FORM_TOKEN_REFUSED`), and one for an older version of the form (409, "reload"). Its nonce is stored in `formTokenNonce`, unique per workspace, so a token makes one request: a replay fails inside the insert's own transaction (409). |
| Proof-of-work | SHA-256 of `<token>:<counter>` with 14 leading zero bits (`src/lib/intake/pow.ts`), solved by the page with WebCrypto while the person types, in batches that yield to the page (no worker and no third-party script, so the Content-Security-Policy is unchanged). |
| Trap | A field off screen and out of the tab order, labelled "Leave this field empty". Filled, the submit answers the success screen and stores nothing. |
| Edge | The middleware meters `/f/<publicId>` and the submit at `intakeform:<publicId>` and the confirmation at `intakeconfirm`, every method, at the read tier's budget (120/min), before the allowlist returns (`publicIntakeRateKey`). |
| Handler | 5 a minute and 30 an hour per IP and form (`INTAKE_PUBLIC_SUBMIT_LIMIT`, `INTAKE_PUBLIC_SUBMIT_HOURLY_LIMIT`); 10 a minute per IP for the confirmation (`INTAKE_CONFIRM_LIMIT`). |
| Daily caps | The form's `dailyCap` (default 200) and 2,000 public requests per workspace, each over the last 24 hours, counted from `IntakeRequest` rows under the form's row lock (the in-memory limiter forgets on every deploy, decision 50). Past either, 429 "This form is not taking requests right now". |
| Request hygiene | `application/json` only (415); `Sec-Fetch-Site: cross-site` refused (403); the body read through a capped stream, 64 KB for the submit and 4 KB for the confirmation (413 before parsing, `src/lib/intake/public/capped-body.ts`; bytes that are not UTF-8 are a 400); the form's own compiled schema; every text sanitised, control and bidirectional-override characters removed from the title and from the requester's name (`cleanRequesterName`, `src/lib/intake/requester-name.ts`, which both channels use). |

**The requester** (decision 89). A form's requester rules ask for a name and an address, each off, optional or required (`compilePublicIntakeRequestSchema`). After sending, the page shows the form's confirmation text and the request's short reference (`intakeReference`: the id's last eight characters). Ticking "Email me about this request" beside an address sends one `INTAKE_CONFIRM_ADDRESS` email, at most one per address per form per day (the outbox's dedupe entity is the form), linking to `/f/confirm/<token>`. That page confirms nothing until its button POSTs the token (mail scanners follow links); the token is 256 bits, stored as its SHA-256, single use, valid for seven days. Confirming confirms the address on that form: the link's request and the address's other requests on the form that asked for updates, and an address confirmed once on a form is not asked again there. After confirmation, a decision emails the requester from inside its transaction (`enqueueRequesterNotices`): `INTAKE_ACCEPTED` (also to the requests merged into an accepted one), `INTAKE_DECLINED` with the reason and the explanation the triager wrote (never for spam), `INTAKE_MERGED`; a decision made before the confirmation is sent when it comes. Snoozing sends nothing. Every requester email names the form and the reference only, never the title, text or answers typed (`src/app-layer/notifications/email/intake-requester.tsx`), and links nowhere into the app.

**In the app.** Settings → Intake offers "Anyone with the link" in the builder only while public forms are open (or to turn a public form back), the requester rules for a public form, a preview rendered by the public page's own form, and a copy-link button for a published public form. The Intake page shows a public request's requester, address and whether they confirmed updates.

**Audit.** `INTAKE_REQUEST_RECEIVED` (channel `PUBLIC_FORM`, as the system user, with `updatesRequested` and whether the address was confirmed before) and `INTAKE_REQUEST_REQUESTER_CONFIRMED` name ids and booleans only, never a name, address, title, text or answer.

**Not in 2.6b.** Blocked senders and "decline as spam and block" arrived with 2.6c (below), and a blocked address is answered like the trap; embedding a form in another site (decision 86: a link, never an embed); a status page for the requester (decision 89).

## Inbound email (2.6c)

Owner decisions 87, 98, 99 and 120 to 123. Facts about Resend below were taken from search summaries, not a live page (this environment cannot reach resend.com): **re-check each against resend.com before go-live.** They are marked "(re-check)".

**The provider** (decisions 120 and 121). Resend receives mail for `in.islestone.cloud` and posts an `email.received` webhook, signed the Svix way, for each email (re-check); the webhook carries metadata only, so the message is fetched with the API (re-check). Resend is a sub-processor: received mail and logs are very likely stored in the US for 30 days, with no deletion API found (re-check); the owner accepted it on the Standard Contractual Clauses (Resend's DPA, resend.com/legal/dpa) and the EU–US Data Privacy Framework. See [`docs/sub-processors.md`](sub-processors.md). The same company sends Islestone's mail over SMTP (configuration only, `docs/deployment.md`).

**The switch and the address.** `INTAKE_EMAIL` (`isInboundEmailOpen`, `src/lib/intake/email/switch.ts`): `1` opens inbound email, `0` closes it; unset, it is closed in production and open elsewhere. Closed, the webhook answers 200 and reads nothing, and no project's address can be turned on (409 `INBOUND_EMAIL_CLOSED`); turning one off is always allowed. Production refuses `INTAKE_EMAIL=1` without `RESEND_API_KEY` and a `whsec_` `RESEND_WEBHOOK_SECRET`, in `src/env.ts` and again at startup (`startup-intake-email-check.ts`). Turning a project's email on in Settings → Intake mints its address the first time: `<token>@<INTAKE_EMAIL_DOMAIN>` (default `in.islestone.cloud`), a 12-character token from an alphabet without `0`, `1`, `i`, `l` or `o` (`src/lib/intake/email/address.ts`). "Generate a new address", behind an inline confirmation, retires the old address and every reply address built on it at once (`POST /projects/<id>/intake/email-address`, `intake.manage`, audited `INTAKE_ADDRESS_ROTATED`).

**The webhook** (decision 122). `POST /api/intake/email/resend` is the `/api/intake/email` `MACHINE_CALLER_PREFIXES` entry, gated by `acceptResendWebhook` (`src/lib/intake/email/http.ts`; its contract is in `tests/guards/machine-caller-paths-self-authenticate.test.ts`):

1. inbound email closed: 200, nothing read;
2. a per-IP bound (`INTAKE_EMAIL_WEBHOOK_LIMIT`, 300 a minute: Resend's senders share few addresses);
3. the body read through a capped stream, 256 KB (413), never parsed before it is verified; bytes that are not UTF-8 are a 400 (H2f);
4. the Svix signature, verified by hand (`src/lib/intake/email/svix.ts`): HMAC-SHA256 over `${svix-id}.${svix-timestamp}.${rawBody}` keyed with the base64-decoded `whsec_` secret, the `svix-signature` header a space-separated list of `v1,<base64>` of which any one may match, its base64 text compared exactly and in constant time (never the decoded bytes, which a lenient decoder makes equal for other spellings; H2f), refused outside five minutes either side of now (401 `WEBHOOK_SIGNATURE_REFUSED`);
5. then `receiveInboundEmailEvent` (`src/app-layer/usecases/intake-email.ts`), which resolves the address among the event's `to` and `cc` under `runWithoutRls({ reason: 'webhook-ingest' })`, the surface's one pre-tenant read. An unknown address, another domain, a project whose intake or email is off, an archived or deleted project or workspace, or another event type stores nothing. Everything after runs in the workspace's context as its system user: a repeat `svix-id` or `email_id` is a no-op (a still-pending one is enqueued again, so a lost enqueue heals on Resend's retry); the throttles and the blocked senders record a `DROPPED` row; anything else records a `PENDING` row and enqueues `intake-email-fetch` with the row's id as the job id.

The answer to Resend is the same `{ "received": true }` 200 whatever was found. Nothing is fetched inline.

**The job.** `intake-email-fetch` (on demand, five attempts, exponential backoff) claims the row, retrieves the email (`GET https://api.resend.com/emails/receiving/<email_id>` with `RESEND_API_KEY`; re-check the path), then downloads the raw `.eml` from the answer's signed `download_url` (re-check the field). The download host is **pinned** to `resend.com`, `resend.app` and `amazonaws.com` and their subdomains, https only, no credentials or port (`DOWNLOAD_HOST_SUFFIXES`, re-check against a real delivery), and then fetched through `safeFetch` (private, loopback and metadata addresses refused, the DNS answer pinned), read through a capped stream (10 MB). The URL comes from an authenticated call to a fixed host, so it is not a person's value; the pin is there so a tampered answer cannot steer the worker elsewhere, and `safeFetch` so an allowed name that resolves inward is still refused (`tests/guards/ssrf-egress-coverage.test.ts` lists the sink). A refused host, an oversized message or a vanished object falls back to the API's parsed fields, unauthenticated. An unknown email or a refused key settles the row `FAILED` (`FETCH_NOT_FOUND`, `FETCH_UNAUTHORISED`); another failure is retried, and the fifth settles it `FAILED`. A settled row is skipped, and a message already stored (by its Message-ID's hash, `sourceDedupeHash`) is not stored twice.

**Parsing** (`src/lib/intake/email/`, in-house, no mail library): the MIME walk (`mime.ts`: nested multiparts to depth 8 and 200 parts, base64 and quoted-printable, charsets), RFC 2047 headers (`rfc2047.ts`), the plain-text part, else the HTML part turned into text (`text.ts`: block tags to lines, scripts, styles and quoted blockquotes dropped, entities decoded), quoted history stripped (`>` lines; from an "On … wrote:" line, wrapped or not, its French and German forms, an Outlook `From:`/`Sent:` block, "Original Message", a signature delimiter on). The subject is the title (decoded, controls removed, 200 characters, "No subject" when empty), the text the body (10,000), both sanitised by the usecase. Every step is linear in the message's length, and the work on a large message is bounded (H2f): at most 512 KB of a text part is decoded, 100,000 characters of text or 32 KB of HTML make the body (the HTML sanitiser's time grows with the square of unclosed nesting), 16 KB of the `Subject` and `From` are decoded, parts past the 200th are counted without their headers being read. Attachments are counted in `attachmentsDropped` and never stored. Auto-replies and bounces (`Auto-Submitted` other than `no`, `Precedence: bulk/junk/list/auto_reply`, `X-Autoreply`, a null `Return-Path`, `multipart/report`, `mailer-daemon@` or `postmaster@`) are dropped and never answered, which also keeps another system's auto-responder from looping with the acknowledgement.

**Sender authentication** (decision 123; `auth-results.ts`). Only an `Authentication-Results` header whose authserv-id is `INTAKE_EMAIL_AUTHSERV_ID` is read, because any sender can write one; the topmost such header decides. `VERIFIED` is a DMARC pass whose `header.from` is the From domain, or with no DMARC result, a DKIM pass whose signing domain is the From domain or a parent of it; a DMARC fail is `FAILED`; anything else, and every email while the variable is unset, is `NONE`. Whether Resend's receiving server writes the header, and with which authserv-id, is not known from here (re-check): an operator inspects a real delivery's raw message and sets the variable. Only `VERIFIED`:

- gets the `INTAKE_RECEIVED_ACK` email (the reference only, never the text; Reply-To the request's reply address);
- counts as a confirmed address, so the accepted, declined and merged notices of 2.6b reach it;
- attributes the request to an active member whose sign-in address it is (decision 99).

`FAILED` and `NONE` are stored as new requests and never answered; nothing is declined automatically. Letter case is folded in ASCII only when reading the header, an authserv-id or an intake address, so a look-alike such as `K` (the Kelvin sign) never matches `k`; and a `From` address holding a character that case-folds into ASCII is never `VERIFIED`, because the lookups that attribute, confirm and match follow-ups fold case fully (`hasAsciiLookalike`; H2f). The request records the result in `senderCheck` (`IntakeSenderCheck`: `VERIFIED`, `FAILED`, `NONE`; a CHECK sets it exactly on the `EMAIL` channel), shown on the Intake page.

**Follow-ups.** Each emailed request has a reply token; its address is `<token>+r<replyToken>@<domain>`. A later email to it from the request's own requester address adds an `IntakeFollowUp` (its text encrypted by the manifest and sanitised; audited `INTAKE_FOLLOW_UP_RECEIVED`) and brings a snoozed request back to New. From any other address it is a new request. A reply to a decided request is dropped without an answer (`REQUEST_CLOSED`). The Intake page lists a request's follow-ups under its text.

**Throttles.** Per address, 30 emails an hour and 300 a day; per sender per address, 10 an hour (`INBOUND_THROTTLE`), counted from `IntakeInboundEmail` rows in the database (the in-memory limiter forgets on every deploy) under the project intake row's lock, so simultaneous deliveries count one after another (H2f). Past a limit the delivery is recorded `DROPPED` and never fetched.

**Blocked senders** (`IntakeBlockedSender`; ADR 0006 "Blocked sender"). An address is blocked on one project or across the workspace and kept **only as its lookup hash** (`hashForLookup`), as ADR 0006 defines a blocked sender: the address itself is never stored. Its emails are dropped at the webhook and again at the job, and its public-form requests are answered like the trap, with nothing stored or sent. Settings → Intake lists the blocks (where, when, by whom), finds an address's blocks by the address a manager types (`POST /intake/blocked-senders/find`: a body, so the address never sits in a URL or a log), blocks an address on a project or the workspace, and unblocks through the undo toast. Declining a request as spam offers "Block this sender", which blocks the requester's address on the request's project. All of it is `intake.manage` (decision 92), the spam decline's block too, so a Member may decline as spam but not block; audited `INTAKE_SENDER_BLOCKED` and `_UNBLOCKED` with the scope, never the address or its hash.

**Deliveries** (`IntakeInboundEmail`). One row per accepted delivery: the project, `svixId` and `emailId` (each globally unique), the status (`PENDING`, `PROCESSED`, `DROPPED`, `FAILED`) with its outcome code, the sender's lookup hash (for the throttle), the reply token the address carried, attempts, and the request or follow-up it made. No address, subject or text. `intake-purge` removes settled rows 30 days after they arrived (`INBOUND_RETENTION_DAYS`, audited `INTAKE_INBOUND_EMAILS_PURGED`); a pending row stays until it settles.

**Migrations.** `20261201120000_email_type_intake_ack` adds `INTAKE_RECEIVED_ACK` alone; `20261201120100_intake_email` adds the columns, the three tables with their grants and RLS, the CHECKs (an address takes mail only with a token; the sender check with the EMAIL channel; follow-up text present; no negative attachment counts; a dropped or failed delivery names why, a pending one nothing) and the partial unique index that keeps one workspace-wide block per address. Each has a catalogue test (`tests/integration/intake-sql-constructs.test.ts`).

**Before go-live.** The operator steps are in `docs/deployment.md`, "Email (Resend)": the eu-west-1 domains, the MX record, the webhook and its secret, the API key, the authserv-id, asking Resend about deletion or zero retention, and opening `INTAKE_EMAIL` only once the release runs on both images.

## Fuzzing (Hardening H2f)

Owner decision 178; `docs/porting/maps/H2.md` part 6. Three layers, all over the code above.

**Property fuzzing** (`tests/unit/intake/fuzz/`, fast-check, the Jest node project). Each file states what must hold for any input: the capped body reader (a body within the cap read whole, a longer one refused with the typed 413 before more than the cap and one chunk is pulled, no header widening the cap, invalid UTF-8 a typed 400); the compiled request schema (never throws; only the form's keys, never `__proto__`, `constructor` or `prototype`; every string within its limit, every choice among the options; a definition past any limit refused when saved); the form token (any changed byte, another form or version, too young or too old: never verifies) and the proof-of-work (only a counter in range that meets the difficulty; bounded time); the email parsers over messages mutated from a seed corpus (`tests/fixtures/intake-email-corpus/`, written for Islestone in the shapes Gmail, Outlook and Apple Mail send, with nested multiparts, a forwarded message, unknown charsets, bad base64, bidirectional controls, NUL bytes, an auto-reply and a bounce; the 1,000-part and 100 KB-header messages are built by `email-corpus.ts`): never throws, under 50 ms per message up to 256 KB, a bounded title, body, address and attachment count, no markup re-formed in what is stored, and within two seconds at the raw message's 10 MB cap; the sender check (decided by the topmost trusted header by decision 123's rule for any order, case, comments and duplicates; any other server, look-alikes included, never verifies); the address parser (exactly the configured domain and token alphabet in any case; never a homoglyph); the Svix signature (any change to body, id, timestamp or signature refuses; outside five minutes refuses); and the stored requester name. Where a pattern was replaced for speed, a property holds the new code equal to the old on short inputs.

**Run sizes and replay.** `tests/helpers/fuzz.ts`: 100 cases per property on a pull request; the nightly `intake-fuzz` job in `.github/workflows/perf.yml` sets `FC_NUM_RUNS=20000` and runs in band. A failure prints `FC_SEED` and `FC_PATH`, and `FC_SEED=<seed> FC_PATH=<path> npx jest <file>` replays it; a red night opens or comments on the nightly issue. Time bounds (`bestTimeMs`) take, for each try, the smaller of the wall-clock and this process's processor time, and the fastest of four tries, so neither other test workers nor the garbage collector's threads nor a pause count: a pattern that backtracks is slow every time. A seed found failing becomes an example test in the pull request that fixes it, in the file's "examples the fuzzing found" block.

**Route fuzzing** (`tests/integration/intake-fuzz.test.ts`, through the real handlers): injection payloads in every public field and in an email's subject and sender are stored sanitised and no CR or LF reaches an outbox header field; bodies at the cap, past it, endless and chunked, gzip, nested 10,000 deep and with 10,000 keys get the typed 413 or 400 and the reader stops at the cap; 200 simultaneous submissions take exactly the form's daily cap; the per-IP limit answers 429 with `Retry-After`, and no forwarding header the app does not trust mints a budget (the proxy sets the first `X-Forwarded-For` entry from the connection, `tests/unit/deploy/caddy-snippet.test.ts`); 100 simultaneous webhooks of one `svix-id` record one delivery; the throttles hold under concurrency; a blocked sender's flood stores no request and enqueues nothing. Public submits and an address's deliveries wait their turn in the process before they open their transaction (`src/app-layer/usecases/intake-write-queue.ts`, the pattern of decision 174): without it a flood emptied the connection pool and every transaction expired.

**End to end and DAST.** `tests/e2e/intake.spec.ts` sends a hostile name and title through a public form and checks the Intake page shows the stored text literally with no Content-Security-Policy report. The nightly ZAP baseline (`dast.yml`) publishes a public form and passes `/f/<publicId>`, a submit and a confirmation through ZAP (`.zap/public-intake-hook.py`), so the passive rules read them.
