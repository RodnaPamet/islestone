# 0011 — External calendar sync

Status: Accepted (owner decisions 225 to 244, 2026-11-21): Islestone the source of truth; a member's open assigned items' due dates pushed and a moved day read back as their own edit while Islestone's date is unchanged; the milestones of their projects pushed read-only (227, a departure); title and a link back in a private, free, all-day event (229, a departure) in a calendar of Islestone's own; personal connections an admin enables, through the sign-in apps outside the auth library's seam (236, a departure); tokens under the workspace's key; a leaving member's events deleted before the token

## Context

Roadmap 3.3 asks to "port the calendar surface: a calendar view of items, cycles and milestones, and two-way sync of due dates to Google and Microsoft calendars through the ported connection model. Event titles pushed outside carry item titles only." H3 adds: "Calendar sync tested for revoked tokens, duplicates and deleted events." The porting map is `docs/porting/maps/3.3.md`; its open questions 1 to 20 were decided by the owner as decisions 225 to 244 (`docs/HANDOFF.md`). The calendar view itself (the `CALENDAR` layout, inflect's month grid, moves through ADR 0010's "Change dates") is an ordinary view; this ADR records the rules for data leaving Islestone and coming back.

- **Verified** (inflect-compliance at `5528464fc54a8681956f3b9ce23ca9c075f0420e`). Inflect has a per-user, tenant-scoped calendar connection with an encrypted token blob, a mapping row per pushed event, a pure reconciler, a sweep over `CalendarWriter` and `MappingStore` interfaces, Microsoft admin consent and a daily push job; nothing implements the writer, its job pushes nothing, it never reads back, and it encrypts tokens under the deployment's global key with `encryptField`, which no Islestone rotation sweep would reach. It uses the sign-in OAuth clients for calendar.
- **Verified** (Islestone at `5a32629`). `ViewLayout` reserves `CALENDAR`; `src/env.ts` declares `GOOGLE_CLIENT_ID`, `GOOGLE_CLIENT_SECRET`, `MICROSOFT_CLIENT_ID`, `MICROSOFT_CLIENT_SECRET` and `MICROSOFT_TENANT_ID` (default `common`) for sign-in; work-item titles are plaintext by ADR 0002 §11 ("quoted in commit messages and calendar events"); `appLink` builds absolute links; the auth library loads only in the files of `NEXT_AUTH_SEAM` (ADR 0004 A3).
- **Verified** (provider documentation, 2026-11-21: Google Calendar API v3 discovery document, revision 20261118; Microsoft Graph v1.0 docs at `2329b086341e67d36de55a5743af1a47224046ca`). Google's `calendar.app.created` scope reaches only calendars the app created; Google event ids are base32hex and collisions are not guaranteed to be detected at creation; Graph's `transactionId` avoids redundant creates on retries; Graph's `calendarView/delta` covers the primary calendar only. Not verified: the class of `calendar.app.created` and its verification on a sign-in app, and the reach of Google's token revocation across one app's grants (both re-checked before 3.3b).

## Decision

1. **Islestone is the source of truth** (decisions 225, 230). It pushes; it reads back exactly one thing: a moved day on an item event it created, applied as the member's own versioned due-date edit, through every check that edit has, and only while the item's due date is still the one Islestone last pushed. A change that loses, or that the member may not make, is undone at the next sync and the member gets one in-app notification saying why. Titles, descriptions, attendees and everything else changed in a calendar are not read and are restored by the next push. No item is ever created from a calendar.
2. **What is pushed** (decisions 226, 227). A member's open items assigned to them with a due date from 30 days ago to a year ahead (at most 1,000, nearest first, the rest counted and said), and **the milestones of the live, visible, unarchived projects in which they have open assigned items** (decision 227, the owner's choice B over the recommended A), with a due date in the same window, as read-only events: a moved milestone event is undone at the next sync with the notification of §1. Cycles are not pushed.
3. **What an event carries** (decisions 228, 229). An all-day event on the due date in a calendar named "Islestone" that the connection creates, Islestone's only place in the member's account: the item's title or the milestone's name, and **one absolute link back** to it, built with `appLink` (decision 229, the owner's choice B over the recommended A), which carries the workspace's address and the item's key, or the project's key and the milestone's id. Private and free; no other description, no people, no location, no reminders of its own. Nothing else of the workspace leaves.
4. **Edge cases** (decisions 231, 232, 233). A timed or multi-day event means its last day in the event's own time zone. An event deleted in the calendar leaves the item untouched and stops that item syncing until its due date changes or the member chooses "Resync"; a deleted Islestone calendar stops the connection until the member recreates it or disconnects. Islestone's changes are pushed within about a minute; calendars are read every 15 minutes; no provider webhook and no new public endpoint.
5. **Who** (decisions 234, 235). Calendar sync is off until the operator's deployment switch is on and an Owner or Admin turns it on for the workspace (`integrations.manage`); turning it off removes every synced event. Each member who sees work items may connect their own calendar, and only their own; a member who may not edit work items, a Viewer for instance, gets a read-only feed from which nothing is read back. A guest connects nothing until roadmap 8.4.
6. **The apps and the flow** (decisions 236, 237). Calendar connections use **the same Google and Microsoft OAuth clients as sign-in** (decision 236, the owner's choice B over the recommended A); no separate calendar client exists. The calendar flow is hand-written outside the auth library's seam (ADR 0004 A3 unchanged): its own redirect URI on the same app registrations, PKCE, a sealed single-use state bound to the member and workspace, and only the calendar scope, never `openid`, `email` or `profile`; it writes no `Account`, links no identity and redeems no invite. Consequences accepted with it: the calendar scope's verification at Google is the sign-in app's; a person removing the app at Google or Microsoft withdraws both sign-in consent and calendar; and **Islestone never calls Google's revoke endpoint**, because revoking one token may withdraw the person's whole grant to the shared app; tokens are destroyed locally. Microsoft 365 is authorised once per customer directory by its admin (`/adminconsent`), ported from inflect.
7. **Tokens** (decision 238) are one blob per connection in a column of the encryption manifest, under the workspace's own key, so both key-rotation sweeps re-encrypt them; a token is never logged and never stored on a sign-in row.
8. **Leaving and teardown** (decision 239). A member who disconnects, leaves or is removed, a switch turned off and a consent withdrawn all run one teardown: Islestone deletes its own events with the stored token first, then the Islestone calendar only if it is empty, then destroys the token, then its own rows. A record of a pushed event outlives its item or milestone until its remote event is gone. The teardown writes nothing in Islestone and touches nothing but what Islestone created.
9. **Sub-processors** (decision 240). Google and Microsoft are listed in `docs/sub-processors.md` with what leaves (titles, due dates and links of a member's items and their projects' milestones), on whose instruction (the member's, after the admin's switch) and where it lands (a calendar in the member's own account).
10. **Words** (decision 243): Calendar connection (`CalendarConnection`), Synced event (`CalendarSyncedEvent`), Calendar sync settings (`TenantCalendarSettings`), the settings section "Calendar sync" (ADR 0006).

## Consequences

- The provider clients and the read-back are new code; inflect's reconciler, sweep, token module and consent flow are ported, renamed and given real writers.
- Events leave a link, so the workspace's address and an item's key (or a project's key and a milestone's id) are in a third party's calendar and visible to whoever the member shares it with; the link opens Islestone only for a signed-in member.
- One app per provider couples calendar access to sign-in at Google and Microsoft: a removal or a verification problem at the provider can touch both; the deployment documentation registers the extra redirect URIs on the sign-in apps.
- Tokens are not revoked at Google on disconnect; a member who wants Islestone gone from their Google account removes it there, which also affects signing in with Google.
- Milestone events are the only events that ignore a move; the notification explains it.
- A workspace or user deletion (roadmap 8.3) must run the teardown before its cascade.

## What would change this decision

- Customers need calendar changes in seconds (provider webhooks and a "watch" record), or titles back from calendars (a sanitising and mention path for calendar text).
- A customer or a reviewer requires calendar access to be separable from sign-in at the provider (separate calendar apps, the recommended option of the map's question 12).
- Google's or Microsoft's terms for the calendar scope change, or the scope's verification cannot be completed on the sign-in app.
- Members need items beyond their assigned ones (waiting on, watched, a saved view), cycles in calendars, or events with more than a title and a link.
