# 0010 — Scheduling and working calendars

Status: Accepted (owner decisions 182 to 203, 2026-11-20): one pure scheduling engine over date-only days, blocks read as finish-to-start with no lag, dates as "not before" and planned finish with conflicts reported and no stored date moved, milestone and project due dates as deadlines (188, a departure: each project gains a due date), workspace and team working calendars managed by admins, a person's confirmed date change with an optional "also move the items it pushes later" (194, a departure), and SVAR React Gantt's MIT core at 2.8.3 with its notice left in its installed licence files (199, a departure)

## Context

Roadmap 3.1 asks for a timeline view on SVAR React Gantt's MIT core and an in-house scheduling engine: "forward and backward pass over the dependency graph, float, critical path, working calendars and holidays. The engine takes plain data and returns plain data, with no database access. Dragging a bar creates a change the user confirms." H3 adds property tests of the scheduler and a 5,000-item budget. The porting map is `docs/porting/maps/3.1.md`; its open questions 1 to 22 were decided by the owner as decisions 182 to 203 (`docs/HANDOFF.md`).

- **Verified** (2026-11-20, the 3.1 map's "SVAR React Gantt, verified 2026-11-20"). `@svar-ui/react-gantt` 2.8.3 and its whole dependency tree declare MIT; the MIT core draws and edits bars but schedules nothing: no forward or backward pass, float, critical path, working calendar, today line or undo (those are in the paid PRO edition, removed from the public code). The old name `wx-react-gantt`, and `@svar-ui/react-gantt` 2.3.1 to 2.3.4, are GPL-3.0. The compiled chart exposes almost nothing to assistive technology and its bars cannot be moved by keyboard. SVAR's `dist` carries no licence banner.
- **Verified** (Islestone at `e60fac3`). `WorkItem.startDate` and `dueDate` are optional and date-only (`@db.Date`); `WorkItemRelation` stores `BLOCKS` edges, a cycle refused under `lockWorkItemGraph` (ADR 0002 §6); `Milestone.dueDate` is date-only; `Project` has no date. Review steps count working days Monday to Friday in `NOTIFICATIONS_TZ` (ADR 0009 §13). `ViewLayout` already holds `TIMELINE`.
- **Verified** (inflect-compliance at `5528464fc54a8681956f3b9ce23ca9c075f0420e`). No scheduling, holiday or working-day code; `src/app-layer/libraries/dependency-graph.ts` is a deterministic Kahn sort, taken as a pattern only.

## Decision

1. **One engine** (decisions 184, 197). Scheduling is a pure, client-safe module (`src/app-layer/domain/schedule/`): plain data in, plain data out, no database, no clock, no time zone. Dates are whole days since 1970-01-01 (epoch days), so daylight saving cannot move a result; results are sorted by id, so input order never matters. The server computes the schedule for every timeline read; the browser runs the same module to preview a drag's effects. Every later caller (roadmap 7.5's rescheduling proposals, 3.6's forecasts) uses it; there is no second implementation.
2. **Dependencies** (decision 183) are `BLOCKS` relations read as finish-to-start with no lag; no other dependency kind exists. A cycle is refused by the relation usecase (ADR 0002 §6) and again by the engine, which names it.
3. **What dates mean** (decisions 184 to 188). The start date is "not before", the due date is the planned finish, and the working days between them, on the item's calendar, are the duration. An item with only a due date is one working day ending on it, only a start date one working day from it; with neither it is listed under "No dates", and inside a chain it counts as zero days and the chain is flagged. An item with sub-items in view is a summary spanning them, not scheduled itself: a dependency into it applies to its sub-items, one out of it leaves from its last-finishing one. A done item is fixed at its actual dates; a cancelled item is left out with its edges; an unstarted item cannot start before today and a started one cannot finish before today. **Two deadlines** (decision 188, the owner's choice C over the recommended A): an item's milestone's due date, and **its project's due date**, a new optional date-only field (`Project.dueDate`, ADR 0002 §16), each a date its items must finish by; the earlier bounds the backward pass, and missing either is reported on its own. Start after due is refused on write (`START_AFTER_DUE`) from 3.1a; rows written before are reported, never rewritten.
4. **Computed, never stored** (decisions 184, 198). Earliest and latest starts and finishes, float and the critical path are computed on every read; nothing writes them, and the engine never changes a stored date. The critical path is the open and started items with the least total float in view (zero when everything fits, negative when a deadline is missed) and the edges between them that leave no gap.
5. **Working calendars** (decisions 189 to 193). A workspace has one working calendar and each team may have one that replaces it for the team's items: a time zone (default `NOTIFICATIONS_TZ`), working weekdays and holidays entered by hand ("Copy last year's"; no country preset, no feed). An item uses its team's calendar, else the workspace's; with neither, Monday to Friday in `NOTIFICATIONS_TZ`. "Today" for an item is today in its calendar's time zone; the nightly jobs keep firing in `NOTIFICATIONS_TZ`. Calendars are written under `admin.manage` and read under `projects.view`. Review steps opened after 3.1 count working days on the item's calendar; a stored `dueOn` never moves (ADR 0009 §13). Changing a calendar changes no stored date.
6. **A person changes dates; the timeline asks first** (decisions 194, 195). Dragging a bar or drawing a dependency opens a confirmation ("Change dates", "ISL-1 blocks ISL-2?") showing the change and its effects; confirming sends the ordinary versioned write, with every field-set, permission and audit rule the API applies; a dependency's removal goes through the undo toast. **The confirmation also offers "Also move the N items it pushes later"** (decision 194, the owner's choice B over the recommended A): one all-or-nothing change of the dragged item's dates and of the open, unstarted items downstream of it that the change would make start before a blocker finishes, each moved later only to its earliest start, at most 100 at once; it takes the graph lock, then every item's row lock in id order (decision 173), checks each item's version, downstream reach and field set against the locked rows, writes one audited, versioned change per item and one summary entry, and is held five seconds behind the undo toast. None of this is a proposal: proposals are agents' (ADR 0003), and an agent's schedule change is a proposal (roadmap 7.5) that reuses the engine, never this confirmation.
7. **The widget** (decisions 182, 196, 199, 203). SVAR React Gantt, MIT core only, `@svar-ui/react-gantt` 2.8.3 pinned exactly, loaded through one adapter file, for display and input only: no PRO package, no PRO option, no vendor CDN request (its icon font is never loaded); the word "Gantt" stays inside that import (ADR 0006). One timeline shows at most 5,000 items; a larger scope is refused with its count, never truncated. A schedule table beside the chart ("Chart / Table") shows the same rows and numbers and edits dates by keyboard through the same confirmation: it is the accessible way to use the timeline. **SVAR's MIT notice travels only in the licence files installed with its packages** (decision 199, the owner's choice C over the recommended A): no generated notices file and no `NOTICE` section. Recorded as a known risk: the MIT licence asks that its notice "be included in all copies or substantial portions of the Software", and the minified browser bundle will carry none. This changes no licence policy or gate.

## Consequences

- The timeline's critical path, today line, shading and accessibility layer are Islestone's own code over the widget, and its tests (H3) are Islestone's.
- A team's calendar can differ from its neighbour's, so a cross-team dependency is computed per calendar: a blocker's finish on its own calendar, its successor's start on its own.
- `Project` gains a column and its create and edit forms a field; project templates carry the due date as an offset from creation. A project's due date moves nothing and rings no reminder.
- One bulk route changes dates for a chain. Its reach is bounded by the graph (`NOT_DOWNSTREAM`), its direction by `PUSH_NOT_LATER`, its size by 100 items; it is the only multi-item date write besides the bulk due-date action.
- The browser bundle carries SVAR's code without its notice (decision 199).
- A scope over 5,000 items is refused, never truncated; the engine's own limit (20,000) protects 7.5 and other server callers.

## What would change this decision

- Customers need start-to-start or finish-to-finish dependencies, or lag (a new relation shape and an ADR 0002 amendment).
- Personal time off must shape schedules (roadmap 3.5), or automatic levelling or rescheduling is wanted beyond agent proposals.
- SVAR PRO is bought (an ADR 0007 exemption and a new pin), or the core changes its licence.
- The engine cannot meet the 5,000-item budget.
- The owner revisits decision 199 (a generated notices file, or a `NOTICE` section).
