## Summary

- **Three things.** (1) **Measures over the transition record**: throughput, cycle time, lead time, the age of started items, cumulative flow, the burn-up for any scope (today it exists per cycle only), and time waiting per person (decision 76), each defined exactly over `WorkItemTransition` below. (2) **A forecasting module**, pure and seeded: Monte Carlo over the scope's daily completions on the working days of its working calendar, giving the dates by which its open items are 50, 85 and 95 percent likely to be finished, the chance of finishing by the scope's due date, and a refusal with its reason when the history is too thin. (3) **Dashboards**: personal and shared pages of widgets on the mirrored `DashboardGrid`, with a default composition in code.
- **Everything is computed on read and nothing computed is stored** (principle 4): no forecast, series or count is written. The only new tables are the dashboards'. The record gains one column, `fromStateCategory` (question 2), so a completion is found by an index rather than by reconstructing every item's history.
- **What is taken.** From inflect-compliance: the grid and the widget shell are already mirrored byte for byte (part B of 1.2); 3.6 copies and adapts the widget schema's bounds and discriminated union, the default-composition idea and the view/edit page of its org dashboard (Epic 41), its mulberry32 generator (`seededRng`, RQ-3) and its loss-exceedance curve as the "chance finished by" curve; its calibration back-test (PR-L) is taken as a pattern. From jira-auto-scheduler: **pattern only**, the Monte Carlo shell (N trials, sorted outcomes, percentile read-out, a histogram and "chance it finishes by this date", the refusal when there is nothing to forecast). Its sampling model is estimate-driven, not throughput-driven (study §2), so the throughput sampler is written fresh, and its estimate model is left for 7.5.
- **The words collide** with ADR 0006 in seven places: "Velocity" and "Burn-down" are banned, "Confidence" is the Health row's ban (so the percentiles are "likely", never "confidence"), "Dashboard" is banned by the My Work row (the schema guard would refuse a `Dashboard` model today), "Report" is the View row's ban, "Card" is a work item's ban, and "Lead" and "Cycle" name a person and a time box. Decision 309 gives the rows.
- **A new `analytics` permission domain** (question 21): `view` (charts, forecasts and one's own dashboards), `share` (shared dashboards) and `people` (time waiting per person, beyond one's own). Everyone who reads projects reads analytics over the items they can see; a guest sees nothing until 8.4 (decision 204).
- **H3** gets a defined back-test (the date of the next N completions, replayed from every weekly origin with the history as it stood then), published from the scale seed's back-dated history, and property tests for determinism, refusals and monotonicity.

## What makes the prompt harder than it reads

- **The record stores pictures, not events.** Each `WorkItemTransition` row is the item's picture right after a change (state, cycle, milestone, project, deletion, waiting on, estimates; `burn-up.ts`' header). "Completed on 4 March" has to be inferred from two consecutive pictures, and a reopen, a deletion, a purge, a move to another project and a restore each change what "completed" means for a past day.
- **"Any scope"** means two scopes: the items whose remaining work is forecast, and the history that feeds the forecast. A milestone's own past completions are usually too few; its project's are not, but the project also serves other milestones.
- **"A stated minimum of completed items"** needs a definition of history (which items, over which days) and wording a person can act on.
- **Time waiting is not all in one field.** Waiting on is cleared while a review round is open, and the item waits on the open step's reviewers, read from their rows (decisions 152 and 153). The transition record shows nothing during a review.
- **"Per person"** metrics are the most sensitive numbers 3.6 can show: time waiting per person is a measure of how quickly someone responds.
- **100,000 items** carry several transitions each. A chart over a large scope cannot ship every row to Node, and the filters a person sets are mostly about an item's current values (labels, type, assignee), while the record holds only some fields at each instant.
- **Dates and zones.** Day boundaries, working days and holidays must come from 3.1's calendars (ADR 0010 §1: one engine), and the existing cycle burn-up uses `NOTIFICATIONS_TZ`, from before calendars existed.

## The record (3.6a; decision 310)

### What `WorkItemTransition` holds, and what it does not

One row after every insert of a work item, and after every update that changes its `stateId`, `cycleId`, `milestoneId`, `projectId`, deletion, `waitingOnUserId`, `estimatePoints` or `estimateMinutes` (migration `20261220120000_estimates`, lines 45 to 79), holding the item's picture after the change: `projectId`, `teamId`, `stateId`, `stateCategory`, `cycleId`, `milestoneId` (since 2.5b), `waitingOnUserId` (since 2.4b-a), both estimates (since 3.4a), `deleted` and `at` (`clock_timestamp()` in UTC), ordered by `at` then `id`. `app_user` holds `SELECT` only; the SECURITY DEFINER trigger is the only writer. Rows have no foreign key to `WorkItem` and outlive the purge (decision 66).

Not recorded: the assignee, the type, labels, priority, the parent, custom fields, the goal (3.2a: "The transition trigger does not watch it"), due and start dates. History filters on those read the item's **current** values (question 7). A purged item has no `WorkItem` row, so its current values are gone: it can be counted only by recorded fields.

Items created before migration `20261121120000_cycles_and_transitions` (2.5a) have no insert row; their record starts at their first recorded change. Islestone had no production data before 2.5a, so this is a rule for completeness, not a migration: such an item's lead time starts at its first row.

### One new column (question 2, decision 310)

`WorkItemTransition.fromStateCategory WorkflowStateCategory?`: the item's state category **before** the change, `OLD."stateCategory"` on an update, NULL on the insert row. The trigger function gains the column (the next `CREATE OR REPLACE` in the chain of 2.5a, 2.5b, 2.4b-a, 3.2a and 3.4a); its `UPDATE OF` list is unchanged. The migration backfills existing rows with `LAG("stateCategory") OVER (PARTITION BY "tenantId", "workItemId" ORDER BY "at", "id")`, as the superuser (the record has no immutability trigger; `app_user` still holds `SELECT` only). Then:

- **A completion is a row**: `"stateCategory" = 'DONE' AND "fromStateCategory" IS DISTINCT FROM 'DONE'` (an insert straight into Done counts at creation). A reopen is `"fromStateCategory" = 'DONE' AND "stateCategory" <> 'DONE'`. A deletion, a restore, a move or an estimate change of a Done item keeps both Done and is not a completion.
- **The partial index** `WorkItemTransition_completions` on `("tenantId", "projectId", "at") WHERE "stateCategory" = 'DONE' AND "fromStateCategory" IS DISTINCT FROM 'DONE'`, written in SQL (Prisma cannot declare it; the drift check is told, as for 2.7b's search indexes), serves throughput, cycle time, lead time and the forecast's history at any workspace size. A query spells the predicate exactly.
- A catalogue test holds the column, the trigger body and the index; `tests/integration/` checks the backfill on planted histories (a reopen, a deletion of a Done item, a move, an insert into Done).

## The measures (3.6a)

### Reconstruction

An item's **picture at an instant** `t` is its latest row with `at ≤ t`, ties broken by `id` (2.5a's rule, unchanged). The pure module `src/app-layer/domain/analytics/record.ts` groups facts by item, orders them, and answers pictures; every measure below calls it, and the SQL reads that aggregate in the database implement the same rule (each SQL read has a property test against the pure module on random histories).

A reading has a **moment** `R`: now for the pages, an origin in the past for the back-test. Only rows with `at ≤ R` are read, so a back-test never sees the future.

### Completion (question 3, decision 311)

An item is **completed as of `R`** when its picture at `R` is in a Done state and not deleted. Its **completion** is its last entry into Done at or before `R` (the last completion row). Each completed item counts **once**, on the day of its completion:

- **A reopened item** leaves the completed set while it is open, and counts again, on its new completion day, when it is done again. A past week's throughput can therefore fall when an item done that week is reopened later: the history "as it stands" at `R`.
- **A deleted item** (its picture deleted) is not completed: deleting retracts it from every past day. A restored item returns.
- **A purged item**'s last row is its deletion row, so it never counts.
- **Cancelled is not done**: a cancelled item is never completed and never in throughput, cycle time or lead time.

### Throughput

The number of items whose completion (as of `R`) falls on a day, in the scope's zone (question 8), counted per day and summed per ISO week (Monday to Sunday, `WEEK_STARTS_ON = 1`) for the charts. The read: completion rows in the window from the partial index, less those superseded before `R` by a later reopen, deletion or cancellation of the same item (a `NOT EXISTS` probe on `WorkItemTransition_tenantId_workItemId_at_idx`), grouped by day in SQL. Bounded: at most `ANALYTICS_COMPLETION_LIMIT` (50,000) completions in the window, read one past, else 422 `ANALYTICS_TOO_LARGE` with the count, never cut.

### Cycle time and lead time (questions 4 and 5, decisions 312 and 313)

For each item completed in the window:

- **Cycle time** runs from its **first** entry into Started (the earliest row with `stateCategory = 'STARTED'`, at or before its completion) to its completion. An item that reached Done without ever being Started has no cycle time; it is counted in the answer as "Done without starting".
- **Lead time** runs from its **creation** (its first row; for an item created before 2.5a, its first recorded change) to its completion.
- **Both are measured as elapsed working time** on the **item's own** working calendar (its team's, else the workspace's; ADR 0010 §5), in that calendar's zone: the elapsed time between the two instants, less every whole non-working day (a weekend day or a holiday) of that calendar between them, in days to one decimal (24 hours is one day). Started and finished the same afternoon is 0.2 days; Friday 16:00 to Monday 10:00 on a Monday-to-Friday calendar is 0.75 days. The function, `elapsedWorkingDays(start, end, calendar)` in `domain/analytics/elapsed.ts`, walks days with `isWorkingDay` and `startOfDayInTz`; no second working-day rule.
- **The answer** gives, over every completion in the window, the 50th, 85th and 95th percentiles (nearest rank: the value at index `ceil(p × n) − 1` of the sorted list, the one definition 3.6 uses), the count, and the newest 2,000 completions as points (key, completion day, cycle time, lead time) for the scatter, named "the newest 2,000" when there are more.

### Age

For each open item in a Started state now: elapsed working time from its first entry into Started to now, on its own calendar. Drawn on the cycle-time chart as a second series ("Age") against the cycle time percentiles, so an item already older than the 85th percentile shows. 7.4's "ageing items" signal reads this measure.

### Cumulative flow

At the end of each day of the window (in the scope's zone; "now" for today, as the burn-up does), the items **in scope** at that instant, by state category: Backlog, Unstarted and Started counted at that instant; **Done counted as items completed since the window's start** (so the chart's scale follows the window, not the project's whole life); Cancelled and deleted items out. With one team in scope, the bands may be that team's workflow states by name, in its order; otherwise the four categories.

"In scope at an instant" judges the recorded fields at that instant (question 7): a moved item belongs to its old project before the move and its new one after.

The read (question 23, decision 331): one SQL statement over the scope's items' rows up to the window's end, with `LAG` per item, turning each change into a +1 and a −1 on the day it happens, then a running sum from the window's start. Bounded: the items ever in scope during the window at most `ANALYTICS_FLOW_ITEM_LIMIT` (20,000; the 100k seed's largest project has exactly 20,000), read one past, else 422 `ANALYTICS_TOO_LARGE`; the window at most 366 days, drawn by day up to 92 days and by week beyond.

### The burn-up, for any scope

Today `getCycleBurnUp` draws a cycle's scope, started and completed items per day from the cycle's start to today (2.5a; points and hours since 3.4a). 3.6 keeps that route and chart unchanged in behaviour and generalises the computation:

- **The pure `burnUp` is re-expressed over `domain/analytics/flow.ts`** (an event sweep: sort each item's changes once, walk the days), with 2.5a's and 3.4a's tests as the oracle and a property test that the new sweep equals the old per-day scan on random histories. The old scan is O(days × items × rows); the sweep is O(rows log rows + days).
- **Any scope and window**: a project, milestone, goal, initiative, team or the list's filters, over a chosen window. Scope is items in scope, not deleted, not cancelled at each day's end; started is Started or Done; completed is Done (totals, as the cycle's chart counts them, not "since the window's start").
- **Measures**: items always; points or hours when **every** team in scope estimates in that unit (`burnUpMeasuresFor` per team, intersected), with unestimated items counted and named (decision 259). Points and hours are never mixed or converted (ADR 0012).
- **The forecast on the burn-up**: where a forecast is shown for the same scope (3.6c), its 50, 85 and 95 percent dates are drawn as vertical markers past today, labelled.

### Time waiting (3.6b; decisions 76, 317 and 329)

A **wait** is a maximal run of an item's consecutive rows naming the same person in `waitingOnUserId`, while the item is live. It starts at the run's first row and ends at the next row naming someone else or no one, at the item's deletion row, or at `R` if still running. A close always ends one (closing clears waiting on, and 2.4b-a's CHECK `WorkItem_waiting_on_open_only` refuses a closed item that names someone). A reopen does not restore waiting on, so it starts no wait.

**Review waits** (question 9, decision 317): while a review step is open, each reviewer asked waits from the step's `openedAt` until their response (`respondedAt`, the latest, since a response may change while the step is open; decision 162), their withdrawal (`withdrawnAt`) or the step's `endedAt`. These rows are never rewritten once ended (ADR 0009 §4), so they are a record too, and they cover exactly the time waiting on is cleared (decisions 152 and 153). They are shown beside field waits ("Waited on Ana 3.5 days, 2 of them as a reviewer").

Over a window, each wait counts its overlap with the window as elapsed working time on the item's own calendar (question 5). The answer, per person:

- **total time waited** in the window, items waited on (distinct), waits ended in the window with their median and 85th percentile length (whole waits), and **waiting now** (items waiting on them at `R`, with the age of each wait);
- **totals across everyone**: time open items spent waiting on someone, as a share of open item time in the window;
- a removed member's waits as one row, "Former members", never their name; the name of an active or deactivated member from the membership.

Bounded by the flow chart's item limit. **Who sees which rows** is decision 329: everyone holding `analytics.view` sees the totals and their own row; per-person rows for others need `analytics.people`.

Later parties: from 6.3, waiting on may name an agent session (decision 106); the record gains that column in 6.3's migration and time waiting groups it by agent. From 8.4, a guest's firm tag groups its guests' waits "per firm" (decision 76); the read groups by a person's firm when the tag exists. Neither is built in 3.6.

### What counts and what does not

| Case | Rule |
|---|---|
| Cancelled | Never completed; out of throughput, cycle time, lead time; out of flow and burn-up scope from the moment it is cancelled (as 2.5a's scope). |
| Deleted | Out at every instant it is deleted; a deleted item's completion is retracted (question 3). Restored, it returns. |
| Purged | Its last row is its deletion: never counted; its rows are kept (decision 66) but read as deleted. |
| Reopened | Counted once, at its last completion as of `R`; open between (question 3). Cycle time from its first start. |
| Moved to another project | Judged by the recorded project at each instant (question 7): counted in the old project before the move, the new one after; a completion counts in the project it was completed in. |
| Moved to another team | The team follows the project; cycle membership is cleared by the move (2.5a), and the record shows it. |
| Sub-items and parents | Each item counts once, a parent and its sub-items alike (question 6), as progress counts (decision 212). A type filter narrows to one level when a team wants it. |
| Archived projects | Their history counts (as progress counts archived projects' items, decision 212); a deleted project's does not (its items are deleted with it). Forecasts exclude archived projects' open items (they take no work). |
| Projects the caller cannot see | Never counted, now or in history; a moved item's rows count only while in a project the caller can see. Before 8.4 every role but Guest sees every live project. |
| Items before 2.5a | Record from their first recorded change; lead time from it (above). |
| Changing type, labels, assignee | Not recorded: a filter on them judges current values (question 7). |

### Time zone and calendars (question 8, decision 316)

- **Day boundaries** (throughput days, flow days, burn-up days, the forecast's "today"): the **scope's working calendar's zone**, by `getWorkItemCalendar`'s rule (one team, project, cycle or milestone: that team's calendar; anything wider: the workspace's), through `todayIn` and `todayClock`. A calendar's zone defaults to `NOTIFICATIONS_TZ` (decision 191), so nothing moves for a workspace with one zone.
- **Durations** (cycle time, lead time, age, waits): the item's own calendar, its non-working days removed (above).
- **The forecast** simulates on the scope's calendar's working days and holidays (below).
- **The cycle burn-up** moves to the same rule (its team's calendar zone, defaulting to `NOTIFICATIONS_TZ`), so the cycle page and an analytics burn-up of the same cycle agree. The nightly jobs stay on `NOTIFICATIONS_TZ` (decision 191).
- No date is ever passed through a zone; dates are epoch days (ADR 0010 §1). Results are byte-equal under five process time zones (H3).

### Filters in history (question 7, decision 315)

The analytics reads take the list's filters (`resolveListFiltersInTx`, never a second filter path), except the state, category, due and closed filters, which describe status and which each measure owns (the answer says they were set aside). Of the rest:

- **Recorded fields are judged as recorded at each instant**: project (and so team and initiative, through the project's current initiative link), cycle, milestone, state, deletion.
- **Every other filter judges current values**: type, label, assignee, waiting on, priority, custom fields, goal, created by, reviewer, watching, `q`. "Label is Bug" counts items labelled Bug now, at every past instant. This is the usual behaviour of flow tools and the only one possible for fields the record does not hold; `docs/analytics.md` says so.
- A purged item has no current values, so a current-value filter excludes it.

## Forecasting (3.6c)

### What is forecast

"When will these open items be done?" For a **scope** (the list's filters; a project, milestone, cycle, goal, initiative or team), the **remaining** items are its live, open (Backlog, Unstarted, Started) items in visible, unarchived projects, counted now. The forecast gives:

- the dates by which the remaining items are **50, 85 and 95 percent likely** to be finished;
- where the scope has a due date (a milestone's or project's `dueDate`, a cycle's `endsOn`, a goal's or initiative's due date), **the chance of finishing by it**, unless it has passed;
- the distribution as a histogram of completion dates and a "Chance finished by" curve;
- what it was based on: the history's projects or team, its window, how many items finished in it, the share assumed, the number of trials, the seed, and the refusals or cautions.

Units are items (question 12, decision 320): Monte Carlo over item counts is the standard and needs no estimates.

### The history (decisions 318, 321 and 322)

- **Which completions** (question 13, decision 321): those of the projects the remaining items belong to, or, for a cycle, its team's; listed in the answer ("Based on Website and Mobile") and changeable to any subset, or to the team, or to the workspace. A milestone's own completions alone are rarely enough history.
- **The window** (question 10, decision 318): the last 12 weeks by default, chosen among 4, 8, 12 and 26 weeks, ending yesterday (today's partial day is left out), and never starting before the history's first recorded row (so a two-week-old project is not read as ten weeks of zeros).
- **The sample** (question 10, decision 318): the daily completion counts of each **working day** of the scope's calendar in the window. A completion on a non-working day is added to the next working day, so nothing is lost. The sample is ordered by date.
- **The share and growth** (question 14, decision 322): the forecast assumes the scope receives a stated share of that throughput, 100 % unless the person chooses 75, 50 or 25 %, and that no items are added to the scope; both assumptions are printed with the result ("Assumes these items get all of Website's throughput and no new items are added").

### The simulation

`forecastCompletion(input)` in `src/app-layer/domain/forecast/simulate.ts`, pure and client-safe (no database, clock, zone or environment; it imports `domain/schedule/calendar.ts` and its own `rng.ts`):

1. **Refusals first** (question 11, decision 319), each returning no date: remaining zero ("Nothing left to forecast: no open items"); fewer than 20 completions in the window ("Not enough history to forecast. These projects finished 12 items in the last 12 weeks; a forecast needs at least 20."); fewer than 20 working days of recorded history in the window ("Not enough history to forecast: this scope has 11 working days of history; a forecast needs at least 20."); a share of 0. Below 50 completions the result carries a caution: "Based on few items; the range is wide."
2. **The generator** is mulberry32 (`rng.ts`, copied), seeded by the input's seed.
3. **Each trial** (10,000 of them): remaining `L` = remaining items; for working day `d = 1, 2, …` of the scope's calendar after today, draw one day from the sample uniformly (`floor(rng() × n)`), take its count × the share, and subtract it, carrying fractions exactly (counts and shares are integers in hundredths, so a 50 % share of 3 is 150 hundredths, and the arithmetic stays in integers); the trial's outcome is the `d` at which `L ≤ 0`, or **beyond** if it reaches the cap of 520 working days (about two years).
4. **Percentiles**: the 10,000 outcomes are counted per day (integers allow a counting histogram, not a sort; study §7 item 6), and the 50th, 85th and 95th percentiles are the nearest-rank outcomes. A percentile whose outcome is beyond the cap is shown as "Later than <the cap's date>"; if the 50th is beyond the cap, the forecast refuses: "No forecast within two years at the recent pace: 4 items finished in the last 12 weeks."
5. **Dates**: an outcome `d` is `addWorkingDays(calendar, firstWorkingDayAfterToday, d − 1)`, so holidays ahead are skipped and the dates are calendar dates (ADR 0010 §1).
6. **Chance by the due date**: the share of trials whose date is on or before it.

The result carries the dates, the chance, the histogram (per date, the number of trials finishing that day, merged into weeks past 92 days), the cumulative curve, and `{ seed, trials, inputsDigest, historyFrom, historyTo, completions, workingDays, remaining, share }`.

### The seed (question 15, decision 323)

The seed is **derived from the inputs**: FNV-1a (32-bit) over the canonical JSON of the scope's key, the moment's epoch day, the sample, the remaining count, the share and the trial count. The same data on the same day gives the same dates on every reload and every machine; a new completion gives a new seed and a new draw, as it should. Tests and the back-test pass an explicit seed. The seed is shown under "How this was computed". mulberry32's integer arithmetic makes the stream identical on every JavaScript engine, and no floating-point sum enters a count.

### Calibration (H3; question 17, decision 325)

`src/app-layer/domain/forecast/backtest.ts`, pure: given a scope's daily completion series (as it stood at each origin) and the forecast's parameters, for every origin `T` five working days apart, from the first day with a full window and the minimum history to the last day that can be scored:

- forecast "the date of the next `N` completions after `T`" with the history as it stood at `T` (rows with `at ≤ T` only, so reopens and deletions after `T` are not seen), for `N` in 10, 25 and 50;
- the actual date is the day the `N`-th completion after `T` happened (as it stands now);
- an origin is **scorable** when the actual date is known, or when the forecast date has passed without it (a miss); otherwise it is left out;
- a hit is an actual date on or before the forecast's date; the result is each percentile's hit rate with its scorable count and a 95 % binomial band.

"When will N more items be done" is what the forecast answers for a fixed scope, and it is scorable without knowing the scope's future additions. **Published** (H3): `npm run forecast:calibration` runs the back-test over the 100k seed's back-dated history (below) and writes the table in `docs/analytics.md`, "Calibration": for each `N` and percentile, the hit rate and the number of origins, marked as synthetic history. A test on the 10k seed holds the 85 % hit rate between 80 and 90 % over at least 200 scorable origins (the band recorded in `docs/analytics.md`, never widened to pass). The real calibration is 9.1's weekly review. **Shown per scope**: each forecast's "How this was computed" gives its scope's own record when it has at least 10 scorable origins in the last 52 weeks ("Of 34 earlier forecasts here, the 85% date held 29 times"), computed live from the same completion read over a longer window; below that, "Not enough history to show how forecasts held here."

### What the forecast never does

It is never stored, cached, audited or logged per scope; it writes nothing; it never reads a description, comment, field value or estimate; it never invents a date the module did not compute (principle 4): 7.5's explanation reads the result and its inputs. It is not capacity-based (3.5 map: "forecasts stay throughput-based"), and it does not run the scheduling engine (an estimate-driven Monte Carlo over the engine, jira-auto-scheduler's model, is 7.5's to decide).

### Performance of the module

10,000 trials over a remaining count of 2,000 at one completion a day reach the cap; the module counts per day and stops a trial at the cap, so the worst case is 5.2 million draws: under 150 ms p95 in Node (`tests/unit/domain/forecast-performance.test.ts`, failing at four times the budget, as the engine's and the workload module's do). The typical case (500 remaining, 6 a day) is under 20 ms.

## Performance (H2's rules; decision 331)

- **The scale seed writes no history today**: `bulk.ts` inserts each item and its trigger writes one row at seed time (`bulk.ts` line 18; `verify.ts` checks one transition per item, line 121). 3.6a adds a **back-dated history**: for each seeded item, a plausible sequence over the 52 weeks before the anchor (created, started, done or cancelled, about 8 % reopened, about 3 % moved between projects, waiting-on spells on about 20 %, review rounds where the seed writes them), written by the seeding superuser directly into `WorkItemTransition` with explicit `at` and `fromStateCategory`, ending in each item's current picture (the verifier checks every item's last row equals its row). Throughput is non-stationary on purpose (a holiday dip, a slow quarter, growth across the year) so the back-test is not trivially calibrated. About 6 rows per item: 60,000 at 10k, 600,000 at 100k. Verified on every run, cached in the dump.
- **The coverage guard learns the record**: `tests/guards/plan-checked-queries-coverage.test.ts` adds `workItemTransition` to `PRISMA_READ` and `"WorkItemTransition"` to `GUARDED_TABLE`, which brings 2.5a's two burn-up reads into scope; they are plan-checked with the new ones.
- **Plan-checked queries** (`PLAN_CHECKED_QUERIES`, each with its index, a 10k `maxBuffers` and a 100k `maxBuffers100k`): "analytics: completions in a window, workspace" and "…, one project" (`WorkItemTransition_completions`), "analytics: the first start of completed items" (`WorkItemTransition_tenantId_workItemId_at_idx`), "analytics: flow over a project" (the same index, the 20,000-item project at 100k), "analytics: waits over a project", "cycles: the burn-up's items and rows" (`WorkItemTransition_tenantId_cycleId_workItemId_idx`, `…_workItemId_at_idx`), "dashboards: a person's list" (small, unguarded table, listed for its index). Budgets estimated beside each until measured.
- **Statements per request** (`tests/perf/statements.test.ts`): each analytics read at most 7 (the tenant binding, visibility, calendars, the filter resolution's reads, the aggregate, names); the forecast at most 9 (with its remaining count and the calibration's longer read); a dashboard's list at most 4. A dashboard page is one request per widget, each within its own count.
- **Latency** (decision 167's method; budgets only go down): `analytics-throughput` (the workspace, 12 weeks: p50 200 ms, p95 400 at 100k), `analytics-flow` (the 20,000-item project, 12 weeks: p50 350, p95 700), `analytics-forecast` (a project: p50 250, p95 500), reported and not enforced until measured, as the timeline's and the roadmap's were; `dashboard-widgets-set` a `write` scenario run last.
- **Pre-aggregation** (option B of question 23) is the fallback if the nightly 100k run misses the flow budget: a per-project daily count table kept by a nightly job, giving up label, type and assignee filters on flow charts.

## H3 and 3.6

The roadmap's H3 names three checks for 3.6; the pull requests carry them and more:

- **Same seed, same forecast**: `forecastCompletion` byte-equal for equal inputs, across five process time zones (each in its own process), and on reload (the derived seed); a different seed changes the draw and keeps every percentile within the sampling error a test states.
- **Tiny or empty history never a confident date**: property tests over random histories below each minimum (no date ever returned, the refusal names the counts); remaining zero refused; a 50th percentile at the cap refused; a caution below 50 completions.
- **Calibration**: the back-test on the 10k seed holds the 85 % hit rate between 80 and 90 % over at least 200 scorable origins; `npm run forecast:calibration` writes `docs/analytics.md`, "Calibration", from the 100k seed; the back-test never reads a row after its origin (a property test plants a later reopen and checks the origin's forecast is unchanged).
- **Monotonicity**: with one seed, more remaining items never give an earlier date; a larger count on every sampled day never gives a later one; `p50 ≤ p85 ≤ p95`; the chance by a due date never falls as the due date moves later.
- **The measures**: the SQL reads equal the pure module on random histories (reopens, deletions, restores, moves, inserts into Done, purges); the flow sweep equals 2.5a's per-day scan; a completion counts once; cancelled and deleted items never count; elapsed working time removes whole non-working days and is never negative; waits never overlap per item and person; nearest-rank percentiles on hand-computed vectors.
- **Visibility and keys**: per-person waiting rows absent for every role without `analytics.people` and both custom roles; a guest refused on every route; a moved item counted only while in a visible project.

## ADR impacts (applied 2026-11-27, decision 309)

4. **Refusals.** The minimums and their wording (question 11); a refusal returns no date.
5. **Calibration.** The back-test's definition, its publication and the per-scope record (question 17).
6. **Dashboards.** Personal and shared, the default in code, widgets over the list's filters (questions 18 to 20).

**Words and the record**

1. **What are the new things called, and where are the rules recorded?** (decision 309)
   - A (recommended). "Analytics" (the sidebar entry), "Throughput", "Cycle time", "Lead time", "Age", "Cumulative flow", "Time waiting", "Forecast" with "N% likely" dates, "Dashboard" (`Dashboard`, the My Work row's ban qualified) and "Widget" (`DashboardWidget`, the mirrored component's word); a new ADR 0014 "Analytics and forecasting" and ADR 0006 rows. Industry words people know, none banned, and the forecast never says "confidence".
   - B. The same words, but "Insights" for the area and no new ADR (the rules in `docs/analytics.md` and ADR 0010). One fewer document; the forecasting and privacy rules have no decision record.
   - C. As A, but "Time in progress" for cycle time and "Time to done" for lead time, so neither shares a word with Cycle or Lead. No word reused; unfamiliar to anyone coming from another tool.
   - **Decided:** A, the words as proposed and a new ADR 0014. Applied: ADR 0014 (Accepted), ADR 0006 "Analytics words of sub-phase 3.6" with My Work's ban of "Dashboard" qualified (decision 309, 2026-11-27).

2. **Does the transition record learn the state an item came from?** (decision 310)
   - A (recommended). Add `fromStateCategory` (the category before the change, written by the trigger, backfilled once) and a partial index on completions. A completion becomes one indexed row, so throughput, cycle time and forecasts stay fast at 100,000 items.
   - B. Nothing new; every read reconstructs completions with `LAG` over each item's rows. No migration; every read sorts all the scope's rows, so large scopes get slow.
   - C. Record the whole picture before the change (project, cycle, milestone, deletion, waiting on). Flow charts read only rows inside their window; doubles the record's width for every change, mostly unused.
   - **Decided:** A, `fromStateCategory` written by the trigger and backfilled, and the partial index `WorkItemTransition_completions`. Applied: ADR 0002 §14 (decision 310, 2026-11-27).

**Definitions**

3. **When does an item count as completed, for throughput and durations?** (decision 311)
   - A (recommended). Once, on the day of its last move into Done, if it is in Done and not deleted at the moment of reading; a reopened item moves to its later completion, a deleted one leaves the history, cancelled never counts. Every item counts once; history can change after a reopen or a deletion.
   - B. Every move into Done counts on its day, and a reopen or a deletion of a Done item subtracts on its own day (net counts). A past week never changes; a week can show negative throughput, and one item counts more than once.
   - C. Every move into Done counts (gross), nothing subtracts. Simplest and stable; reopened work is double-counted and forecasts run optimistic.
   - **Decided:** A, once, at the last move into Done as of the moment of reading; a reopen moves the count, a deletion retracts it, cancelled never counts (decision 311, 2026-11-27).

4. **Where do cycle time and lead time start?** (decision 312)
   - A (recommended). Cycle time from the item's first move into Started; lead time from its creation. Items done without ever being Started have no cycle time and are counted as "Done without starting". The usual definitions; a team that skips Started sees those items only in lead time.
   - B. Cycle time from its first move out of Backlog or Unstarted (into Started or Done); lead time from creation. Every completed item has a cycle time; skipped items show near zero and pull the percentiles down.
   - C. Cycle time from the first move into Started; lead time from its first move out of Backlog (the moment the team committed to it). Lead time ignores time in the backlog; items created straight into Unstarted start at creation, items that stay in Backlog are never "committed".
   - **Decided:** A, cycle time from the first move into Started, lead time from creation, "Done without starting" counted (decision 312, 2026-11-27).

5. **How are durations measured?** (decision 313)
   - A (recommended). Elapsed working time on the item's own working calendar: the time between the two moments less every whole weekend day and holiday, in days to one decimal. Weekends and holidays do not inflate cycle time or time waiting, and short spans stay short (a 3-hour wait is 0.1 days).
   - B. Elapsed calendar time, in days to one decimal. Simplest and what most tools show; a Friday-to-Monday wait counts three days.
   - C. Whole working days inclusive (the engine's count: started and done the same day is 1). Matches the timeline's day arithmetic; every short wait counts a full day.
   - **Decided:** A, elapsed working time on the item's own working calendar, in days to one decimal (decision 313, 2026-11-27).

6. **How are parents and sub-items counted?** (decision 314)
   - A (recommended). Every item counts once, parents and sub-items alike, as progress counts (decision 212); a type filter narrows to one level. One rule everywhere; a team that tracks both levels sees both in throughput.
   - B. Only leaves (items with no live sub-items at the moment of reading). Counts the work done once; a parent closing after its children counts nothing, and the rule depends on structure the record does not hold.
   - C. Only top-level items. Counts deliverables; sub-items' work disappears from throughput.
   - **Decided:** A, every item counts once, parents and sub-items alike (decision 314, 2026-11-27).

7. **How do filters, moves and archived projects apply to history?** (decision 315)
   - A (recommended). Recorded fields (project, team, cycle, milestone, state, deletion) are judged as recorded at each moment, so a moved item counts in each project for its time there; every other filter (labels, type, assignee, custom fields …) judges current values; archived projects' history counts, deleted projects' does not. Correct wherever the record knows; the usual approximation elsewhere, stated on the page.
   - B. Every filter judges current values, the project included: an item's whole history counts in its current project. One simple rule; moving a project's items rewrites both projects' past throughput.
   - C. History charts accept only recorded-field filters. Always exact; no "throughput of Bugs".
   - **Decided:** A, recorded fields judged as recorded, other filters by current values, archived projects' history counted (decision 315, 2026-11-27).

8. **Which time zone draws the day boundaries, and does the cycle burn-up follow?** (decision 316)
   - A (recommended). The scope's working calendar's zone (one team's calendar for a one-team scope, else the workspace's), the rule the calendar view uses; durations on each item's own calendar; the cycle burn-up moves to its team's calendar zone, which defaults to `NOTIFICATIONS_TZ`, so nothing changes for a workspace with one zone. One rule across analytics, the calendar and the cycle page.
   - B. The workspace calendar's zone always. Every chart agrees with every other; a team abroad sees its days cut at the wrong midnight.
   - C. `NOTIFICATIONS_TZ` always, as the burn-up does today. No change to the burn-up; ignores the calendars' zones (decision 191).
   - **Decided:** A, the scope's working calendar's zone, durations on each item's calendar, and the cycle burn-up following. Applied: ADR 0010's consequences (decision 316, 2026-11-27).

**Time waiting**

9. **Does time waiting include review steps?** (decision 317) Waiting on is cleared while a review round is open (decisions 152 and 153), so the field alone shows no waiting during reviews.
   - A (recommended). Yes: a reviewer waits from the step's opening until their latest response, their withdrawal or the step's end, read from the review rows, which are never rewritten once ended (ADR 0009 §4); shown beside field waits ("3.5 days, 2 of them as a reviewer"). Answers decision 76's "whose move it is" for the whole life of an item.
   - B. No: waiting on only, and review response times shown separately on a later review page. Simpler read; time waiting looks short exactly where items wait longest.
   - C. No: waiting on only, and nothing for reviews. Least work; reviews invisible in analytics.
   - **Decided:** A, review waits from the review rows, shown beside field waits. Applied: ADR 0009's consequences (decision 317, 2026-11-27).

**Forecasting**

10. **What does the simulation sample, and over which window?** (decision 318)
    - A (recommended). The daily completion counts of each working day of the scope's calendar (a completion on a weekend folds into the next working day), over the last 12 weeks by default, chosen among 4, 8, 12 and 26 and never before the history's first record, simulated working day by working day and turned into dates through the calendar, holidays included. Dates to the day, holidays ahead respected, and a recent window that follows the team's current pace.
    - B. Weekly completion counts over a fixed 12 weeks, simulated week by week, dates given as the week's last working day. Fewer zero days and a steadier sample; dates only to the week, a holiday week ahead not seen, and no choice of window.
    - C. Daily counts over calendar days, weekends included, over all recorded history. Simplest; ignores working calendars, and a team's pace of a year ago weighs as much as last month's.
    - **Decided:** A, daily completions on the scope's working days over the last 12 weeks by default (4, 8, 12 or 26) (decision 318, 2026-11-27).

11. **What is the minimum history, and what does a refusal say?** (decision 319)
    - A (recommended). At least 20 completed items in the window and 20 working days of recorded history; below either, no date, and a sentence with the counts ("These projects finished 12 items in the last 12 weeks; a forecast needs at least 20."); a caution under 50 items ("Based on few items; the range is wide."); no forecast when even the 50 % date is beyond two years. Honest at the low end without refusing most young projects.
    - B. At least 10 items and 10 working days. Forecasts sooner; ranges built on very few samples.
    - C. At least 50 items and 40 working days. Rarely wrong; new teams wait two months for any forecast.
    - **Decided:** A, at least 20 completed items and 20 working days, a refusal naming the counts, a caution under 50, none past two years (decision 319, 2026-11-27).

12. **What does the forecast count?** (decision 320)
    - A (recommended). Items only. The standard for throughput forecasting; needs no estimates, so every team gets one.
    - B. Items, or points or hours where every team in scope estimates in that unit (sampling daily completed points; remaining as the sum of open estimates). Fits estimating teams; unestimated open items must be refused or guessed.
    - C. Points or hours where available, items otherwise. Most precise for estimating teams; two kinds of forecast that read alike.
    - **Decided:** A, items only (decision 320, 2026-11-27).

13. **Whose completions feed a scope's forecast?** (decision 321)
    - A (recommended). The completions of the projects the open items belong to (a cycle: its team's), named on the result and changeable to a subset, the team or the workspace. A milestone or filter gets enough history; the person sees and can change what it is based on.
    - B. The scope's own completions only (a milestone's own completed items). The purest match; most milestones and filters are refused for thin history.
    - C. The workspace's completions always. Always enough history; a small team's milestone is forecast from the whole workspace's pace.
    - **Decided:** A, the completions of the scope's projects (a cycle: its team's), named and changeable (decision 321, 2026-11-27).

14. **What does a forecast assume about its share of that throughput and about new items?** (decision 322)
    - A (recommended). All of the history's throughput unless the person chooses 75, 50 or 25 %, and no new items; both assumptions printed with the dates. Simple, visible and correctable; optimistic until someone sets a share.
    - B. All of it, no new items, no choice. Simplest; a project working on three milestones gets three optimistic forecasts.
    - C. The share computed from the scope's own recent share of completions, and new items added at the history's rate of growth. Most realistic; a new milestone has no share yet, and two derived numbers are harder to explain.
    - **Decided:** A, all of that throughput unless a share is chosen, no new items, both printed (decision 322, 2026-11-27).

15. **How is a forecast seeded?** (decision 323)
    - A (recommended). From its inputs (a hash of the scope, the day, the sample, the remaining count, the share and the trial count): the same data gives the same dates on every reload and machine, and the seed is shown. Stable pages and reproducible answers; any new completion changes the draw slightly.
    - B. One fixed seed for every forecast. Fully reproducible; every forecast shares one random stream, which is harmless but looks arbitrary.
    - C. A random seed per read, shown. Honest about randomness; the dates jitter by a day between reloads.
    - **Decided:** A, a seed derived from the inputs and shown (decision 323, 2026-11-27).

16. **What does a forecast show, and where?** (decision 324)
    - A (recommended). The 50, 85 and 95 percent dates, the chance of finishing by the scope's due date, a histogram and a "chance finished by" curve; on milestone, project, cycle, goal and initiative pages, as a "Forecast" action for the Work items list's current filters, and as a dashboard widget. "Any scope", where people already look.
    - B. The three dates only, on the Analytics page only. Smallest; people must go looking, and the due date question is unanswered.
