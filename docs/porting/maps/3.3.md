## Summary

- **The calendar view** is the `CALENDAR` layout (already in `ViewLayout`, refused until now) on the Work items, project, cycle and milestone pages and in saved views: a month grid of items on their due dates, cycles as bands, milestones and project due dates as markers, non-working days from 3.1's working calendars. It is **ported from inflect's `CalendarMonth`** (decision 242), not a library. Moving an item opens 3.1c's "Change dates" confirmation (decision 241).
- **The connection model is ported from inflect** (`UserCalendarConnection`, `UserCalendarEventMapping`, `TenantCalendarConsent`, the token module, the pure reconciler, the sweep, the consent-health rules), renamed to `CalendarConnection`, `CalendarSyncedEvent` and `TenantCalendarSettings` (decision 243). What inflect **did not build** is the part that talks to the providers: its push job pushes nothing and it never reads back. The Google and Microsoft clients and the whole inbound direction are new code.
- **Two-way, narrowly** (decision 225): Islestone is the source of truth. It pushes the due dates of the person's open items assigned to them (decision 226) and **the due dates of the milestones of those items' projects, read-only** (decision 227, a departure); the only thing read back is **a changed day on an item event Islestone created**, applied as the person's own versioned edit, only while the item's due date is still the one Islestone last pushed (decision 230). Everything else changed in the calendar, a moved milestone event included, is undone at the next sync, with one in-app notification.
- **What an event carries** (decision 229, a departure): the item's title (or the milestone's name) and **an absolute link back** to it in the event's description, as a private, free, all-day event on the due date; nothing else. The link carries the workspace's address and the item's key (or the project's key and the milestone's id).
- **A dedicated "Islestone" calendar** per connection (decision 228), so Islestone reads and writes only its own events; Google's `calendar.app.created` scope, Microsoft's `Calendars.ReadWrite`.
- **The sign-in apps** (decision 236, a departure): calendar connections use the same Google and Microsoft OAuth clients as sign-in (`GOOGLE_*`, `MICROSOFT_*`, already in `src/env.ts`), through their own hand-written flow outside the NextAuth seam, their own redirect URI and only the calendar scope. No calendar client variables are added.
- **Personal and opt-in twice** (decisions 234, 235): an Owner or Admin turns calendar sync on for the workspace (`integrations.manage`) behind the operator's switch, and for Microsoft records the customer directory's admin consent (decision 237); then each member who sees work items connects their own calendar, Viewers as a read-only feed. A GUEST sees and connects nothing (decision 204).
- **Tokens** are one encrypted blob per connection in a manifest column under the workspace's key (decision 238). A leaving member's events are deleted, then the token (decision 239).
- **Jobs**: a dispatcher every 15 minutes, a per-connection sync (push, then read back), a teardown; plus a push enqueued after any commit that changes what a person should see (decision 233). Polling, not provider webhooks, so no new public endpoint.
- **Egress**: fixed provider hosts over plain `fetch`; a server-supplied paging link only through `safeFetch` with the host pinned. No CSP change. Google and Microsoft enter the sub-processor register (decision 240), naming titles, due dates and links.
- **Three pull requests** (decision 244): 3.3a the calendar view, 3.3b connections, the push and milestone events, 3.3c the read-back and H3's tests. About 6,200 lines of source and 5,200 of tests.

## Vocabulary (ADR 0006, decided)

- **"Calendar" alone** stays reserved by the Working calendar row for "the `CALENDAR` layout and 3.3's sync". The layout's label is "Calendar"; the sync's models qualify the word.
- **"Event"** is reserved for automation; the provider's own term is used inside the adapter, and Islestone's record of a remote entry is a **Synced event** (decision 243).
- **"Subscription" (as a model)** is banned, so if webhooks are ever added the stored Graph subscription is a "watch". **"Copy"** is banned schema-wide, so the record is not `CalendarEventCopy`. **"Session"** unqualified is banned. **"Sync engine"** is ADR 0005's deferred option; "sync" alone is free.

The layout switcher (List, Board, Timeline) gains Calendar where Timeline is offered; `SAVED_VIEW_LAYOUTS` gains `CALENDAR`; `SavedViewConfigSchema` gains `calendar: { showCycles, showMilestones }` (`.strict()`); `saved-view-config-keys.test.ts` gains them. Every list filter applies through `resolveListFiltersInTx`, so a calendar and a list with the same filters hold the same items.

### The read

`GET /api/t/:slug/work-items/calendar?from=YYYY-MM-DD&to=YYYY-MM-DD&<the list's filters>` (`requirePermission('work_items.view')`), usecase `getWorkItemCalendar` in `src/app-layer/usecases/work-item-calendar.ts`:

1. The window is at most 62 days; 400 `CALENDAR_RANGE` otherwise. Dates are date-only, never passed through a zone.
2. Items with a due date in the window under the list's filters, closed categories included (shown done), at most **2,000**, read one past the limit; above it 422 `CALENDAR_TOO_LARGE` with the count ("Narrow the filter: 2,311 items this month"), never a cut-off grid. Items with no due date are not shown; the summary line counts them ("37 items have no due date").
3. Cycles overlapping the window of the teams in scope (`cycles.view`; a caller without it gets `omitted: ['cycles']`, named), milestones and project due dates in the window (`projects.view`), each bounded.
4. The working calendar of the scope (one team's, else the workspace's) for shading, and today in its zone (3.1's rule).

Every raw read carries `tenantId`, `deletedAt` and `projectVisibilitySql`; H2's plan registry gains the item read. Description, comments and field values are not read. Not audited.

### The client

- **Data:** `useWorkItemCalendar(scope, filters, month)` in `src/components/work-items/data/`, registered with `work-item-cache.ts` so a write anywhere places the changed row.
- **The grid** (decision 242): the ported `CalendarMonth` (a 7×6 grid, week start from the mirrored `week-start.ts`), chips "ISL-12 Title" with the state category tone, "+N more" opening the day panel (the list's columns for that day's items), cycle bands across the top of each week row, diamonds for milestones and project due dates, non-working days and holidays shaded with a legend; keyboard as inflect's (arrows, Home/End, Enter opens the day); a chip opens the item's sheet.
- **Moving an item** (decision 241): dragging a chip to another day (pragmatic-drag-and-drop, already in the tree) or "Move to date…" from the chip's menu (the keyboard path, with the mirrored `DatePicker`) opens 3.1c's `ChangeDatesDialog` with the due date moved (and the start date, if any, by the same number of days), the engine's preview and "Also move the N items it pushes later"; confirming sends the same `PATCH` or `POST /work-items/bulk/dates`. ## Calendar sync: the model (3.3b)

```prisma
enum CalendarProvider        { GOOGLE MICROSOFT }
enum CalendarConnectionState { CONNECTED REVOKED DISCONNECTING CALENDAR_REMOVED }
enum CalendarSyncedEventState { PUSHED PENDING_DELETE FAILED REMOVED_IN_CALENDAR }
enum CalendarSyncedEventKind  { WORK_ITEM MILESTONE }

model TenantCalendarSettings {                  // one row per workspace; no row = sync off
  tenantId                      String   @id
  syncEnabled                   Boolean  @default(false)
  microsoftExternalTenantId     String?             // the customer's directory the consent was granted in
  microsoftConsentGrantedAt     DateTime?
  microsoftConsentGrantedByUserId String?           // actor stamp, no FK
  microsoftConsentRevokedAt     DateTime?
  version                       Int      @default(1)
  updatedAt                     DateTime @updatedAt
}

model CalendarConnection {
  id                 String                  @id @default(cuid())
  tenantId           String
  userId             String                  // the member who connected; FK to User (cascade with the user only, 8.3 tears down first)
  provider           CalendarProvider
  state              CalendarConnectionState @default(CONNECTED)
  tokenEncrypted     String?                 // ENCRYPTED (manifest): { accessToken, refreshToken, expiresAt } as one blob; NULL once revoked or disconnected
  scopesGranted      String[]                // what the provider granted, which may be narrower than asked
  externalCalendarId String?                 // the dedicated calendar's id at the provider
  syncCursor         String?                 // Google's syncToken; opaque, useless without the token
  revokedReason      String?                 // a fixed phrase, never a provider body (CHECK: one of a closed list)
  lastSyncedAt       DateTime?
  connectedAt        DateTime                @default(now())
  createdAt          DateTime                @default(now())
  updatedAt          DateTime                @updatedAt
  @@unique([tenantId, userId, provider])
  @@unique([id, tenantId])
  @@index([tenantId, state])
  @@index([userId])
}

model CalendarSyncedEvent {
  id             String                   @id @default(cuid())
  tenantId       String
  connectionId   String                   // FK (connectionId, tenantId) -> CalendarConnection, RESTRICT: rows go only after their remote events
  kind           CalendarSyncedEventKind
  workItemId     String?                  // set when kind = WORK_ITEM; no FK: the row must outlive an item's purge to delete its remote event
  milestoneId    String?                  // set when kind = MILESTONE (decision 227); no FK, for the same reason
  remoteEventId  String                   // empty until the first push succeeds
  clientEventId  String                   // deterministic (length-prefixed sha256 over workspace, connection, kind and id, 40 hex)
  pushedDueDate  DateTime                 @db.Date   // what Islestone last pushed: the read-back's check (decision 230)
  contentHash    String                   // over exactly what is pushed (title, date, link)
  remoteVersion  String?                  // Google etag / Graph changeKey after our last write: our own write is not read back as a change
  state          CalendarSyncedEventState @default(PUSHED)
  lastError      String?                  // a code from a closed list, never a provider body
  lastPushedAt   DateTime?
  createdAt      DateTime                 @default(now())
  updatedAt      DateTime                 @updatedAt
  @@index([tenantId, connectionId, state])
  @@index([tenantId, workItemId])
  @@index([tenantId, milestoneId])
}
// In the migration: CHECK exactly one of workItemId, milestoneId, matching kind;
// partial uniques (connectionId, workItemId) WHERE workItemId IS NOT NULL and
// (connectionId, milestoneId) WHERE milestoneId IS NOT NULL.
```

- **Why not `Account`** (inflect's reasoning holds, decision 236 does not change it): `Account` is a sign-in provider link with no `tenantId`, so it cannot carry an RLS policy or tell "connected for this workspace" from another; and a calendar grant never rides on a sign-in token. The two share an OAuth client, not a row or a token.
- **Why the membership is not the FK**: a removed member's events must still be deleted from their calendar (decision 239) with the token the row holds; a cascade from the membership would lose the only record that the events exist. Removal moves the connection to `DISCONNECTING` and enqueues the teardown; the rows go after the remote events.
- **No FK to the work item or the milestone** for the same reason (`WorkItemTransition`'s precedent): an item's purge or a milestone's delete must not take the record of its remote event with it; the next sync deletes the event, then the row.
- **One row per connection and item or milestone**: two partial uniques, written in the migration with the CHECK (Prisma cannot declare either); a catalogue test reads them.
- **Tenant-table rules** for all three: `tenantId`, tenant-leading indexes, the four RLS policies with FORCE, grants in the creating migration (DELETE on `CalendarSyncedEvent` and `CalendarConnection` for the teardown; none on the settings row), two-tenant tests in `ISOLATION_TESTED`; Layer B and C of `schema-index-coverage`.
- **Encryption** (decision 238): `CalendarConnection.tokenEncrypted` joins `ENCRYPTED_FIELDS`, encrypted under the workspace's DEK by the extension, so the tenant DEK sweep and the master-key sweep both rewrite it. The other text columns are opaque identifiers or fixed codes, classified plaintext in `encryption-manifest-coverage.test.ts` with reasons. No email address of the connected account is stored (no scope returns one).
- **Migrations**: `202612xx130000_calendar_sync_enums` (four new types, not new values, so one migration), `202612xx130100_calendar_sync` (the tables, CHECKs, partial uniques, RLS, grants), `202612xx130200_notification_type_calendar_change` (the one new `NotificationType` value, alone, as 2.4b and 2.7 did), each forward-only.

## Connecting a calendar (3.3b)

1. **Settings → Calendar sync** (`integrations.manage`, Owner and Admin; decision 234): "Allow members to sync their calendars" (`PUT /calendar-sync/settings { syncEnabled }`, audited `CALENDAR_SYNC_SETTINGS_SET`), and for Microsoft "Authorise Microsoft 365" (decision 237): `POST /calendar-sync/microsoft-consent` returns the `/adminconsent` URL for the sign-in app registration with the calendar scope (sealed state cookie); `/api/integrations/calendar/admin-callback` receives `admin_consent=True&tenant=<id>` (no code), records the directory and who granted it, audited `CALENDAR_MICROSOFT_CONSENT_GRANTED`; "Withdraw" stamps the revocation and tears down every Microsoft connection (`…_REVOKED`). The page shows how many members are connected, never who.
2. **The member's own page** (`/t/<slug>/inbox/preferences` gains a "Calendar sync" section; the copy says what leaves: **"Your open items' titles, due dates and links, and your projects' milestones, in a calendar called Islestone"**): `POST /calendar-sync/connect?provider=google|microsoft` (`requirePermission('work_items.view')`; refused 409 `CALENDAR_SYNC_OFF` while the workspace switch is off, `MICROSOFT_NOT_AUTHORISED` without consent) answers `{ authorizeUrl }` as JSON, which the browser navigates to: a top-level navigation, so the CSP is unchanged.
3. **The state** is an HttpOnly, `SameSite=Lax`, 10-minute cookie, HMAC-sealed (the platform key), carrying a random state, the workspace slug, the member's user id, the provider and the **PKCE verifier** (S256); single-use (its nonce recorded in Redis for 10 minutes).
4. **The callback**, `/api/integrations/calendar/callback` (outside `/t/` because each provider redirects to one fixed URI; a signed-in path, not public, not a machine caller; distinct from NextAuth's `/api/auth/callback/<provider>`): the state must match and its user must be the session's user, else nothing is stored; `error=` from the provider records only the code; the workspace switch and (Microsoft) the consent are re-checked; the code is exchanged with the verifier; a missing refresh token refuses (inflect's rule); the dedicated calendar is created ("Islestone"); the connection is upserted (re-connecting replaces and clears a revocation), audited `CALENDAR_CONNECTED` (provider and granted scope count, never a token); the first sync is enqueued.
5. **The apps** (decision 236): the authorize and token calls use the sign-in apps' `GOOGLE_CLIENT_ID` / `GOOGLE_CLIENT_SECRET` and `MICROSOFT_CLIENT_ID` / `MICROSOFT_CLIENT_SECRET`, read through `@/env`. The calendar flow is still hand-written in `src/app-layer/integrations/providers/calendar/` and imports nothing from `next-auth` (ADR 0004 A3 untouched; `local/next-auth-single-seam` holds it). Its redirect URIs (`/api/integrations/calendar/callback`, and for Microsoft `/api/integrations/calendar/admin-callback`) are added to the same app registrations beside sign-in's; `docs/deployment.md` gains them in 3.3b, as the operator steps to register. Microsoft's authority is the consented directory's id, `organizations` before consent, whatever `MICROSOFT_TENANT_ID` sign-in uses; calendar sync for a customer's directory therefore needs the registration to accept other directories, as sign-in at `common` already does.
6. **Scopes** (decision 228): Google `https://www.googleapis.com/auth/calendar.app.created`; Microsoft `offline_access Calendars.ReadWrite`. Recorded as constants so a widening is a reviewed diff. **Only the calendar scope is asked** (incremental authorization): never `openid`, `email` or `profile`, and Google's authorize URL does **not** set `include_granted_scopes`, so the calendar token carries the calendar scope alone and not the sign-in grant's scopes (the reading recorded for decision 236).
7. **What the flow never does**: write `Account`; link an identity; redeem an invite; read the external account's address; touch a sign-in token. `oauth-email-trust.ts` has nothing to judge, and a member may connect a calendar under a different external account than they sign in with.
8. **Consequences of one app per provider** (decision 236), recorded as the owner asked:
   - **Google's verification of the calendar scope applies to the sign-in app**: the scope joins that app's consent screen configuration, and its verification (the class of `calendar.app.created` was not verified from here) is the sign-in app's. Whether a pending or refused verification changes what the sign-in consent screen shows was not verified from here; re-check before 3.3b.
   - **A person removing the app at Google** (their account's third-party access) or **from Microsoft's My Apps** withdraws both the sign-in linkage's consent and the calendar grant: their next sign-in with that provider asks consent again, and their connections go `REVOKED` at the next sync.
   - **A customer admin removing the enterprise application** in their Microsoft directory removes Microsoft sign-in for that directory as well as calendar access; withdrawing only the calendar consent is Settings → Calendar sync's "Withdraw".
   - **Islestone never calls Google's revoke endpoint** (the rule recorded for decision 236): with one app, revoking a calendar token may withdraw the person's whole grant to the app, their sign-in consent and their calendar connections in other workspaces included. A disconnected or torn-down connection's token is destroyed locally instead, and the copy tells the member where to remove Islestone from their Google account themselves, and that doing so also affects signing in with Google. (Microsoft offers no per-grant revoke for a delegated refresh token either.)
   - **The deployment doc** gains the two redirect URIs to register for each provider.

## The push (3.3b)

- **Items** (decision 226): for each `CONNECTED` connection of an ACTIVE member in a workspace with sync on, the desired set is generated by one function, `generateSyncedEvents(ctx)`, under the member re-resolved when the job runs (`resolveMemberContext`; `null` → the teardown, never a system context; `actorType: 'JOB'`): their open items (Backlog, Unstarted, Started) assigned to them, with a due date from 30 days ago to 365 days ahead, in live, visible (`visibleProjects`), unarchived projects; nearest first, at most **1,000**; above it the nearest 1,000 are pushed and the member's settings say "1,000 of 1,240 items are in your calendar" (the count is the read's, never guessed).
- **Milestones** (decision 227, a departure): the milestones of the live, visible, unarchived projects in which the member has at least one open item assigned to them (with or without a due date), with a due date in the same window, nearest first, at most **200**, are pushed as read-only events. A milestone the member can no longer reach (the project archived, deleted or hidden, or no open assigned item left in it) is removed like an item.
- **What an event carries** (decision 229, a departure): summary = the item's title exactly (plaintext by ADR 0002 §11, already sanitised), or the milestone's name exactly; description = **one absolute link and nothing else**, built with `appLink`: `/t/<slug>/work-items/<KEY>` for an item, `/t/<slug>/projects/<projectKey>/milestones/<milestoneId>` for a milestone (Google `description`, Graph `body` as plain text). An all-day event on the due date (Google `start.date` = due, `end.date` = due + 1, exclusive; Graph `isAllDay: true` with midnight `start` and `end` in one zone); visibility private (Google `visibility: private`, Graph `sensitivity: private`); free (Google `transparency: transparent`, Graph `showAs: free`); no location, no attendees, no key or project name beyond the link, no reminder overrides. A private extended property holds the client id so Islestone can recognise its own events; it carries no content. The link opens Islestone and needs a signed-in member; it never carries a token. An item moved to another project gets its new key's link at the next push (the content hash changes); the old key still resolves as an alias.
- **Reconcile** (the ported pure diff): insert for a desired item or milestone with no row; patch when the content hash differs or the row is `FAILED`; delete for a row whose item or milestone is no longer desired: deletes are driven by the rows. A nightly run over an unchanged calendar makes no provider call.
- **Duplicates** (H3): the client id is deterministic per (workspace, connection, kind, id). Google takes it as the event's `id` (40 lowercase hex is inside base32hex) and answers 409 to a repeat; Google documents that collisions are not guaranteed to be detected at creation, so a retry after an unrecorded insert first lists the dedicated calendar by `privateExtendedProperty=islestone=<clientEventId>` and adopts what it finds. Graph takes it as `transactionId`. The row is written immediately after the remote insert; the partial uniques hold one row per item and per milestone.
- **Failures**: a remote delete that fails keeps the row (`PENDING_DELETE`) and retries next run; a push that fails marks `FAILED` with a code; an auth failure (`400 invalid_grant` on refresh, 401/403 on a call) is terminal for the connection: `REVOKED`, token destroyed, one counter, audited `CALENDAR_CONNECTION_REVOKED` as the workspace system user, no further calls, no retry (`NonRetryableJobError`); a throttle (429, `Retry-After`) is not terminal. One member's failure never stops the batch.
- **Loop prevention**: after every write the row stores the provider's version (`etag` / `changeKey`), so the read-back does not mistake Islestone's own write for a person's.

## The read-back (3.3c; decisions 225, 227, 230, 231, 232, 235)

- **What is read**: only the dedicated calendar, only events Islestone created (matched by `remoteEventId`). Google: `events.list` with the stored `syncToken` (deleted events included); a `410 Gone` resets to a full listing of the window. Microsoft: `GET /me/calendars/{id}/calendarView` over the push window, compared with each row's `remoteVersion`, because Graph v1.0's `calendarView/delta` covers the primary calendar only. An event a person added to the Islestone calendar by hand is ignored, never imported.
- **A day changed on an item event** (decision 225): the new due date is the event's date (decision 231: for a timed or multi-day event, its last day in the event's own time zone). It is applied **as the member**, re-resolved, through the same versioned write as the detail's due-date edit: `work_items.edit`, the field set (`FIELD_HIDDEN`), `START_AFTER_DUE`, `PROJECT_ARCHIVED`, `ITEM_CLOSED`, the item's `version`; audited `WORK_ITEM_UPDATED` naming `dueDate` with `via: 'calendar'` and the provider in `detailsJson`; the due bell and the watcher fan-out run as for any edit. **Only while the item's due date still equals the row's `pushedDueDate`** (decision 230): otherwise Islestone's newer date wins.
- **When it is not applied** (a stale change, no edit permission, a hidden field, a closed item, start after due) **and every moved milestone event** (decision 227: milestones are read-only from a calendar): the next push puts Islestone's date back, and (decision 230) the member gets one in-app notification, `CALENDAR_CHANGE_NOT_APPLIED` ("Your calendar change to ISL-12 was not applied: the due date changed in Islestone" / "…you cannot edit this item" / "Milestones cannot be moved from your calendar: Launch"), in-app only, no email, under their preferences; it names the item or milestone, never the text. One notification per event and changed day, not one per sync.
- **A Viewer's connection is a read-only feed** (decision 235): nothing is read back from it, so a moved event simply returns at the next push, with no notification, as the member's settings say ("Changes in your calendar are not sent back"). The same holds for a member who loses `work_items.edit` everywhere: the read-back stops when the re-resolved member holds no edit key (the rule recorded for decisions 230 and 235: a notification answers a change the member could have made; a read-only feed promised none).
- **Anything else changed** (title, the description's link, attendees, colour) is not read; the next push restores what the content hash says changed.
- **An event deleted in the calendar** (decision 232): the item or milestone is untouched; the row moves to `REMOVED_IN_CALENDAR` and it is not pushed again until its due date changes or the member chooses "Resync my calendar". **The whole Islestone calendar deleted**: the connection moves to `CALENDAR_REMOVED`, its rows are dropped, and the member is offered "Recreate" or "Disconnect".
- **Cadence** (decision 233): every 15 minutes per connection (the dispatcher), plus a push enqueued after a commit that changes an item's due date, title, project (and so its key), assignee, state category, deletion or project archive, or a milestone's name, due date or deletion (`enqueueCalendarSync` in `work-item-shared.ts` and the milestone usecase, after the commit, a deduplicating `jobId` per connection and minute), so Islestone's changes reach the calendar within about a minute and the calendar's reach Islestone within 15.

## Disconnecting and teardown (3.3b; decision 239)

- **A member disconnects** (`DELETE /calendar-sync/connections?provider=…`, their own only): the connection moves to `DISCONNECTING` and `calendar-teardown` runs: every Islestone event is deleted remotely first, then the dedicated calendar is deleted **only if it is empty**, then the token is destroyed locally (never revoked at Google, decision 236's consequence), then the rows go; audited `CALENDAR_DISCONNECTED`. A failed remote delete keeps its row and the teardown retries; the settings page shows "Removing your events…".
- **A member is removed**: `removeTenantMember` moves their connections to `DISCONNECTING` in its transaction and enqueues the teardown after the commit; the teardown runs as the workspace system user, uses the stored token only to delete Islestone's own events, then destroys it (decision 239). It writes nothing in Islestone and touches nothing but events Islestone created.
- **An admin turns sync off or withdraws Microsoft's consent**: every affected connection is torn down the same way.
- **A revoked token**: nothing can be deleted remotely; the rows stay (`PENDING_DELETE`), the member sees "Access was withdrawn. Reconnect to resume, or remove the Islestone calendar yourself", and reconnecting runs the reconcile, which removes what should go.
- **A workspace or user deletion** (8.3, not built) must run the teardown before its cascade; recorded in `docs/calendar-sync.md` as a rule for 8.3.

## H3: the calendar tests

- **Revoked tokens**: refresh answering `400 invalid_grant`, a call answering 401: the connection becomes `REVOKED` once, the token column is NULL, one audit entry, one metric, no further provider call in that run or later ones, no queue retry; a 429 is not revocation; a token refreshed while the row was revoked does not revive it (`updateMany … state: CONNECTED`). No test or code path calls Google's revoke endpoint.
- **Duplicates**: a fake provider that crashes the job between the remote insert and the row write; the next run adopts and the calendar holds one event; a property test over random crash points and retries asserts at most one remote event per (connection, kind, id); a re-connect replaces the connection without duplicating events.
- **Deleted events**: deleted in the calendar → item or milestone untouched, not recreated, recreated only after a due-date change or "Resync"; item deleted, closed, reassigned, hidden or its project archived, or milestone deleted or out of reach → the remote event deleted; a failed remote delete keeps its row; disconnect deletes every event before the token is destroyed (call order asserted); member removal tears down; the whole calendar deleted → `CALENDAR_REMOVED`.
- **Two-way**: a calendar change applied as the member when the due date still matches; refused, reverted and notified once when Islestone moved first, when the member lacks `work_items.edit` on the item, when the field is hidden or start would follow due; a moved milestone event reverted and notified once; a Viewer's moved event reverted with no notification and no read; Islestone's own write not read back as a change (versions); date-only edges (all-day exclusive ends, a timed event late in the evening in UTC−10 and early in UTC+14, a daylight-saving day).
- **Content**: an event's description is exactly one `appLink` URL; no other field of the item or milestone reaches the provider (the fake records every request body and the test reads it).
- **Isolation**: two-tenant tests on all three tables; a connection of workspace A never pushes B's items or milestones; the callback refuses a state minted for another user or workspace.
- **No network in CI**: the provider clients take a `fetchImpl` test seam, and a guard refuses a `fetchImpl` passed from `src/`; the fakes are in-process.

## Rules no decision names (3.3a, as built)

Where building 3.3a needed a rule no decision states, the choice and its reason; each is also in `docs/work-items.md` ("The calendar view (3.3a)"). None contradicts an ADR.

1. **The preview's data.** Decision 241 opens 3.1c's `ChangeDatesDialog`, whose preview and "Also move the N items it pushes later" need the blocks relations around the item, which the calendar read does not carry. The move reads the item's project's timeline (`GET /work-items/timeline?projectId=<the item's project>`, through `useWorkItemTimeline`, in the one cache) and the dialog runs over it: the project is the scope the item's chain lives in, a relation to another project is one of the timeline's context rows, and the project page's timeline shows the same preview. Not the calendar's own filters: a workspace-wide calendar's filters often select more than the timeline's 5,000 items, which would leave the Work items page's calendar unable to move anything. A project above 5,000 items cannot be previewed: the dialog says so and offers the item; nothing is changed without a preview.
2. **Summaries.** Which chips may move is the timeline's `editableRows` over the calendar's items (`movableItems`). The calendar read runs no schedule, so a parent whose dates follow its sub-items is not known to be a summary there: `editableRows` is asked again on the project's timeline, and such a parent is refused with "The dates of ISL-1 follow its sub-items: change theirs.", offering the item, as the timeline offers no date change on a summary row.
3. **The moved dates.** The map's "the due date moved (and the start date, if any, by the same number of days)" is read as calendar days (`moveDueTo`): the due date is the day dropped on or picked, not snapped to a working day, and the start keeps its calendar-day distance. The timeline's bar drag keeps working days (`proposeDates`) because a bar is drawn over working days; a chip names one day. The dialog lets the person adjust both dates, and the preview shows the effect on working days.
4. **"Move to date…" by keyboard** is a button beside each chip the person may move (shown on hover and focus) and a row action in the day panel, not a menu: the chip itself is the other action (it opens the sheet), so a menu would hold one item. It opens a small dialog with the product's `DatePicker` ("New due date"), then "Change dates".
5. **Which cycles and markers.** Cycles: the `cycleId` filter's cycles, else those of the teams in view (the `teamId` filter's, the items' and the projects'). Markers: the `milestoneId` filter's milestones, else those of the projects in view (the `projectId` filter's and the items'), and those projects' due dates. One rule over the filters, so a cycle's page also shows its items' projects' milestones and a milestone's page its project's due date (the map's table names the primary content). On the Work items page an empty window shows no markers: the markers describe what is in view. Bounds: 200 cycles, 500 milestones, each named in `truncated` when reached.
6. **The scope's working calendar.** The team's when the scope is one team (`teamId`), one project (`projectId`), one cycle or one milestone; else the workspace's. A cycle's page gets its team's calendar; a multi-team view gets the workspace's.
7. **Omitted sources.** Without `cycles.view` the cycles are named omitted; without `projects.view` the milestones are, and the projects' due dates are withheld too (they are project settings). The items stay: they are `work_items.view`'s.
8. **The undated count** is the scope's items with no due date under the same filters, every category.
9. **The day panel's columns** are key, title, state, project and start, from the calendar read, not the list's whole column set: the read carries no field values (the map: "field values are not read"), labels or assignee names. The panel is the shared `DataTable`, each row opening the item's sheet.
10. **Order within a day**: started, not started (unstarted, then backlog), then closed; by key within each. Three chips per cell, then "+N more". At most two cycle bands per cell are drawn (overlapping cycles of different teams); every one is named in the cell's label. A band is named on the cycle's first day and on each week row's first day.
11. **The cache.** A held row whose due date moves out of the window stays in the cached calendar, not drawn, so Undo can put it back; a row the calendar does not hold that may now be due in its window re-reads it; a row that no longer matches the filters (`rowBelongs` "no") leaves. A change of dates is placed in every mounted calendar and timeline at once (`placeDates`).
12. **The month.** The view opens on the browser's current month (UTC); "Today" goes to the month of the server's `asOf`, which also rings today. The month is not stored in a saved view (the map's `calendar` settings are the two switches).
13. **The window** is inclusive at both ends; 62 days means `to − from + 1 ≤ 62`; a missing or impossible date is a 400 (`CalendarWindowSchema`), a reversed or longer window 400 `CALENDAR_RANGE`.
14. **Performance.** The item read and the undated count joined `PLAN_CHECKED_QUERIES` (a workspace month and a project month) and the route's statements are counted; no latency-harness scenario was added (H2's per-surface table names none for the calendar).

## Rules no decision names (3.3b, as built)

Where building 3.3b needed a rule no decision states, the choice and its reason; each is also in `docs/calendar-sync.md`. None contradicts an ADR.

1. **Migrations.** `20261218130000_calendar_sync_enums` and `20261218130100_calendar_sync`, timestamped after main's latest. The third migration the model section lists (`…_notification_type_calendar_change`, `CALENDAR_CHANGE_NOT_APPLIED`) moves to 3.3c with its only writer, the read-back: a `NotificationType` value nothing writes would still owe its preference defaults and copy now.
2. **An insert is recorded before it is sent.** The row is written with no remote id (`FAILED`, `lastError` `UNCONFIRMED`), the provider is called, the row is confirmed at once. A row with no remote id is looked up by its client id before it is inserted (or deleted) again: Google by `privateExtendedProperty=islestone=<id>` with `showDeleted`, Graph by a single-value extended property under a fixed property set (`GRAPH_PROPERTY_ID`) beside `transactionId`. This is how "a retry after an unrecorded insert first lists the calendar" knows it is a retry.
3. **Google keeps a deleted event's id.** A reopened item's insert answers 409; the event is found (`showDeleted`) and restored by a PATCH with `status: confirmed`, never inserted under a second id.
4. **"Nearest first"** is the earliest due date first in the window (30 days back to 365 ahead), then id; the cap drops the furthest-future items. The section shows "1,000 of 1,240" from a count read when it is opened, for a CONNECTED connection only.
5. **Milestones' projects** are found by the member's open assigned items (any due date), at most 1,000 projects; a member without `projects.view` gets no milestone events.
6. **The push after a change** (`enqueueCalendarSync`, `enqueueCalendarSyncForItems`): by the assignees when the caller knows them (a create with an assignee, a title or due-date edit, an assignment before and after, a bulk assignment), by one statement joining the changed items to their assignees' connections (every state change through `afterStateCommit`, delete, restore, the bulk due date, a reschedule, a move), and the workspace's connections (at most 500) for a milestone's change or delete and a project's archive, delete or restore. An unassigned create queues nothing and reads nothing (the statements budgets hold). It never throws: the dispatcher catches up within 15 minutes.
7. **A deactivated member** is torn down by their next run (the re-resolution refuses them), as a removed one is by `removeTenantMember`.
8. **A teardown that cannot reach the provider** (a refused token, or a REVOKED connection disconnected) finishes locally: the token destroyed, the rows and the connection deleted; the events stay, and the member's copy says to remove the Islestone calendar. A refused token keeps the connection's rows `PENDING_DELETE` until a reconnect (whose reconcile removes what should go) or a disconnect.
9. **Disconnect** is audited when the member asks (`CALENDAR_DISCONNECTED`, the member as actor); the teardown is counted by the metric. A connection being torn down refuses a reconnect (409 `CALENDAR_DISCONNECTING`); a reconnect reuses the connection's Islestone calendar when it has one.
10. **Microsoft's admin consent** uses the v2 `/adminconsent` endpoint with the calendar scope named, at `organizations` (inflect's v1 endpoint grants every permission the registration declares, which on the shared sign-in app is more than calendar access). Withdrawing stamps `microsoftConsentRevokedAt` and keeps the directory; authorising again overwrites both.
11. **The admin-consent callback** re-asks `integrations.manage` for the state's workspace and requires the session's user to be the state's (inflect's asked neither).
12. **Redirect URIs** are built on `NEXT_PUBLIC_APP_URL` (`appLink`), never the request's host; without a public origin the connect and the consent refuse 409 `NO_PUBLIC_ORIGIN`.
13. **The state cookies** (`isl_cal_connect`, `isl_cal_admin`) are scoped to `/api/integrations/calendar`; the nonce is claimed in Redis with `SET NX` for the state's lifetime, and an unreachable Redis (two seconds) refuses. The cookie is signed, not encrypted: the verifier sits in the browser that started the flow.
14. **Callback outcomes** return to the page (`?calendar=` from a closed list, shown as a status line); a provider's `error` is kept only when it is code-shaped; nothing of a provider's text reaches a page or a log.
15. **Settings' read** (`GET /calendar-sync/settings`) takes `integrations.manage` too; the member's section reads `GET /calendar-sync/connections`, which also says which providers are offered and why not (`SYNC_OFF`, `NOT_CONFIGURED`, `MICROSOFT_NOT_AUTHORISED`).
16. **Disconnect and "Withdraw"** go through the undo toast (they remove events from people's calendars); `src/components/calendar-sync/` joins the undo-toast guard's roots.
17. **Throttles.** Google's 403 with a rate reason (`rateLimitExceeded`, `userRateLimitExceeded`, `quotaExceeded`) is a throttle, never a revocation.
18. **Graph's all-day events** take midnight to midnight in UTC; a provider's 404 or 410 on a delete counts as removed, on a patch the event is inserted again.
19. **The callbacks' rate limit** is a middleware block at the read tier's budget keyed `calendarcallback` by IP and user, since the read tier itself covers only `/api/t/`.
20. **Plan checks.** The member's items, their count and their projects (`listAssignedForCalendarSync`, `countAssignedForCalendarSync`, `projectsWithOpenAssigned`) joined `PLAN_CHECKED_QUERIES` on `WorkItem_tenantId_assigneeId_stateCategory_dueDate_idx`; the after-change join (`connectedOfAssignees`) is exempt (the named items by primary key).
21. **`syncCursor`** is created now with the model and stays empty until 3.3c.
22. **`lastSyncedAt`** is stamped only by a run that wrote to the calendar: the Prisma audit extension records every row write, so a quiet run every quarter hour stamping it would add an audit row each time while changing nothing. The synced-event rows' own writes are audited by the same extension (no title or text is in them); the map's "pushes are not audited per event" holds for `logEvent` entries.

## Open questions for the owner (all decided, 2026-11-21)## Open questions for the owner (all decided, 2026-11-21)

Each question stands alone. The recommended option is first and marked; each option carries its main trade-off. Each carries the owner's decision.

**What syncs**

1. **How far does "two-way sync of due dates" go?**
   - A (recommended). Islestone pushes items' due dates to the person's calendar, and reads back only one thing: a person moving one of those events to another day, which changes the item's due date as that person's own edit, with every check an edit in Islestone has. Matches the roadmap with the smallest surface; everything else changed in the calendar is overwritten.
   - B. Push only, as inflect-compliance designed it ("nothing is ever read back"). Simplest and safest; moving an event in the calendar silently changes nothing and is undone at the next sync, which people may find confusing.
   - C. Full two-way: titles and dates both come back. Lets people rename items from their calendar; a title typed in a phone calendar would bypass Islestone's sanitising and mention rules unless every path is rebuilt for it.
   - **Decided:** A, push, and read back only a moved day (decision 225, 2026-11-21).

2. **Which items go into a person's calendar?**
   - A (recommended). Their open items assigned to them, with a due date (from 30 days overdue to a year ahead, at most 1,000). Small, clearly "mine", and the person is accountable for each.
   - B. Those, plus items waiting on them. Covers the "whose move is it" items; adds items the person is not accountable for, whose due date they usually should not move.
   - C. Those, plus items they watch. Broadest; watching is cheap, so calendars fill with other people's work.
   - D. Whatever a saved view of their choice shows. Most flexible; a shared view's filters could push items the person did not mean to export, and the set changes when the view is edited.
   - **Decided:** A, the person's open items assigned to them (decision 226, 2026-11-21).

3. **Do cycles and milestones go into personal calendars too?**
   - A (recommended). No: only items' due dates, as the roadmap's sync names. Cycles and milestones show on Islestone's calendar view only. Keeps personal calendars to the person's own work.
   - B. Milestones of the projects the person has items in, as read-only events. Useful dates; they cannot change from the calendar, so they would be the only events that ignore a move.
   - C. Milestones and the person's team's cycles. Most context; a team's two-week cycles fill every member's calendar.
   - **Decided:** **B, milestones read-only, a departure from the recommendation** (decision 227, 2026-11-21): the milestones of projects the person has open assigned items in go into their calendar as read-only all-day events on the milestone's due date, carrying the milestone's name and the link of decision 229. A moved milestone event is undone at the next sync, with the notification of decision 230. Cycles stay out. Built in 3.3b.

4. **Where in the person's calendar do the events go?**
   - A (recommended). A separate calendar called "Islestone" that the connection creates. Islestone reads and writes only that calendar (Google's narrower "calendars this app created" access), never the person's own appointments, and the person can hide it in one click.
   - B. The person's main calendar, as inflect-compliance planned. Events sit beside meetings; Islestone needs access to the person's whole calendar and must pick its own events out of everything else when reading back.
   - **Decided:** A, a separate "Islestone" calendar (decision 228, 2026-11-21).

5. **What does each event carry?**
   - A (recommended). The item's title only, as an all-day event on the due date, marked private and free, with no description, link, key, project name, people or reminders of its own. The least that leaves, as the roadmap says.
   - B. The title plus a link back to the item. One tap to open it; the link carries the workspace's address and the item's key into a third party's calendar, and anyone the calendar is shared with sees them.
   - C. The key and the title ("ISL-12 Fix login"). Easier to recognise; the project's key prefix leaves too, beyond "titles only".
   - **Decided:** **"Title plus a link back", a departure from the recommendation** (decision 229, 2026-11-21): each event carries the title and an absolute link to the item built with `appLink`, which carries the workspace's address and the item's key. Events stay private and free, with no description beyond the link and no people or reminders. The sub-processor rows, the privacy section and the copy ("Your open items' titles, due dates and links") follow.

**How conflicts and edge cases resolve**

6. **When the calendar and Islestone disagree, which wins?**
   - A (recommended). Islestone wins unless the item's due date is still the one Islestone last pushed; a calendar change that loses, or that the person may not make, is undone at the next sync, and the person gets one in-app notification saying why. Nobody's newer Islestone edit is overwritten from a calendar, and nobody is left wondering why their change vanished.
   - B. The same rule, without the notification. Simpler; changes undone in silence.
   - C. The most recent change wins, by timestamp. Intuitive; the calendar's clock and Islestone's can disagree, and a stale phone sync could overwrite a teammate's newer date.
   - **Decided:** A, Islestone wins unless its due date is still the one last pushed; a losing or disallowed change is undone with one in-app notification (decision 230, 2026-11-21). A Viewer's read-only feed (decision 235) is not read back, so it is reverted without one ("The read-back").

7. **If a person turns an event into a timed meeting or stretches it over several days, what due date does that mean?**
   - A (recommended). The event's last day, in the event's own time zone. A due date is a finish, so the last day is the natural reading.
   - B. Only single all-day events count; any other shape is ignored and undone at the next sync. Unambiguous; a person who drags the event into a time slot sees it snap back.
   - C. The event's first day. Matches where people often drop events; contradicts "due" for multi-day events.
   - **Decided:** A, the event's last day in the event's own time zone (decision 231, 2026-11-21).

8. **If a person deletes one of these events in their calendar, what happens?**
   - A (recommended). Nothing happens to the item; Islestone stops putting that item in their calendar until its due date changes or they choose "Resync my calendar". Deleting reads as "not in my calendar", never as "clear the due date".
   - B. Islestone puts the event back at the next sync. The calendar always matches; a person cannot remove one event they do not want to see.
   - C. The item's due date is cleared. True two-way; a slip of a finger in a calendar app removes a date the team plans around.
   - **Decided:** A, the item is untouched and stops syncing until its due date changes or "Resync" (decision 232, 2026-11-21); the same for a milestone event.

9. **How quickly do changes travel?**
   - A (recommended). Islestone's changes are sent within about a minute (a sync queued after each change), and calendar changes are read every 15 minutes. No new public endpoint; a calendar change takes up to 15 minutes to show.
   - B. Provider notifications (Google push channels, Microsoft subscriptions renewed at least weekly) with the 15-minute read as a fallback. Calendar changes in seconds; two new public endpoints to verify, renew and defend.
   - C. Once a night. Least load; a moved event is wrong in one place or the other for a day.
   - **Decided:** A, push within about a minute, read every 15 minutes (decision 233, 2026-11-21).

**Who and how**

10. **Who decides whether a workspace's members may sync their calendars?**
    - A (recommended). It is off until an Owner or Admin turns it on in Settings → Calendar sync, and the operator's deployment switch must also be on. Sending workspace content to outside calendars is a workspace decision, and turning it off removes every synced event.
    - B. Only the operator's switch; once it is on, any member may connect. Fewer steps; a workspace cannot keep its item titles out of personal calendars.
    - **Decided:** A, an Owner or Admin toggle plus the operator's switch (decision 234, 2026-11-21).

11. **Which members may connect a calendar?**
    - A (recommended). Anyone who can see work items (Viewers included); calendar changes are applied back only for those who may edit items. A Viewer gets a read-only feed of their own assigned items.
    - B. Only members who may edit work items. Every connection is fully two-way; Viewers with assigned items get nothing.
    - **Decided:** A, anyone who sees work items, Viewers as a read-only feed (decision 235, 2026-11-21).

12. **Do calendar connections use the same Google and Microsoft apps as sign-in?**
    - A (recommended). No: separate calendar apps with their own client IDs. Google's review of the calendar permission never touches the sign-in app, and removing calendar access at Google never affects sign-in.
    - B. Yes, the sign-in apps, as inflect-compliance does. Two fewer registrations and secrets; the sign-in app carries the calendar permission and its review, and one Google "remove access" removes both.
    - **Decided:** **"Reuse the sign-in apps", a departure from the recommendation** (decision 236, 2026-11-21): the same Google and Microsoft OAuth client ids and secrets as sign-in (`GOOGLE_*`, `MICROSOFT_*`); no calendar client variables. The calendar flow stays hand-written and outside the NextAuth seam (ADR 0004 A3 untouched), with its own redirect URI added to the same app registrations, asking only the calendar scope (incremental authorization), never `openid`, `email` or `profile`. Consequences recorded under "Connecting a calendar", step 8: Google's verification of the calendar scope applies to the sign-in app; removing the app at Google revokes both sign-in linkage and calendar; the deployment doc gains the redirect URIs; Islestone never calls Google's revoke endpoint.

13. **How is Microsoft 365 authorised?**
    - A (recommended). Port inflect-compliance's flow: an admin of the customer's Microsoft directory authorises Islestone once, then members connect without a consent screen. Works under Microsoft's recommended consent policy, where members usually cannot approve calendar access themselves.
    - B. Google first; Microsoft in a later pull request. Smaller 3.3; Outlook users wait.
    - C. Each member consents for themselves. No admin step; in most company directories members are blocked with "Approval required".
    - **Decided:** A, port inflect's admin consent (decision 237, 2026-11-21), for the sign-in app registration (decision 236).

14. **How are the calendar tokens encrypted?**
    - A (recommended). In a column listed in the encryption manifest, under the workspace's own key, so both key-rotation jobs re-encrypt them like every other secret. One rule for every secret.
    - B. Encrypted explicitly under the deployment's global key, as inflect-compliance does. Matches the source; no rotation job reaches that column, so a key rotation would leave the tokens on the old key.
    - **Decided:** A, a manifest column under the workspace key (decision 238, 2026-11-21).

15. **When a member leaves the workspace, what happens to the events already in their calendar?**
    - A (recommended). Islestone deletes its own events from their calendar with the stored token, then destroys the token. Workspace titles do not stay in a former member's personal calendar.
    - B. Islestone destroys the token and leaves the events. Islestone never uses a departed member's access; their calendar keeps stale item titles until they delete them.
    - **Decided:** A, the events are deleted, then the token (decision 239, 2026-11-21).

16. **Are Google and Microsoft listed as sub-processors?**
    - A (recommended). Yes, both, in `docs/sub-processors.md`, with what leaves (titles and due dates), at whose instruction (the member's, after the admin's switch) and where it is stored (the member's own account). The cautious reading, and what inflect-compliance does.
    - B. Recorded as a transfer the member directs to their own account, not as sub-processors. Arguably more accurate, since the data lands in the member's own account under their own terms; customers' checklists may still expect them listed.
    - **Decided:** A, both listed as sub-processors (decision 240, 2026-11-21); the rows name the links (decision 229) and the milestone events (decision 227) too.

**The calendar view and delivery**

17. **Can items be moved on Islestone's calendar view?**
    - A (recommended). Yes: dragging an item to another day (or "Move to date…" by keyboard) opens the timeline's "Change dates" confirmation, with its preview and "Also move the N items it pushes later". One way to change dates everywhere.
    - B. Yes, at once, with an undo toast, as the board moves items. Faster; a second rule for date changes beside the timeline's confirmation.
    - C. No: the calendar view is read-only. Smallest; people switch to the timeline or the item to change a date.
    - **Decided:** A, through 3.1c's "Change dates" confirmation (decision 241, 2026-11-21).

18. **What draws the calendar view?**
    - A (recommended). inflect-compliance's month grid, copied and adapted. No new dependency, and its keyboard and screen-reader behaviour is already built and tested.
    - B. FullCalendar's MIT core (`@fullcalendar/core` 7.1.5, MIT). Week and day views and drag included; a new dependency, its premium plugins are paid and must never be imported, and its accessibility must be checked from scratch.
    - **Decided:** A, inflect's month grid, adapted (decision 242, 2026-11-21).

19. **What are the new things called?**
    - A (recommended). "Calendar connection" (`CalendarConnection`), "Synced event" (`CalendarSyncedEvent`) and "Calendar sync settings" (`TenantCalendarSettings`), with the settings section "Calendar sync"; plus a new ADR 0011 "External calendar sync" recording the rules. "Calendar" alone stays the view's word, as ADR 0006 reserves it.
    - B. "Calendar link" and "Calendar event", and no new ADR (the rules go in ADR 0006 and the subsystem page). Shorter; "Link" already names a work item's links, and "Event" is reserved for automation.
    - **Decided:** A, the three names, the settings section "Calendar sync" and a new ADR 0011 (decision 243, 2026-11-21). Applied: `docs/adr/0011-external-calendar-sync.md` (Accepted) and the ADR 0006 rows.

20. **How is 3.3 delivered?**
    - A (recommended). Three pull requests: 3.3a the calendar view, 3.3b connections and the push, 3.3c reading changes back. The view ships without any outside flow; the push can run for a while before anything is read back.
    - B. Two: the view, then the whole sync. Fewer reviews; the second is large and mixes the new data flow with its trickiest rules.
    - C. One. Simplest to track; about 11,000 lines in one review.
    - **Decided:** A, three pull requests, 3.3a, 3.3b and 3.3c (decision 244, 2026-11-21); milestone events (decision 227) land in 3.3b.
