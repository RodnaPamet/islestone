## Summary

- **The calendar view** is the `CALENDAR` layout (already in `ViewLayout`, refused until now) on the Work items, project, cycle and milestone pages and in saved views: a month grid of items on their due dates, cycles as bands, milestones and project due dates as markers, non-working days from 3.1's working calendars. It is **ported from inflect's `CalendarMonth`** (a dependency-free HTML grid with full keyboard navigation), not a library; FullCalendar (MIT core) is not needed and not added (question 18).
- **The connection model is ported from inflect** (`UserCalendarConnection`, `UserCalendarEventMapping`, `TenantCalendarConsent`, the token module, the pure reconciler, the sweep, the consent-health rules), renamed to Islestone's words. What inflect **did not build** is the part that talks to the providers: its push job enumerates connections and pushes nothing ("the actual push needs a provider writer … the job is registered, scheduled and enumerating, and the writer is the one remaining seam"), its sweep takes a `CalendarWriter` interface nothing implements, and it **never reads back** from a calendar by design. The Google and Microsoft clients and the whole inbound direction are new code.
- **Two-way, narrowly** (question 1): Islestone is the source of truth. Islestone pushes the due dates of the person's items as all-day events titled with the item's title and nothing else; the only thing read back is **a changed date on an event Islestone created**, applied as the person's own versioned edit through every check the API applies, and only while the item's due date is still the one Islestone last pushed (question 6). Everything else changed in the calendar is overwritten on the next push.
- **A dedicated "Islestone" calendar** per connection (question 4), so Islestone reads and writes only its own events and never the person's other appointments; with Google's `calendar.app.created` scope ("Make secondary Google calendars, and see, create, change, and delete events on them"), Microsoft's `Calendars.ReadWrite`.
- **Personal and opt-in twice**: an admin (`integrations.manage`) turns calendar sync on for the workspace, and for Microsoft records the customer directory's admin consent; then each member connects their own calendar (`work_items.view`), and only their own; the routes take no user id. A GUEST sees and connects nothing (decision 204).
- **Tokens** are one encrypted blob per connection in a manifest column (the workspace's DEK, reached by both rotation sweeps; question 14), refreshed with the same scopes, destroyed on revocation while the row stays to drive cleanup. A connection's OAuth flow is separate from sign-in: no `openid` or `email` scope, no `Account` row, no invite redemption, so `oauth-email-trust` is never consulted.
- **Jobs**: a dispatcher every 15 minutes, a per-connection sync (push, then read back), a teardown; plus a push enqueued after any commit that changes what a person should see (question 9). Polling, not provider webhooks, so no new public endpoint.
- **Egress**: fixed provider hosts over plain `fetch` (the `resend-api.ts` precedent); a server-supplied paging link only through `safeFetch` with the host pinned (the `resend-download.ts` precedent). No CSP change. Google and Microsoft enter the sub-processor register (question 16).
- **Three pull requests** (question 20): 3.3a the calendar view, 3.3b connections and the push, 3.3c the read-back and H3's tests. About 6,000 lines of source and 5,000 of tests.

## Vocabulary (ADR 0006)

- **"Calendar" alone** is reserved by the Working calendar row for "the `CALENDAR` layout and 3.3's sync". The layout's label is "Calendar"; the sync's models qualify the word.
- **"Event"** is banned for agent activity, activity, audit entries and transitions, and reserved for automation; it stays legal for another concept (the schema guard treats it as context-dependent). A remote calendar entry is the provider's own term, used inside the adapter; Islestone's record of it needs its own word (question 19).
- **"Subscription" (as a model)** is banned (Notification preference row), so if question 9 ever chooses webhooks, the stored Graph subscription is a "watch". **"Copy"** is banned schema-wide (Projection row), so the record is not `CalendarEventCopy`. **"Session"** unqualified is banned. **"Sync engine"** is ADR 0005's deferred option; "sync" alone is free.

The layout switcher (List, Board, Timeline) gains Calendar where Timeline is offered; `SAVED_VIEW_LAYOUTS` gains `CALENDAR`; `SavedViewConfigSchema` gains `calendar: { showCycles, showMilestones }` (`.strict()`); `saved-view-config-keys.test.ts` gains them. Every list filter applies through `resolveListFiltersInTx`, so a calendar and a list with the same filters hold the same items.

### The read

`GET /api/t/:slug/work-items/calendar?from=YYYY-MM-DD&to=YYYY-MM-DD&<the list's filters>` (`requirePermission('work_items.view')`), usecase `getWorkItemCalendar` in `src/app-layer/usecases/work-item-calendar.ts`:

1. The window is at most 62 days (a month grid shows 42; the extra covers a week view later); 400 `CALENDAR_RANGE` otherwise. Dates are date-only, never passed through a zone.
2. Items with a due date in the window under the list's filters, closed categories included (shown done), at most **2,000**, read one past the limit; above it 422 `CALENDAR_TOO_LARGE` with the count ("Narrow the filter: 2,311 items this month"), never a cut-off grid. Index `[tenantId, projectId, dueDate, id]` and `[tenantId, stateCategory, dueDate]` (both exist). Items with no due date are not shown; the summary line counts them ("37 items have no due date").
3. Cycles overlapping the window of the teams in scope (`cycles.view`; a caller without it gets `omitted: ['cycles']`, named, as inflect's `omittedSources`), milestones and project due dates in the window (`projects.view`), each bounded.
4. The working calendar of the scope (one team's, else the workspace's) for shading, and today in its zone (3.1's rule).

Every raw read carries `tenantId`, `deletedAt` and `projectVisibilitySql`; H2's plan registry gains the item read. Description, comments and field values are not read. Not audited.

### The client

- **Data:** `useWorkItemCalendar(scope, filters, month)` in `src/components/work-items/data/` (the shared-query guard's folder), registered with `work-item-cache.ts` so a write anywhere places the changed row (a due date moved off the month removes it).
- **The grid:** the ported `CalendarMonth` (a 7×6 grid, week start from the mirrored `week-start.ts`), chips "ISL-12 Title" with the state category tone, "+N more" opening the day panel (the list's columns for that day's items), cycle bands across the top of each week row, diamonds for milestones and project due dates, non-working days and holidays shaded with a legend; keyboard as inflect's (arrows, Home/End, Enter opens the day); a chip opens the item's sheet.
- **Moving an item** (question 17, A): dragging a chip to another day (pragmatic-drag-and-drop, already in the tree) or "Move to date…" from the chip's menu (the keyboard path, with the mirrored `DatePicker`) opens 3.1c's `ChangeDatesDialog` with the due date moved (and the start date, if any, by the same number of days), the engine's preview and "Also move the N items it pushes later"; confirming sends the same `PATCH` or `POST /work-items/bulk/dates`. Closed items, archived projects and a caller without `work_items.edit` or whose field set hides the due date get no drag (`editableRows`, 3.1c's rule, reused).
- **Accessibility:** the grid is a real `role=grid` (inflect's audit), and the day panel is a table; no second "table" mode is needed. jest-axe in the rendered tests, and the scripted screen-reader pass of decision 177 at the gate.
- **Copy** is `workItems.calendar.*`; `h2` headings under the page's `h1`.

## Calendar sync: the model (3.3b)

```prisma
enum CalendarProvider        { GOOGLE MICROSOFT }
enum CalendarConnectionState { CONNECTED REVOKED DISCONNECTING CALENDAR_REMOVED }
enum CalendarSyncedEventState { PUSHED PENDING_DELETE FAILED REMOVED_IN_CALENDAR }

model TenantCalendarSettings {                  // one row per workspace; no row = sync off
  tenantId                      String   @id
  syncEnabled                   Boolean  @default(false)
  microsoftExternalTenantId     String?             // the customer's directory the consent was granted in
  microsoftConsentGrantedAt     DateTime?
  microsoftConsentGrantedByUserId String?           // actor stamp, no FK
  microsoftConsentRevokedAt     DateTime?
  version                       Int      @default(1)
  updatedAt                     DateTime @updatedAt
}

model CalendarConnection {
  id                 String                  @id @default(cuid())
  tenantId           String
  userId             String                  // the member who connected; FK to User (cascade with the user only, 8.3 tears down first)
  provider           CalendarProvider
  state              CalendarConnectionState @default(CONNECTED)
  tokenEncrypted     String?                 // ENCRYPTED (manifest): { accessToken, refreshToken, expiresAt } as one blob; NULL once revoked or disconnected
  scopesGranted      String[]                // what the provider granted, which may be narrower than asked
  externalCalendarId String?                 // the dedicated calendar's id at the provider
  syncCursor         String?                 // Google's syncToken; opaque, useless without the token
  revokedReason      String?                 // a fixed phrase, never a provider body (CHECK: one of a closed list)
  lastSyncedAt       DateTime?
  connectedAt        DateTime                @default(now())
  createdAt          DateTime                @default(now())
  updatedAt          DateTime                @updatedAt
  @@unique([tenantId, userId, provider])
  @@unique([id, tenantId])
  @@index([tenantId, state])
  @@index([userId])
}

model CalendarSyncedEvent {
  id             String                   @id @default(cuid())
  tenantId       String
  connectionId   String                   // FK (connectionId, tenantId) -> CalendarConnection, RESTRICT: rows go only after their remote events
  workItemId     String                   // no FK: the row must outlive an item's purge to delete its remote event
  remoteEventId  String                   // empty until the first push succeeds
  clientEventId  String                   // deterministic (length-prefixed sha256, 40 hex): Google's event id, Microsoft's transactionId
  pushedDueDate  DateTime                 @db.Date   // what Islestone last pushed: the read-back's check (question 6)
  contentHash    String                   // over exactly what is pushed (title, date)
  remoteVersion  String?                  // Google etag / Graph changeKey after our last write: our own write is not read back as a change
  state          CalendarSyncedEventState @default(PUSHED)
  lastError      String?                  // a code from a closed list, never a provider body
  lastPushedAt   DateTime?
  createdAt      DateTime                 @default(now())
  updatedAt      DateTime                 @updatedAt
  @@unique([connectionId, workItemId])
  @@index([tenantId, connectionId, state])
  @@index([tenantId, workItemId])
}
```

- **Why not `Account`** (inflect's reasoning holds): `Account` is a sign-in provider link with no `tenantId`, so it cannot carry an RLS policy or tell "connected for this workspace" from another; and a calendar grant must never ride on a sign-in token.
- **Why the membership is not the FK**: a removed member's events must still be deleted from their calendar (question 15) with the token the row holds; a cascade from the membership would lose the only record that the events exist. Removal moves the connection to `DISCONNECTING` and enqueues the teardown; the rows go after the remote events.
- **No FK to the work item** for the same reason (`WorkItemTransition`'s precedent): the item's purge must not take the record of its remote event with it; the next sync deletes the event, then the row.
- **Tenant-table rules** for all three: `tenantId` (the settings row's key), tenant-leading indexes, the four RLS policies with FORCE, grants in the creating migration (DELETE on `CalendarSyncedEvent` and `CalendarConnection` for the teardown; none on the settings row), two-tenant tests in `ISOLATION_TESTED`; Layer B and C of `schema-index-coverage`.
- **Encryption** (question 14, A): `CalendarConnection.tokenEncrypted` joins `ENCRYPTED_FIELDS`, encrypted under the workspace's DEK by the extension, so the tenant DEK sweep and the master-key sweep both rewrite it. inflect called `encryptField` explicitly (under the global key), which in Islestone no sweep would reach. The other text columns are opaque identifiers or fixed codes, classified plaintext in `encryption-manifest-coverage.test.ts` with reasons. No email address of the connected account is stored (no scope returns one).
- **Migrations**: `202612xx130000_calendar_sync_enums` (the three new enums together are new types, not new values, so one migration), `202612xx130100_calendar_sync` (the tables, CHECKs, RLS, grants), `202612xx130200_notification_type_calendar_change` (if question 6 chooses A: the one new `NotificationType` value, alone, as 2.4b and 2.7 did), each forward-only.

## Connecting a calendar (3.3b)

1. **Settings → Calendar sync** (`integrations.manage`, Owner and Admin): "Allow members to sync their calendars" (`PUT /calendar-sync/settings { syncEnabled }`, audited `CALENDAR_SYNC_SETTINGS_SET`), and for Microsoft "Authorise Microsoft 365" (question 13): `POST /calendar-sync/microsoft-consent` returns the `/adminconsent` URL (sealed state cookie); `/api/integrations/calendar/admin-callback` receives `admin_consent=True&tenant=<id>` (no code), records the directory and who granted it, audited `CALENDAR_MICROSOFT_CONSENT_GRANTED`; "Withdraw" stamps the revocation and tears down every Microsoft connection (`…_REVOKED`). The page shows how many members are connected, never who.
2. **The member's own page** (`/t/<slug>/inbox/preferences` gains a "Calendar sync" section, or a sibling page under the account; the copy says what leaves: "Your open items' titles and due dates, in a calendar called Islestone"): `POST /calendar-sync/connect?provider=google|microsoft` (`requirePermission('work_items.view')`; refused 409 `CALENDAR_SYNC_OFF` while the workspace switch is off, `MICROSOFT_NOT_AUTHORISED` without consent) answers `{ authorizeUrl }` as JSON, which the browser navigates to: a top-level navigation, so `form-action 'self'` is not involved and the CSP is unchanged.
3. **The state** is an HttpOnly, `SameSite=Lax`, 10-minute cookie, HMAC-sealed (the platform key), carrying a random state, the workspace slug, the member's user id, the provider and the **PKCE verifier** (S256; both providers support it); single-use (its nonce recorded in Redis for 10 minutes).
4. **The callback**, `/api/integrations/calendar/callback` (outside `/t/` because each provider redirects to one fixed URI; a signed-in path, not public, not a machine caller): the state must match and its user must be the session's user, else nothing is stored; `error=` from the provider records only the code; the workspace switch and (Microsoft) the consent are re-checked; the code is exchanged with the verifier; a missing refresh token refuses (inflect's rule); the dedicated calendar is created ("Islestone", the workspace's name in it would leave data, so not added); the connection is upserted (re-connecting replaces and clears a revocation), audited `CALENDAR_CONNECTED` (provider and granted scope count, never a token); the first sync is enqueued.
5. **What the flow never does**: ask `openid`, `email` or `profile`; write `Account`; link an identity; redeem an invite; read the external account's address. `oauth-email-trust.ts` therefore has nothing to judge, and a member may connect a calendar under a different external account than they sign in with. ADR 0004's seam is untouched: nothing here imports `next-auth`; the calendar OAuth is hand-written as inflect's is.
6. **Scopes** (question 4, A): Google `https://www.googleapis.com/auth/calendar.app.created`; Microsoft `offline_access Calendars.ReadWrite` (Graph has no narrower permission that can create a calendar). Recorded as constants so a widening is a reviewed diff.

## The push (3.3b)

- **Who and what** (question 2, A): for each `CONNECTED` connection of an ACTIVE member in a workspace with sync on, the desired set is generated by one function, `generateSyncedItems(ctx)`, under the member re-resolved when the job runs (`resolveMemberContext`; `null` → the teardown, never a system context; `actorType: 'JOB'`): their open items (Backlog, Unstarted, Started) assigned to them, with a due date from 30 days ago to 365 days ahead, in live, visible (`visibleProjects`), unarchived projects; nearest first, at most **1,000**; above it the nearest 1,000 are pushed and the member's settings say "1,000 of 1,240 items are in your calendar" (inflect's honest truncation; the count is the read's, never guessed).
- **What an event carries** (question 5, A): summary = the item's title, exactly (plaintext by ADR 0002 §11, already sanitised); an all-day event on the due date (Google `start.date` = due, `end.date` = due + 1, exclusive; Graph `isAllDay: true` with midnight `start` and `end` in one zone); visibility private (Google `visibility: private`, Graph `sensitivity: private`); free (Google `transparency: transparent`, Graph `showAs: free`); no description, no location, no attendees, no link, no key, no project or workspace name, no reminder overrides (the person's calendar defaults apply). A private extended property holds the client id so Islestone can recognise its own events; it carries no content.
- **Reconcile** (the ported pure diff): insert for a desired item with no row; patch when the content hash differs or the row is `FAILED`; delete for a row whose item is no longer desired (closed, deleted, reassigned, out of the window, out of sight, project archived): deletes are driven by the rows, so an item the member can no longer see is removed although the generation never mentions it. A nightly run over an unchanged calendar makes no provider call.
- **Duplicates** (H3): the client id is deterministic per (workspace, connection, item). Google takes it as the event's `id` (40 lowercase hex is inside base32hex) and answers 409 to a repeat; Google documents that collisions are not guaranteed to be detected at creation, so a retry after an unrecorded insert first lists the dedicated calendar by `privateExtendedProperty=islestone=<clientEventId>` and adopts what it finds. Graph takes it as `transactionId` ("to avoid redundant POST operations in case of client retries"). The row is written immediately after the remote insert; `@@unique([connectionId, workItemId])` holds one row per item.
- **Failures**: a remote delete that fails keeps the row (`PENDING_DELETE`) and retries next run, because the row is the only record the event exists; a push that fails marks `FAILED` with a code; an auth failure (`400 invalid_grant` on refresh, 401/403 on a call) is terminal for the connection: `REVOKED`, token destroyed, one counter, audited `CALENDAR_CONNECTION_REVOKED` as the workspace system user, no further calls, no retry (`NonRetryableJobError`); a throttle (429, `Retry-After`) is not terminal and the next run continues. One member's failure never stops the batch (`runUserPushGuarded`'s isolation).
- **Loop prevention**: after every write the row stores the provider's version (`etag` / `changeKey`), so the read-back does not mistake Islestone's own write for a person's.

## The read-back (3.3c; questions 1, 6, 7, 8)

- **What is read**: only the dedicated calendar, only events Islestone created (matched by `remoteEventId`). Google: `events.list` with the stored `syncToken` (deleted events included); a `410 Gone` resets to a full listing of the window. Microsoft: `GET /me/calendars/{id}/calendarView` over the push window, compared with each row's `remoteVersion`, because Graph v1.0's `calendarView/delta` covers "the user's primary calendar" only. An event a person added to the Islestone calendar by hand is ignored, never imported (no item is ever created from a calendar).
- **A date changed in the calendar**: the new due date is the event's date (question 7: for a timed or multi-day event, its last day in the event's own time zone). It is applied **as the member**, re-resolved, through the same versioned write as the detail's due-date edit: `work_items.edit`, the field set (`FIELD_HIDDEN`), `START_AFTER_DUE`, `PROJECT_ARCHIVED`, `ITEM_CLOSED`, the item's `version`; audited `WORK_ITEM_UPDATED` naming `dueDate` with `via: 'calendar'` and the provider in `detailsJson`; the due bell and the watcher fan-out run as for any edit. **Only while the item's due date still equals the row's `pushedDueDate`** (question 6): otherwise Islestone's newer date wins.
- **When it is not applied** (a stale change, no edit permission, a hidden field, a closed item, start after due): the next push puts Islestone's date back, and (question 6, A) the member gets one in-app notification, `CALENDAR_CHANGE_NOT_APPLIED` ("Your calendar change to ISL-12 was not applied: the due date changed in Islestone" / "…you cannot edit this item"), in-app only, no email, under their preferences; it names the item, never the text.
- **A Viewer** may connect (push only, in effect): every inbound change is refused for want of `work_items.edit`, so the member's settings say "Changes in your calendar are not sent back" and the read-back does not notify them each time.
- **Anything else changed** (title, a description, attendees, colour) is not read; the next push restores the title when the content hash says it changed.
- **An event deleted in the calendar** (question 8, A): the item is untouched; the row moves to `REMOVED_IN_CALENDAR` and the item is not pushed again until its due date changes or the member chooses "Resync my calendar". **The whole Islestone calendar deleted**: the connection moves to `CALENDAR_REMOVED`, its rows are dropped (their events went with the calendar), and the member is offered "Recreate" or "Disconnect".
- **Cadence** (question 9, A): every 15 minutes per connection (the dispatcher), plus a push enqueued after a commit that changes an item's due date, assignee, state category, deletion or project archive (`enqueueCalendarSync` in `work-item-shared.ts`, after the commit, a deduplicating `jobId` per connection and minute), so Islestone's own changes reach the calendar within a minute and the calendar's reach Islestone within 15.

## Disconnecting and teardown (3.3b; question 15)

- **A member disconnects** (`DELETE /calendar-sync/connections?provider=…`, their own only): the connection moves to `DISCONNECTING` and `calendar-teardown` runs: every Islestone event is deleted remotely first, then the dedicated calendar is deleted **only if it is empty** (a person may have added their own entries to it), then the token is revoked at Google (`https://oauth2.googleapis.com/revoke`; Microsoft offers no per-grant revoke for a delegated refresh token) and destroyed locally, then the rows go; audited `CALENDAR_DISCONNECTED`. A failed remote delete keeps its row and the teardown retries; the settings page shows "Removing your events…".
- **A member is removed**: `removeTenantMember` moves their connections to `DISCONNECTING` in its transaction and enqueues the teardown after the commit; the teardown runs as the workspace system user, uses the stored token only to delete Islestone's own events, then destroys it (question 15). This is not acting for the person: it writes nothing in Islestone and touches nothing but events Islestone created.
- **An admin turns sync off or withdraws Microsoft's consent**: every affected connection is torn down the same way.
- **A revoked token**: nothing can be deleted remotely; the rows stay (`PENDING_DELETE`), the member sees "Access was withdrawn. Reconnect to resume, or remove the Islestone calendar yourself", and reconnecting runs the reconcile, which removes what should go.
- **A workspace or user deletion** (8.3, not built) must run the teardown before its cascade; recorded in `docs/calendar-sync.md` as a rule for 8.3.

## Egress, CSP and the sub-processor register

- **Hosts, fixed in code**: `https://oauth2.googleapis.com` (token, revoke), `https://www.googleapis.com/calendar/v3` (events, calendars), `https://login.microsoftonline.com` (token, admin consent), `https://graph.microsoft.com/v1.0`. Calls to these fixed origins use plain `fetch` with a timeout and a capped body read, as `resend-api.ts` does (no person configures them).
- **Server-supplied links**: Graph's `@odata.nextLink` is a URL the provider returns; it is followed only when its origin is exactly `https://graph.microsoft.com` and only through `safeFetch` (`resend-download.ts`'s rule), and `ssrf-egress-coverage.test.ts` gains it as a sink. Google pages by token, not URL.
- **Browser**: only top-level navigations to `accounts.google.com` and `login.microsoftonline.com`; no script, frame or fetch from the browser to a provider, so the CSP is unchanged (`connect-src` is not involved; `form-action 'self'` is not involved because the authorize URL comes back as JSON).
- **No deployment egress allowlist exists today** (the compose file has none); if one is added, these four hosts join it.
- **Sub-processors** (question 16, A): `docs/sub-processors.md` gains Google (Google Calendar API) and Microsoft (Microsoft Graph, Outlook calendar), in the same pull request as the flow (3.3b): what leaves (item titles and due dates of a member's own items, in a calendar in that member's own account), on whose instruction (the member's, after the admin's switch), where it is stored (in the member's own Google or Microsoft account, under that account's terms), what Islestone keeps (the token, encrypted; the event ids). Operator steps recorded there: Google's OAuth app verification for the requested scope (the class of `calendar.app.created` was not verified from here), Microsoft's publisher verification for a multi-tenant app.

## H3: the calendar tests

- **Revoked tokens**: refresh answering `400 invalid_grant`, a call answering 401: the connection becomes `REVOKED` once, the token column is NULL, one audit entry, one metric, no further provider call in that run or later ones, no queue retry; a 429 is not revocation and the next run proceeds; a token refreshed while the row was revoked does not revive it (`updateMany … state: CONNECTED`).
- **Duplicates**: a fake provider that crashes the job between the remote insert and the row write; the next run adopts (Google by the private property, Graph by `transactionId`) and the calendar holds one event; a property test over random crash points and retries asserts at most one remote event per (connection, item); a re-connect replaces the connection without duplicating events.
- **Deleted events**: deleted in the calendar → item untouched, not recreated, recreated only after a due-date change or "Resync"; item deleted, closed, reassigned, hidden or its project archived → the remote event deleted; a failed remote delete keeps its row and the next run retries; disconnect deletes every event before the token is destroyed (call order asserted); member removal tears down; the whole calendar deleted → `CALENDAR_REMOVED`.
- **Two-way**: a calendar change applied as the member when the due date still matches; refused and reverted when Islestone moved first, when the member lacks `work_items.edit`, when the field is hidden or start would follow due; Islestone's own write not read back as a change (versions); date-only edges (all-day exclusive ends, a timed event late in the evening in UTC−10 and early in UTC+14, a daylight-saving day).
- **Isolation**: two-tenant tests on all three tables; a connection of workspace A never pushes B's items; the callback refuses a state minted for another user or workspace.
- **No network in CI**: the provider clients take a `fetchImpl` test seam, and a guard refuses a `fetchImpl` passed from `src/` (inflect's MCP-client rule); the fakes are in-process.

## Open questions for the owner

Each question stands alone. The recommended option is first and marked; each option carries its main trade-off.

**What syncs**

1. **How far does "two-way sync of due dates" go?**
   - A (recommended). Islestone pushes items' due dates to the person's calendar, and reads back only one thing: a person moving one of those events to another day, which changes the item's due date as that person's own edit, with every check an edit in Islestone has. Matches the roadmap with the smallest surface; everything else changed in the calendar is overwritten.
   - B. Push only, as inflect-compliance designed it ("nothing is ever read back"). Simplest and safest; moving an event in the calendar silently changes nothing and is undone at the next sync, which people may find confusing.
   - C. Full two-way: titles and dates both come back. Lets people rename items from their calendar; a title typed in a phone calendar would bypass Islestone's sanitising and mention rules unless every path is rebuilt for it.

2. **Which items go into a person's calendar?**
   - A (recommended). Their open items assigned to them, with a due date (from 30 days overdue to a year ahead, at most 1,000). Small, clearly "mine", and the person is accountable for each.
   - B. Those, plus items waiting on them. Covers the "whose move is it" items; adds items the person is not accountable for, whose due date they usually should not move.
   - C. Those, plus items they watch. Broadest; watching is cheap, so calendars fill with other people's work.
   - D. Whatever a saved view of their choice shows. Most flexible; a shared view's filters could push items the person did not mean to export, and the set changes when the view is edited.

3. **Do cycles and milestones go into personal calendars too?**
   - A (recommended). No: only items' due dates, as the roadmap's sync names. Cycles and milestones show on Islestone's calendar view only. Keeps personal calendars to the person's own work.
   - B. Milestones of the projects the person has items in, as read-only events. Useful dates; they cannot change from the calendar, so they would be the only events that ignore a move.
   - C. Milestones and the person's team's cycles. Most context; a team's two-week cycles fill every member's calendar.

4. **Where in the person's calendar do the events go?**
   - A (recommended). A separate calendar called "Islestone" that the connection creates. Islestone reads and writes only that calendar (Google's narrower "calendars this app created" access), never the person's own appointments, and the person can hide it in one click.
   - B. The person's main calendar, as inflect-compliance planned. Events sit beside meetings; Islestone needs access to the person's whole calendar and must pick its own events out of everything else when reading back.

5. **What does each event carry?**
   - A (recommended). The item's title only, as an all-day event on the due date, marked private and free, with no description, link, key, project name, people or reminders of its own. The least that leaves, as the roadmap says.
   - B. The title plus a link back to the item. One tap to open it; the link carries the workspace's address and the item's key into a third party's calendar, and anyone the calendar is shared with sees them.
   - C. The key and the title ("ISL-12 Fix login"). Easier to recognise; the project's key prefix leaves too, beyond "titles only".

**How conflicts and edge cases resolve**

6. **When the calendar and Islestone disagree, which wins?**
   - A (recommended). Islestone wins unless the item's due date is still the one Islestone last pushed; a calendar change that loses, or that the person may not make, is undone at the next sync, and the person gets one in-app notification saying why. Nobody's newer Islestone edit is overwritten from a calendar, and nobody is left wondering why their change vanished.
   - B. The same rule, without the notification. Simpler; changes undone in silence.
   - C. The most recent change wins, by timestamp. Intuitive; the calendar's clock and Islestone's can disagree, and a stale phone sync could overwrite a teammate's newer date.

7. **If a person turns an event into a timed meeting or stretches it over several days, what due date does that mean?**
   - A (recommended). The event's last day, in the event's own time zone. A due date is a finish, so the last day is the natural reading.
   - B. Only single all-day events count; any other shape is ignored and undone at the next sync. Unambiguous; a person who drags the event into a time slot sees it snap back.
   - C. The event's first day. Matches where people often drop events; contradicts "due" for multi-day events.

8. **If a person deletes one of these events in their calendar, what happens?**
   - A (recommended). Nothing happens to the item; Islestone stops putting that item in their calendar until its due date changes or they choose "Resync my calendar". Deleting reads as "not in my calendar", never as "clear the due date".
   - B. Islestone puts the event back at the next sync. The calendar always matches; a person cannot remove one event they do not want to see.
   - C. The item's due date is cleared. True two-way; a slip of a finger in a calendar app removes a date the team plans around.

9. **How quickly do changes travel?**
   - A (recommended). Islestone's changes are sent within about a minute (a sync queued after each change), and calendar changes are read every 15 minutes. No new public endpoint; a calendar change takes up to 15 minutes to show.
   - B. Provider notifications (Google push channels, Microsoft subscriptions renewed at least weekly) with the 15-minute read as a fallback. Calendar changes in seconds; two new public endpoints to verify, renew and defend.
   - C. Once a night. Least load; a moved event is wrong in one place or the other for a day.

**Who and how**

10. **Who decides whether a workspace's members may sync their calendars?**
    - A (recommended). It is off until an Owner or Admin turns it on in Settings → Calendar sync, and the operator's deployment switch must also be on. Sending workspace content to outside calendars is a workspace decision, and turning it off removes every synced event.
    - B. Only the operator's switch; once it is on, any member may connect. Fewer steps; a workspace cannot keep its item titles out of personal calendars.

11. **Which members may connect a calendar?**
    - A (recommended). Anyone who can see work items (Viewers included); calendar changes are applied back only for those who may edit items. A Viewer gets a read-only feed of their own assigned items.
    - B. Only members who may edit work items. Every connection is fully two-way; Viewers with assigned items get nothing.

12. **Do calendar connections use the same Google and Microsoft apps as sign-in?**
    - A (recommended). No: separate calendar apps with their own client IDs. Google's review of the calendar permission never touches the sign-in app, and removing calendar access at Google never affects sign-in.
    - B. Yes, the sign-in apps, as inflect-compliance does. Two fewer registrations and secrets; the sign-in app carries the calendar permission and its review, and one Google "remove access" removes both.

13. **How is Microsoft 365 authorised?**
    - A (recommended). Port inflect-compliance's flow: an admin of the customer's Microsoft directory authorises Islestone once, then members connect without a consent screen. Works under Microsoft's recommended consent policy, where members usually cannot approve calendar access themselves.
    - B. Google first; Microsoft in a later pull request. Smaller 3.3; Outlook users wait.
    - C. Each member consents for themselves. No admin step; in most company directories members are blocked with "Approval required".

14. **How are the calendar tokens encrypted?**
    - A (recommended). In a column listed in the encryption manifest, under the workspace's own key, so both key-rotation jobs re-encrypt them like every other secret. One rule for every secret.
    - B. Encrypted explicitly under the deployment's global key, as inflect-compliance does. Matches the source; no rotation job reaches that column, so a key rotation would leave the tokens on the old key.

15. **When a member leaves the workspace, what happens to the events already in their calendar?**
    - A (recommended). Islestone deletes its own events from their calendar with the stored token, then destroys the token. Workspace titles do not stay in a former member's personal calendar.
    - B. Islestone destroys the token and leaves the events. Islestone never uses a departed member's access; their calendar keeps stale item titles until they delete them.

16. **Are Google and Microsoft listed as sub-processors?**
    - A (recommended). Yes, both, in `docs/sub-processors.md`, with what leaves (titles and due dates), at whose instruction (the member's, after the admin's switch) and where it is stored (the member's own account). The cautious reading, and what inflect-compliance does.
    - B. Recorded as a transfer the member directs to their own account, not as sub-processors. Arguably more accurate, since the data lands in the member's own account under their own terms; customers' checklists may still expect them listed.

**The calendar view and delivery**

17. **Can items be moved on Islestone's calendar view?**
    - A (recommended). Yes: dragging an item to another day (or "Move to date…" by keyboard) opens the timeline's "Change dates" confirmation, with its preview and "Also move the N items it pushes later". One way to change dates everywhere.
    - B. Yes, at once, with an undo toast, as the board moves items. Faster; a second rule for date changes beside the timeline's confirmation.
    - C. No: the calendar view is read-only. Smallest; people switch to the timeline or the item to change a date.

18. **What draws the calendar view?**
    - A (recommended). inflect-compliance's month grid, copied and adapted. No new dependency, and its keyboard and screen-reader behaviour is already built and tested.
    - B. FullCalendar's MIT core (`@fullcalendar/core` 7.1.5, MIT). Week and day views and drag included; a new dependency, its premium plugins are paid and must never be imported, and its accessibility must be checked from scratch.

19. **What are the new things called?**
    - A (recommended). "Calendar connection" (`CalendarConnection`), "Synced event" (`CalendarSyncedEvent`) and "Calendar sync settings" (`TenantCalendarSettings`), with the settings section "Calendar sync"; plus a new ADR 0011 "External calendar sync" recording the rules. "Calendar" alone stays the view's word, as ADR 0006 reserves it.
    - B. "Calendar link" and "Calendar event", and no new ADR (the rules go in ADR 0006 and the subsystem page). Shorter; "Link" already names a work item's links, and "Event" is reserved for automation.

20. **How is 3.3 delivered?**
    - A (recommended). Three pull requests: 3.3a the calendar view, 3.3b connections and the push, 3.3c reading changes back. The view ships without any outside flow; the push can run for a while before anything is read back.
    - B. Two: the view, then the whole sync. Fewer reviews; the second is large and mixes the new data flow with its trickiest rules.
