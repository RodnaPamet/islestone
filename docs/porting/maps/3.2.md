## Summary

- **The shape is already decided by ADR 0002 §2** (owner decision 24): one single-parent chain, goal → initiative → project → work item → sub-item. `Goal.parentId` (goals nest, at most 3 levels), `Initiative.goalId`, `Project.initiativeId`, each nullable; never many-to-many, so every item has exactly one ancestry and progress never counts an item twice. This map builds that chain; it does not reopen it (question 7 asks only whether a project may skip the initiative level).
- **Two new tenant tables, `Goal` and `Initiative`, and one new column, `Project.initiativeId`.** Workspace-scoped (no team, no project), archive-only (question 14), titles and names plaintext, descriptions encrypted and sanitised, an optional date-only due date, an optional lead, a lifecycle status and a **health set by a person** (`Health`: On track, At risk, Off track; none = "No health set"), audited with who set it and when.
- **Progress is derived, never stored**, exactly as a milestone's (2.5b): the live, visible, non-cancelled items of every project under the initiative (or under the goal's whole subtree), done the Done ones; "late" when the due date has passed with open items; zero items is "No items", never 100 %.
- **Ancestry is one statement**: a `WITH RECURSIVE` with two recursive terms, up `WorkItem.parentId` (at most 5) and up `Goal.parentId` (at most 3), joined through the project and its initiative. It is shown as a breadcrumb on the item's detail, served by `GET /work-items/:id/ancestry`, and is the "why" 6.2's read tools and 6.3's agents will read.
- **The roadmap is a layout, not a model** (ADR 0006's Roadmap row): initiatives as summary rows, their projects as bars, milestones and project due dates as diamonds, on the SVAR timeline already in the tree (3.1b), through the same single adapter file, read-only, with a table beside it. Project bars come from stored dates (question 11); the scheduling engine stays on a project's own timeline.
- **"Portfolio page" is a banned word** (ADR 0006 bans "Portfolio" for both Initiative and Roadmap), so the roadmap's portfolio page is **the initiative's page**: its projects, their progress and dates, the initiative's slice of the roadmap, its health and its history (question 16).
- **Permissions:** a new `goals` domain (`goals.view`, `goals.manage`), mirroring decision 72's `cycles` domain (question 9); a project's initiative is a project field under `projects.edit`; health is a person's act only, never an API key's or an agent's (questions 9 and 10). A GUEST sees nothing (decision 204).
- **Ports:** nothing from inflect-compliance (it has no goal model; its visx Gantt chart is not taken unless question 12 chooses it). From Paperclip, **pattern only**: the depth-capped, path-guarded recursive ancestor walk and the same-tenant parent check under `FOR KEY SHARE`; Paperclip's goal fallback, its `issues.goalId`, its `project_goals` many-to-many and its agent-owned goals are not taken.
- **Three pull requests** (question 18): 3.2a the model, the routes and the ancestry; 3.2b progress, health and the goal and initiative pages; 3.2c the roadmap. About 5,200 lines of source and 4,000 of tests.

## Vocabulary (ADR 0006)

ADR 0006 already reserves the three words. Its rows, verbatim:

- **Goal** — "An outcome the workspace wants, with a target and a health status set by a person." Model `Goal`. Words not to use: "Objective, OKR, Key result, Target".
- **Initiative** — "A set of projects that together serve a goal." Model `Initiative`. Not: "Program, Portfolio, Theme, Epic".
- **Roadmap** — "The timeline layout over projects and initiatives." "No model: a view." Not: "Portfolio, Plan".

What this means for 3.2:

- **"Target" is banned schema-wide** (unqualified, and no row names it), so `tests/guards/schema-vocabulary.test.ts` refuses ADR 0002's sketched `Goal.targetDate`. The column is `dueDate`, labelled "Due date", as `Milestone.dueDate` and `Project.dueDate` are. The Goal row's own phrase "with a target" is amended to "with a due date".
- **"Portfolio" is banned twice**, so the roadmap prompt's "portfolio page per initiative" is the initiative's page (question 16). "Plan" (Roadmap row) and "Theme" (Initiative row) are banned schema-wide too; nothing here uses them, and 3.2's identifiers must not (no `planHealth`, no `themeId`).
- **"Epic", "OKR", "Objective" and "Key result"** stay out of code and UI; a workspace may still name a work item type "Epic" (user data, rule 5).
- **"Active" is reserved** for agent and agent-session states (the "Open, closed" row), so neither lifecycle uses it (question 4). **"Owner" and "Responsible" are banned** for an accountable person (Assignee, Agent owner rows), so the person a goal answers to is the **Lead** (question 5).
- **"Status"** in code stays for fixed enums; a goal's and an initiative's lifecycle are fixed enums, so `GoalStatus` and `InitiativeStatus` are correct names; the UI label is "Status", which collides with the workflow state's UI label only on the item's own screens, where no goal status is shown.

Proposed new rows (rule 1: a row before the first model or label; question 4, 5 and 1 choose the words):

| Concept | Model / code | UI | Not |
|---|---|---|---|
| Health | enum `Health`: `ON_TRACK`, `AT_RISK`, `OFF_TRACK`; `Goal.health`, `Initiative.health` (NULL: none set) | Health; On track, At risk, Off track; "No health set" | Status (the lifecycle's word), RAG, Red, Amber, Green, Traffic light, Confidence |
| Lead | `Goal.leadUserId`, `Initiative.leadUserId`: the one member a goal or initiative answers to; never accountable for any work item (ADR 0003 §1 unchanged) | Lead | Owner (banned), Responsible (banned), Sponsor, Champion, Driver |
| Goal status | enum `GoalStatus`: `PLANNED`, `STARTED`, `COMPLETED`, `CANCELLED` | Planned, Started, Completed, Cancelled | Active (reserved for agents), Achieved, Missed, Done (a state category's word) |
| Initiative status | enum `InitiativeStatus`: the same four values | Planned, Started, Completed, Cancelled | Active, Live, Running, Done |
| Progress | No model: the derived `{ scope, done, started }` of a milestone, initiative or goal | Progress; "12 of 40 done" | Completion (as a model), Burn-down, Velocity, Score |
| Ancestry | No model: the read model `WorkItemAncestry` | Shown as a breadcrumb ("Goal › Initiative › Project › …") | Lineage, Hierarchy (as a model), Path (alone), Tree (as a model) |

## The model (3.2a)

```prisma
enum Health           { ON_TRACK AT_RISK OFF_TRACK }
enum GoalStatus       { PLANNED STARTED COMPLETED CANCELLED }
enum InitiativeStatus { PLANNED STARTED COMPLETED CANCELLED }

model Goal {
  id                 String     @id @default(cuid())
  tenantId           String
  parentId           String?                     // goals nest; at most 3 levels (ADR 0002 §2, decision 24)
  title              String                      // plaintext, sanitised, 1..200 (CHECK)
  description        String?                     // ENCRYPTED (manifest), sanitised, <= 10,000 before encryption
  status             GoalStatus @default(PLANNED)
  dueDate            DateTime?  @db.Date         // "Due date"; never "target"
  leadUserId         String?                     // an ACTIVE member when written; FK RESTRICT, cleared on removal
  health             Health?
  healthSetAt        DateTime?
  healthSetByUserId  String?                     // an actor stamp, like an audit row's user id: no FK
  version            Int        @default(1)
  archivedAt         DateTime?
  archivedByUserId   String?                     // actor stamp, no FK
  createdByUserId    String                      // actor stamp, no FK
  createdAt          DateTime   @default(now())
  updatedAt          DateTime   @updatedAt
  parent   Goal?             @relation("GoalTree", fields: [parentId, tenantId], references: [id, tenantId])   // NO ACTION
  lead     TenantMembership? @relation(fields: [tenantId, leadUserId], references: [tenantId, userId], onDelete: Restrict)
  @@unique([id, tenantId])
  @@index([tenantId, parentId])
  @@index([tenantId, leadUserId])
  @@index([tenantId, archivedAt, status])
}

model Initiative {                               // the same columns as Goal, with:
  id, tenantId,
  goalId             String?                     // FK (goalId, tenantId) -> Goal(id, tenantId), NO ACTION
  name               String                      // plaintext, sanitised, 1..120 (CHECK)
  description, status InitiativeStatus, dueDate, leadUserId, health, healthSetAt, healthSetByUserId,
  version, archivedAt, archivedByUserId, createdByUserId, createdAt, updatedAt
  @@unique([id, tenantId]); @@index([tenantId, goalId]); @@index([tenantId, leadUserId]); @@index([tenantId, archivedAt, status])
}

// Project (exists) gains:
//   initiativeId String?   FK (initiativeId, tenantId) -> Initiative(id, tenantId), NO ACTION
//   @@index([tenantId, initiativeId])
// WorkItem (exists) gains one index for the progress counts:
//   @@index([tenantId, projectId, stateCategory])
```

- **Names follow ADR 0002's sketch** (`Goal.title`, an outcome stated as a sentence; `Initiative.name`, like `Project.name`). Neither is unique: two teams may phrase the same goal.
- **Composite foreign keys and NO ACTION**, as `WorkItem.milestoneId` (2.5b): a workspace's cascade removes both ends in one statement; nothing deletes a goal or initiative on its own (question 14), so no SET NULL is needed. If question 14 chooses delete, the FKs become column-scoped `ON DELETE SET NULL ("initiativeId")` / `("goalId")` / `("parentId")` and the delete usecase audits each unlinked child.
- **The lead** is an ACTIVE member when named (any role but GUEST; a Viewer may lead), `RESTRICT` like the assignee's FK, so `removeTenantMember` clears `leadUserId` on every goal and initiative first, audited (`GOAL_UPDATED` / `INITIATIVE_UPDATED`, `reason: 'member_removed'`), in the same transaction as today's waiting-on and person-field clears.
- **Archive-only** (question 14, A): `app_user` holds SELECT, INSERT and UPDATE on both tables and no DELETE, as `RecipientList`. An archived goal or initiative keeps its children and its place in every ancestry (shown "Archived"); it leaves the roadmap and the lists unless "Show archived" is ticked; restoring brings it back.
- **No `sortKey`**: goals and initiatives list by status (Started, Planned, Completed, Cancelled), then due date, then title. A manual order is a later want, not a 3.2 one.
- **Not a work item** (principle 3): goals and initiatives are records above projects and never appear in the work-item list, board, search or any item read; principle 3 concerns tracked work, and every tracked piece of work stays a work item.
- **Migrations**, each forward-only so the previous image keeps working (the deployment rule): `202612xx120000_goals_and_initiatives` (the three enums, both tables, the CHECKs, RLS, grants), `202612xx120100_project_initiative` (the nullable column, its FK and index), `202612xx120200_work_item_progress_index`. No enum value is added to an existing enum, so no single-value migration is needed; the audit actions are strings validated by `event-schema.ts`, not an enum.
- **Tenant-table rules** for both: `tenantId`, the tenant-leading indexes above, `tenant_isolation`, `tenant_isolation_insert`, `superuser_bypass`, FORCE and their own `GRANT … TO app_user` in the creating migration, two-tenant tests in `ISOLATION_TESTED`; `rls-coverage`, `rls-migration-policies`, `schema-index-coverage` (Layer B: `parentId`, `goalId`, `leadUserId`, `initiativeId`; Layer C: the list reads) and `tenant-isolation-forward-lock` hold them. A catalogue test reads the CHECKs and the missing DELETE grant.
- **Encryption and sanitisation**: `Goal.description` and `Initiative.description` join `ENCRYPTED_FIELDS` and `RICH_TEXT_COVERAGE`'s plain-text list (`sanitizePlainText`, as `WorkItem.description`; rich text waits for 4.1). Titles and names are plaintext (shown in breadcrumbs, the roadmap and notifications), sanitised, classified in `encryption-manifest-coverage.test.ts`. Descriptions are **not** added to `SearchVector` (ADR 0008: a new source is an amendment); searching goals by title is a later decision, not 3.2.

### The tree rules (ADR 0002 §2, made concrete)

- **One lock.** Every write that changes `Goal.parentId` or `Initiative.goalId` takes `lockGoalTree(tx, tenantId)` first: `pg_advisory_xact_lock(hashtext('goal-tree:' || tenantId))`, its own key beside `lockWorkItemGraph`'s, in `src/app-layer/usecases/goal-tree-lock.ts`. A write that also touches projects takes it before any project row (decision 173's order: graph locks, then sequence rows, then item rows).
- **Same workspace**: a parent goal or a goal named by an initiative is read `FOR KEY SHARE` in the caller's tenant (Paperclip's check), and the composite FK refuses another workspace's id anyway.
- **No cycle**: re-parenting a goal refuses a new parent inside its own subtree (a bounded recursive read down from the goal, depth ≤ 3), 409 `GOAL_WOULD_CYCLE`. Paperclip lacks this check.
- **Depth ≤ 3**: the new parent's depth plus the moved subtree's height must stay within three levels, 409 `GOAL_TOO_DEEP` naming the depth. A goal under an archived parent is allowed (archive hides, it does not cut).
- **A project has at most one initiative** (single parent, ADR 0002). Moving a project to another initiative is one field write; its items, milestones and cycles do not move.
- **Nothing is inherited downward.** Paperclip's "goal fallback" (an issue taking its project's or the company's default goal) is not taken: in Islestone an item's goal is never stored, only reached through its project, so a project moved under another initiative changes every item's ancestry at once with no rewrite.

## The ancestry query (3.2a)

One statement, two recursive terms, bounded by ADR 0002's caps (5 levels of sub-items, 3 of goals), carrying `tenantId`, `deletedAt` and `projectVisibilitySql` in its own `WHERE` (the repository-predicate test gains it):

```sql
WITH RECURSIVE item_up AS (
  SELECT w.id, w."parentId", w."projectId", 0 AS depth, ARRAY[w.id] AS path
  FROM "WorkItem" w
  WHERE w."tenantId" = $1 AND w.id = $2 AND w."deletedAt" IS NULL AND <projectVisibilitySql(w)>
  UNION ALL
  SELECT p.id, p."parentId", p."projectId", u.depth + 1, u.path || p.id
  FROM "WorkItem" p JOIN item_up u ON p.id = u."parentId"
  WHERE p."tenantId" = $1 AND p."deletedAt" IS NULL AND u.depth < 5 AND NOT p.id = ANY(u.path)
),
goal_up AS (
  SELECT g.id, g."parentId", 0 AS depth, ARRAY[g.id] AS path
  FROM item_up u
  JOIN "Project" pr    ON pr.id = u."projectId" AND pr."tenantId" = $1 AND u.depth = 0
  JOIN "Initiative" i  ON i.id = pr."initiativeId" AND i."tenantId" = $1
  JOIN "Goal" g        ON g.id = i."goalId" AND g."tenantId" = $1
  UNION ALL
  SELECT g.id, g."parentId", gu.depth + 1, gu.path || g.id
  FROM "Goal" g JOIN goal_up gu ON g.id = gu."parentId"
  WHERE g."tenantId" = $1 AND gu.depth < 3 AND NOT g.id = ANY(gu.path)
)
SELECT 'item' AS kind, … FROM item_up …
UNION ALL SELECT 'project', … UNION ALL SELECT 'initiative', … UNION ALL SELECT 'goal', … FROM goal_up …
```

- **Path guards** stop on a repeat even if a cycle ever slipped past the lock (Paperclip's shape); the depth bounds are ADR 0002's caps, so the walk is at most 9 rows plus the project and initiative.
- **One round trip**: `WorkItemRepository.ancestry(db, ctx, visible, id)` (`$queryRaw`, bound parameters, the plan checked by H2's registry), returning `{ item, parents: root-first, project, initiative | null, goals: root-first }`, each with id, key or title, status and archived flag; never a description.
- **Where it shows** (question 15, A): a breadcrumb on the detail (page and sheet) above the title; `GET /api/t/:slug/work-items/:id/ancestry` (`requirePermission('work_items.view')`; 404 when the item is missing or invisible, as `loadWritableItem`'s read); the list's two new filters `initiativeId` and `goalId` (a goal's filter takes its whole subtree), in `WORK_ITEM_FILTER_KEYS`, the saved-view `FilterKey` and `LIST_FILTER_KEYS`, resolved to a project-id set by one bounded read in `resolveListFiltersInTx`, so the list, board, timeline and (3.3) calendar share them.
- **A goal the caller may not read** (no `goals.view`, possible only for a custom role) is left out of the breadcrumb and counted ("1 level hidden"), never named.
- **Tests**: hand-written vectors (no initiative, no goal, three goal levels, five item levels, an archived goal, a hidden goal); a property test over random trees that the walk returns exactly the chain a naive in-memory walk returns; the statement count (one); the plan uses the `[id]` primary keys and `[tenantId, parentId]`.

## Progress and health (3.2b)

### Progress, derived (question 8)

- **One pure function**, `domain/progress.ts` (`progressOf`, `isLate`, moved from `usecases/milestone.ts`, which then imports it): from `(projectId, stateCategory, count)` rows to `{ scope, done, started }`. **Scope** is every live item not cancelled; **done** the Done ones; **started** the Started ones. Milestones keep their meaning exactly.
- **Which items** (question 8, A): every live (`deletedAt IS NULL`), visible (`projectVisibilitySql`), non-cancelled item of every live project under the initiative, or under every initiative of the goal and of every goal below it; **archived projects included** (an initiative's finished projects are usually archived, and leaving them out would make progress fall as work completes); deleted projects excluded. Sub-items count as items, as they do for milestones. Each item is counted once, guaranteed by the single chain.
- **The read**: one grouped count, `WorkItemRepository.countsByProjects(db, ctx, visible, projectIds)` over the new `[tenantId, projectId, stateCategory]` index, the project ids from one bounded read down the tree (a goal's subtree: a recursive read, depth ≤ 3; at most 1,000 projects per goal, refused above with the count, never cut).
- **"Late"**: a due date passed (today in the workspace calendar's zone, as 3.1) with `scope > done`, as a milestone's.
- **Zero is never complete**: a scope of 0 shows "No items", never "100 %" and never "On track" by implication; a goal with no initiatives says so.
- **Not stored, not cached, no job**: computed on every read of a goal, an initiative, the goal and initiative lists and the roadmap, like the burn-up and milestones. 3.4's estimates may later weight progress by points; that is a later decision, recorded in `docs/goals.md`.

### Health, set by a person (questions 1, 2, 3 and 10)

- `PUT /api/t/:slug/goals/:id/health` and `PUT /api/t/:slug/initiatives/:id/health`, body `{ health: 'ON_TRACK' | 'AT_RISK' | 'OFF_TRACK' | null }`, `requirePermission('goals.manage')`, `If-Match` optional; usecase `setGoalHealth` / `setInitiativeHealth` in `src/app-layer/usecases/goal.ts` / `initiative.ts`.
- **A person only** (`assertActingAsPerson`, as marking an answer and responding to a review step): an API key (5.2), an agent session (6.3) and a job are refused, 403 audited. The roadmap's "set by a person" is read literally; an agent may explain a goal's state in 7.4's digest, never set it, and no proposal kind sets health.
- **Never computed**: progress, "late" and the items' states never change health; a goal 100 % done with health "Off track" shows both, because they say different things.
- **Recorded on the row** (`health`, `healthSetAt`, `healthSetByUserId`) and audited `GOAL_HEALTH_SET` / `INITIATIVE_HEALTH_SET` with the old and new values (`category: 'entity_lifecycle'`); the page's "Health history" reads those audit entries (as an item's Activity reads `WORK_ITEM_*`), newest first, bounded. No written update and no notification in 3.2 (question 3 offers a written update; 7.4's digests and a later notification type are where a change would be announced).
- **Archived or cancelled** goals and initiatives keep their last health, shown greyed; setting health on an archived one is 409 `ARCHIVED`.

## The roadmap (3.2c)

### Where it appears

`/t/<slug>/roadmap` (a tab of the Goals entry), and each initiative's page scoped to it. It is **not** a `ViewLayout` value: ADR 0006 calls it "the timeline layout over projects and initiatives", and its rows are initiatives and projects, not work items, so it is a page with its own read rather than a saved view of items. The items' timeline (3.1b) stays the way into item-level dates.

### Rows and bars (question 11, A)

| Row | Bar | Marks |
|---|---|---|
| Goal (an optional grouping header, by `?groupBy=goal`) | the span of its initiatives | its due date as a diamond |
| Initiative (a summary row) | the span of its projects | its due date as a diamond; health and progress in the grid columns |
| Project | from the **earliest start date** to the **latest due date** of its live, non-cancelled items (stored dates; an item with only a due date contributes that day) | the project's due date as a diamond; each milestone's due date as a diamond; "Late" when its due date has passed with open items, or its latest open item is due after it, or a milestone is late |
| "No initiative" (a summary row) | its projects, as above | — |
| A project with no dated item | no bar, listed in the "No dates" panel beside the chart (3.1b's pattern) | its due date diamond if it has one |

- **Stored dates, not the engine.** The engine (ADR 0010) answers per project on that project's timeline, bounded at 5,000 items; a workspace roadmap may span 100,000. The roadmap therefore reads one grouped aggregate per project (`min("startDate")`, `max("dueDate")`, counts by category) and shows planned dates; it never presents a computed forecast it did not compute (principle 4), and each project row links to the project's timeline for the critical path. Question 11 offers running the engine per project instead.
- **Goal and initiative due dates are shown, not enforced**: they are not engine deadlines (ADR 0010 §3 lists the milestone's and the project's), so a project running past its initiative's due date is flagged on the roadmap ("Finishes after the initiative's due date") and nothing else; question 6 offers making them deadlines.

### The read

`GET /api/t/:slug/roadmap?goalId=&initiativeId=&teamId=&leadUserId=&health=&status=&archived=` (`requirePermission('goals.view')`; the usecase asserts `projects.view`), usecase `getRoadmap` in `src/app-layer/usecases/roadmap.ts`:

1. The initiatives and goals in scope (bounded reads; archived only when asked).
2. The visible, live projects in scope (`visibleProjects`), at most **500**; above it 422 `ROADMAP_TOO_LARGE` with the count ("Narrow the filter: 612 projects"), never a cut-off list.
3. One aggregate over `WorkItem` grouped by project (spans and progress), on `[tenantId, projectId, stateCategory]` and `[tenantId, projectId, dueDate, id]`.
4. The milestones of those projects (at most 2,000, refused above with the count) with their progress and "late" (2.5b's read).
5. Today in the workspace calendar's zone (3.1).

No description, comment or custom-field value is read. Every raw read carries `tenantId`, `deletedAt` and `projectVisibilitySql`; H2's plan registry gains the aggregate. Budget on H2's reference seed (500 projects, 100,000 items): p50 300 ms, p95 700 ms, at most 6 statements, response ≤ 400 KB uncompressed.

### The client (question 12, A; question 13, A)

- **The chart**: SVAR React Gantt 2.8.3 through **the same adapter file** (`src/components/work-items/timeline/svar-adapter.tsx`; `tests/guards/svar-core-only.test.ts` allows SVAR imports there only), which gains a second row model, `roadmap-model.ts` (initiatives and goals as `summary` tasks, projects as tasks, milestones and due dates as `milestone` tasks), loaded with `next/dynamic` and `ssr: false` on the roadmap only. `fonts={false}`, no PRO option, every editing action intercepted to `false` and `readonly` set: **the roadmap is read-only in 3.2** (question 13). Zoom by month and quarter (default quarter), stored per viewer in `localStorage` (a convenience, wrapped in try/catch).
- **The accessible equivalent** (decision 203's rule, applied again): "Chart / Table", the table showing goal, initiative, project, start, finish, due date, progress, health, late flags, with the same filters.
- **Today line, shading and the "No dates" panel** reuse 3.1b's code; non-working days are not shaded at month and quarter zoom.
- **Copy** is `roadmap.*`; "Gantt" stays inside the adapter's import (ADR 0006).

## Agents, later (ADR 0003)

## What makes the prompt harder than it reads

- **"Every work item can answer its ancestry in one query"** is easy only because ADR 0002 made every link single-parent. Paperclip, the named source, hangs goals on issues, projects and a many-to-many at once, and so cannot answer "why" with one path; taking its schema would undo ADR 0002.
- **"A portfolio page per initiative"** uses a word ADR 0006 bans twice. The page is built; the word is not.
- **"Progress rolled up from child items"** must not count an item twice, must not drop finished work, and must not call an empty goal complete: the single chain, archived projects included, and "No items" answer the three.
- **"A roadmap view across projects"** cannot reuse the engine at workspace scale without breaking 3.1's 5,000-item bound or inventing a forecast. Stored dates, honestly labelled, are the roadmap; the engine stays where it is bounded.
- **"A health status set by a person"** rules out computing it and rules out agents setting it, which is what gives it meaning beside the derived progress.

## Open questions for the owner

Each question stands alone. The recommended option is first and marked; each option carries its main trade-off.

**Words and states**

1. **What are the health values a person can set on a goal or an initiative?**
   - A (recommended). On track, At risk, Off track, plus "No health set" when nobody has set one. Three plain words most planning tools use; no colour names, so it reads the same in both themes and to screen readers.
   - B. Four values: add "Completed" as a health. Lets a finished goal show green; but it repeats the lifecycle status and a person could set a health that contradicts it.
   - C. A traffic light (Red, Amber, Green). Familiar to some teams; it names colours, which says nothing to a colour-blind reader or a screen reader, and ADR 0006 would need the colour words added.

2. **Which records carry a health?**
   - A (recommended). Both goals and initiatives. The initiative's page (the roadmap's "portfolio page") is where most status conversations happen, and it needs its own health.
   - B. Goals only, as ADR 0002's sketch drew it. Smaller; initiatives would show progress but no human judgement.
   - C. Goals, initiatives and projects. Covers project status too; adds a column and a control to every project surface, beyond what the roadmap asks.

3. **When a person changes a health, do they also write why?**
   - A (recommended). No: the health, who set it and when are stored on the record, and the history is read from the audit entries (values only). Small, and nothing new to encrypt or moderate.
   - B. Yes: each change carries a short written update, kept as a history (encrypted, sanitised, a new table). Gives context ("vendor slipped two weeks"); adds a model, a word and a text that must be protected like a comment.
   - C. An optional line of text on the record itself, replaced at each change. Some context without a history table; earlier explanations are lost.

4. **What lifecycle do goals and initiatives have?**
   - A (recommended). Both: Planned, Started, Completed, Cancelled. One set of words for both, and it avoids "Active", which ADR 0006 reserves for agents.
   - B. No lifecycle: only archive and restore, with health and progress telling the story. Simplest; a finished goal is indistinguishable from an abandoned one except by archiving.
   - C. Goals: Planned, Started, Achieved, Missed, Cancelled; initiatives: Planned, Started, Completed, Cancelled. Says whether a goal was met; two vocabularies to learn, and "Missed" overlaps the derived "Late".

5. **Does a goal or an initiative name one accountable person, and what is that person called?**
   - A (recommended). Yes, optionally: the "Lead", one active member (Viewers included). "Owner" and "Responsible" are banned for this (ADR 0006), and a lead never becomes accountable for any work item.
   - B. No person field. Avoids another people column to clear when members leave; nobody is named as answering for a goal.
   - C. Yes, called "Sponsor". Fits executive-sponsored initiatives; reads oddly on a team-level goal.

**Structure**

6. **Do goals and initiatives have due dates, and do they push on the schedule?**
   - A (recommended). Both may have an optional date-only due date, shown on their pages and as diamonds on the roadmap, flagged when a project finishes after it, but not a deadline for the scheduling engine. Useful dates with no change to 3.1's engine.
   - B. Goals only, as ADR 0002's sketch had (renamed from "target" to "due date"). Smaller; initiatives get their dates only from their projects.
   - C. Both, and an initiative's due date becomes an engine deadline for every item of its projects. Item timelines would show "Misses the initiative's due date"; amends ADR 0010 §3 and widens the timeline read.

7. **May a project link straight to a goal when it belongs to no initiative?**
   - A (recommended). No: the chain stays goal → initiative → project as ADR 0002 decided; a team that wants a project under a goal makes an initiative for it. One path, no exceptions.
   - B. Yes: a project may name a goal or an initiative, never both (a CHECK), so small workspaces skip the middle level. Ancestry stays single; ADR 0002 §2 is amended and every reader handles two shapes.
   - C. Yes, and work items may name a goal directly too, as Paperclip does. Most flexible; an item could then claim a goal its project does not serve, and "why" gets two answers.

8. **Which items count towards an initiative's or a goal's progress?**
   - A (recommended). Every live, visible, non-cancelled item of every project under it, archived projects included, sub-items counted like any item (as milestones count). Finished projects keep counting after they are archived, so progress never falls as work completes.
   - B. The same, but archived projects excluded (as cycle counts are). Progress shows only current work; archiving a finished project lowers both the done count and the scope.
   - C. Leaf items only (items with no live sub-items). Avoids counting a parent and its children separately; differs from how milestones count, so the same items could show two different percentages.

**Permissions**

9. **Who may create and change goals and initiatives?**
   - A (recommended). A new permission domain, `goals` (view, manage): Owners, Admins and Members manage, Viewers read, guests nothing; custom roles can grant or remove it. Mirrors the cycles domain (decision 72); adds a domain to roles, the custom-role editor and the permission matrix.
   - B. No new domain: read under `projects.view`, change under `projects.edit`. Cheapest; a custom role cannot let someone edit projects without editing goals, or the reverse.
   - C. Read under `projects.view`, change under `admin.manage` only. Keeps strategy with admins; members cannot record their own team's goals.

10. **Who may set a goal's or an initiative's health?**
    - A (recommended). Anyone who may change goals (question 9), as a person: never an API key, a job or an agent. Matches "set by a person" and keeps health a human judgement.
    - B. Only its lead, or an admin when it has no lead. Clear accountability; a stale health waits on one person.
    - C. Anyone who can read goals. Most current; a Viewer could change a status that others plan around.

**The roadmap**

11. **Where do a project's bar dates on the roadmap come from?**
    - A (recommended). Stored dates: from its items' earliest start date to their latest due date, with the project's due date and its milestones as diamonds and "late" flags derived. Fast at workspace scale and never shows a number nobody computed; it shows plans, not forecasts.
    - B. The scheduling engine run per project in the request, showing each project's computed finish. The real forecast; heavy (each project up to 5,000 items), so the roadmap would refuse large scopes much earlier.
    - C. A new stored start date on each project, with the bar from it to the project's due date. Simple and editable; a second set of dates that drifts from the items' own.

12. **Which chart draws the roadmap?**
    - A (recommended). The timeline library already in use (SVAR React Gantt, MIT core), through the same single adapter, read-only. One look and one accessibility layer for both timelines; zoom and virtualisation come with it.
    - B. inflect-compliance's visx chart (`gantt-chart.tsx`), copied. Lighter for a few dozen rows; no zoom or virtualisation, and a second chart to theme and keep accessible.
    - C. No chart: a table with dates and progress bars. Cheapest and fully accessible; not a roadmap people can scan at a glance.

13. **Can dates be changed from the roadmap?**
    - A (recommended). No: the roadmap is read-only in 3.2; a project's due date is changed on the project, item dates on the timeline. Nothing new to confirm or undo.
    - B. Dragging a project's due-date diamond opens a confirmation and changes the project's due date. Handy for planners; a second date-editing surface with its own confirmation and tests.

**Lifecycle, pages and delivery**

14. **Can goals and initiatives be deleted?**
    - A (recommended). No: archive and restore only, as recipient lists. Every ancestry and audit entry keeps pointing at something real; clutter is hidden by archiving.
    - B. Yes, through the undo toast; children are unlinked (projects lose their initiative, initiatives their goal, child goals move up). Tidier lists; ancestry history changes silently for every item underneath.
    - C. Yes, but only an archived one with nothing linked under it. Allows cleaning up mistakes; one more rule to explain.

15. **Where does a work item show its ancestry?**
    - A (recommended). A breadcrumb on the item's detail, an ancestry route for agents and integrations, and two list filters ("Initiative", "Goal") that the list, board, timeline and calendar share. Answers "why" where people look, and lets them slice work by goal.
    - B. The breadcrumb and the route only, no filters. Smaller; slicing work by goal has to go through projects.
    - C. All of A plus Initiative and Goal columns in the list. Most visible; two more columns to compute on every list page.

16. **What is the roadmap's "portfolio page per initiative" called?**
    - A (recommended). It is simply the initiative's page, titled with the initiative's name, under Goals → Initiatives. ADR 0006 bans "Portfolio" for both initiatives and the roadmap, so no new word is needed.
    - B. Amend ADR 0006 to allow "Portfolio" as this page's label. Matches the roadmap's wording; reintroduces a word the vocabulary deliberately banned, beside "Initiative" for the same thing.

17. **How do people reach goals, initiatives and the roadmap?**
    - A (recommended). One sidebar entry, "Goals", whose page has three tabs: Goals, Initiatives, Roadmap. Keeps the sidebar short; the roadmap is one click deeper.
    - B. Three sidebar entries. Each is one click away; three more rows in a sidebar that already holds nine.
    - C. "Goals" in the sidebar, and the roadmap as a tab on the Projects page. Puts the roadmap beside the projects it draws; splits the planning pages across two places.

18. **How is 3.2 delivered?**
    - A (recommended). Three pull requests: 3.2a the model, routes and ancestry; 3.2b progress, health and the goal and initiative pages; 3.2c the roadmap. Each reviewable alone, and the ancestry lands first for 6.2.
    - B. Two: the model with progress and pages, then the roadmap. Fewer reviews; the first is large.
    - C. One. Simplest to track; about 9,000 lines in one review.

19. **How are these decisions recorded in the ADRs?**
    - A (recommended). Amend ADR 0002 (§2 made concrete and a new §17 on goals, initiatives and progress) and ADR 0006 (the new words); no new ADR. The chain was already ADR 0002's decision.
