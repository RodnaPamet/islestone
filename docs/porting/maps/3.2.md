## Summary

- **The chain is ADR 0002 §2's** (owner decision 24): goal → initiative → project → work item → sub-item, each link single-parent and nullable; a project never names a goal directly. **One addition (decision 211, a departure):** a work item may also name one goal directly (`WorkItem.goalId`), which may be a goal its project's initiative does not serve. An item's ancestry is therefore its project path plus, at most, one direct goal path.
- **Two new tenant tables, `Goal` and `Initiative`, and two new columns, `Project.initiativeId` and `WorkItem.goalId`.** Workspace-scoped (no team, no project), titles and names plaintext, descriptions encrypted and sanitised, an optional date-only due date (decision 210, not an engine deadline), an optional **Lead** (decision 209), a lifecycle status Planned, Started, Completed, Cancelled (decision 208) and a **health set by a person** on both (decisions 205, 206): On track, At risk, Off track or Completed, and "No health set" when none is. Health and status are independent: health is never derived from the status, progress or anything else.
- **Archive and soft delete** (decision 218, a departure): a Completed or Cancelled goal or initiative may be archived (kept whole, hidden); any goal or initiative may be deleted, softly (`deletedAt`, `deletionId`), and restored like a project; deleting a goal takes its child goals and its initiatives with it in one group; links from projects and items are kept while deleted. **After 30 days a nightly job purges deleted rows** (decision 219, `GOAL_PURGE_DAYS`), and the purge clears every link to them (`ON DELETE SET NULL`).
- **Progress is derived, never stored** (decision 212), as a milestone's (2.5b): the live, visible, non-cancelled items under it, archived projects included, done the Done ones; for a goal, the items reaching it through a project's initiative **or** naming it (or a goal below it) directly, each item counted once; "late" when the due date has passed with open items; zero items is "No items", never 100 %.
- **Ancestry is one statement**: a `WITH RECURSIVE` up `WorkItem.parentId` (at most 5), then the project, its initiative and up `Goal.parentId` (at most 3), and from the item's own `goalId` up `Goal.parentId` again. A breadcrumb on the detail, `GET /work-items/:id/ancestry`, and two list filters, `initiativeId` and `goalId`, the second matching both paths (decision 220).
- **The roadmap is a page, not a model** (ADR 0006's Roadmap row): initiatives as summary rows, their projects as bars, stored due dates as diamonds, on the SVAR timeline already in the tree (3.1b) through the same adapter, read-only (decision 216). **A bar is derived, never stored** (decision 215, a departure): a project's bar is the scheduling engine's span of its items' start and due dates, an initiative's the span of its projects; a scope with no dated item goes in a "No dates" list. **A bar opens 3.1's timeline** scoped to that initiative's or project's items, where dates change through 3.1c's flow (decision 217, a departure).
- **"Portfolio page" is a banned word**, so the roadmap's portfolio page is **the initiative's page** (decision 221).
- **Navigation** (decision 222): one sidebar entry, **Roadmap**, holding three pages, Goals, Initiatives and Roadmap, each its own route under `/roadmap`; a goal's and an initiative's pages are subpages with canonical parents.
- **Permissions:** a new `goals` domain (`goals.view`, `goals.manage`, decision 213); a project's initiative is a project field under `projects.edit`, an item's goal a work-item field under `work_items.edit`, each also asking `goals.view` to name one; health is a person's act only (decision 214). A GUEST sees nothing (decision 204).
- **Ports:** nothing from inflect-compliance. From Paperclip, **pattern only**: the depth-capped, path-guarded recursive ancestor walk and the same-tenant parent check. Paperclip's goal fallback, its `project_goals` many-to-many and its agent-owned goals are not taken; its `issues.goalId` has a counterpart now (decision 211), written fresh and never inherited.
- **Three pull requests** (decision 223): 3.2a the model with the item's goal, soft delete and the purge, the routes and the ancestry; 3.2b progress, health and the pages; 3.2c the roadmap. About 6,500 lines of source and 5,300 of tests.

## Vocabulary (ADR 0006, decided)

ADR 0006 already reserved Goal, Initiative and Roadmap; decision 224 amends the rows and adds the new ones (applied, "Goal and roadmap words of sub-phase 3.2"):

- **Goal** — "An outcome the workspace wants, with a due date and a health set by a person." "Target" stays banned schema-wide, so ADR 0002's sketched `Goal.targetDate` is `dueDate`, labelled "Due date", as `Milestone.dueDate` and `Project.dueDate` are.
- **Initiative** — "A set of projects that together serve a goal." Not: Program, Portfolio, Theme, Epic.
- **Roadmap** — the timeline page over projects and initiatives, **and the sidebar section** that holds the Goals, Initiatives and Roadmap pages (decision 222). The word now names an area and one of its pages; the page's heading and the section's three tabs tell them apart. No model. Not: Portfolio, Plan.
- **"Portfolio" is banned twice**, so the roadmap prompt's "portfolio page per initiative" is the initiative's page (decision 221). "Plan" and "Theme" stay banned schema-wide; 3.2's identifiers use neither.
- **"Active" is reserved** for agent and agent-session states, so neither lifecycle uses it (decision 208). **"Owner" and "Responsible" are banned** for an accountable person, so the person a goal answers to is the **Lead** (decision 209).
- **"Completed" is both a health value and a status value** (decisions 205 and 208). ADR 0006 reserved neither word before 3.2, so nothing clashes with an existing row; within 3.2 the two meanings are kept apart by always showing the field's name with the value ("Health: Completed", "Status: Completed") and by the rule that neither sets the other.

## The model (3.2a)

```prisma
enum Health           { ON_TRACK AT_RISK OFF_TRACK COMPLETED }
enum GoalStatus       { PLANNED STARTED COMPLETED CANCELLED }
enum InitiativeStatus { PLANNED STARTED COMPLETED CANCELLED }

model Goal {
  id                 String     @id @default(cuid())
  tenantId           String
  parentId           String?                     // goals nest; at most 3 levels (ADR 0002 §2, decision 24)
  title              String                      // plaintext, sanitised, 1..200 (CHECK)
  description        String?                     // ENCRYPTED (manifest), sanitised, <= 10,000 before encryption
  status             GoalStatus @default(PLANNED)
  dueDate            DateTime?  @db.Date         // "Due date"; never "target"; not an engine deadline (decision 210)
  leadUserId         String?                     // an ACTIVE member when written; FK RESTRICT, cleared on removal
  health             Health?                     // a person's judgement; never derived (decisions 205, 214)
  healthSetAt        DateTime?
  healthSetByUserId  String?                     // an actor stamp, like an audit row's user id: no FK
  version            Int        @default(1)
  archivedAt         DateTime?                   // only while status is COMPLETED or CANCELLED (CHECK)
  archivedByUserId   String?                     // actor stamp, no FK
  deletedAt          DateTime?                   // soft delete (decision 218)
  deletedByUserId    String?                     // actor stamp, no FK
  deletionId         String?                     // one group per delete; CHECK: both or neither with deletedAt
  createdByUserId    String                      // actor stamp, no FK
  createdAt          DateTime   @default(now())
  updatedAt          DateTime   @updatedAt
  parent   Goal?             @relation("GoalTree", fields: [parentId, tenantId], references: [id, tenantId])
  lead     TenantMembership? @relation(fields: [tenantId, leadUserId], references: [tenantId, userId], onDelete: Restrict)
  @@unique([id, tenantId])
  @@index([tenantId, parentId])
  @@index([tenantId, leadUserId])
  @@index([tenantId, deletedAt, archivedAt, status])
  @@index([tenantId, deletionId])
}

model Initiative {                               // the same columns as Goal, with:
  id, tenantId,
  goalId             String?                     // FK (goalId, tenantId) -> Goal(id, tenantId)
  name               String                      // plaintext, sanitised, 1..120 (CHECK)
  description, status InitiativeStatus, dueDate, leadUserId, health, healthSetAt, healthSetByUserId,
  version, archivedAt, archivedByUserId, deletedAt, deletedByUserId, deletionId, createdByUserId, createdAt, updatedAt
  @@unique([id, tenantId]); @@index([tenantId, goalId]); @@index([tenantId, leadUserId])
  @@index([tenantId, deletedAt, archivedAt, status]); @@index([tenantId, deletionId])
}

// Project (exists) gains:
//   initiativeId String?   FK (initiativeId, tenantId) -> Initiative(id, tenantId)
//   @@index([tenantId, initiativeId])
// WorkItem (exists) gains (decision 211):
//   goalId       String?   FK (goalId, tenantId) -> Goal(id, tenantId)
//   @@index([tenantId, goalId, stateCategory])     // the FK's index and the direct-goal progress count
//   @@index([tenantId, projectId, stateCategory])  // the project-path progress count
```

- **Names follow ADR 0002's sketch** (`Goal.title`, an outcome stated as a sentence; `Initiative.name`, like `Project.name`). Neither is unique: two teams may phrase the same goal.
- **Four composite foreign keys, each `ON DELETE SET NULL` on its own column** (decision 219): `Goal.parentId`, `Initiative.goalId`, `Project.initiativeId` and `WorkItem.goalId` are written in the migration as `ON DELETE SET NULL ("parentId")`, `("goalId")`, `("initiativeId")` and `("goalId")`, as `ProjectIntake.recipientListId` is, so the tenant column is never nulled and a workspace's cascade still removes both ends. Only the purge deletes a goal or an initiative; nothing else does, and the usecases never hard-delete. Prisma cannot declare the column list; the drift check reads the migration as it does for the recipient list's key.
- **The lead** is an ACTIVE member when named (any role but GUEST; a Viewer may lead), `RESTRICT` like the assignee's FK, so `removeTenantMember` clears `leadUserId` on every goal and initiative first, deleted ones included (as the milestone delete clears deleted items), audited (`GOAL_UPDATED` / `INITIATIVE_UPDATED`, `reason: 'member_removed'`), in the same transaction as today's waiting-on and person-field clears.
- **Grants.** `app_user` holds SELECT, INSERT, UPDATE and DELETE on both tables; DELETE is used by the purge alone (`GoalRepository.purgeBatch` and `InitiativeRepository.purgeBatch`, which take only rows deleted more than `GOAL_PURGE_DAYS` ago). This differs from the archive-only map (no DELETE): the purge needs it, as `work-item-purge` does.
- **No `sortKey`**: goals and initiatives list by status (Started, Planned, Completed, Cancelled), then due date, then title.
- **Not a work item** (principle 3): goals and initiatives are records above projects and never appear in the work-item list, board, search or any item read.
- **Migrations**, each forward-only so the previous image keeps working: `202612xx120000_goals_and_initiatives` (the three enums, both tables, the CHECKs, RLS, grants), `202612xx120100_project_initiative_and_item_goal` (the two nullable columns, their column-scoped `SET NULL` keys and indexes), `202612xx120200_field_set_goal` (the `WorkItemTypeField.builtInField` CHECK widened to `goal`), `202612xx120300_work_item_progress_indexes`. No value is added to an existing enum; the audit actions are strings validated by `event-schema.ts`.
- **Tenant-table rules** for both: `tenantId`, the tenant-leading indexes above, `tenant_isolation`, `tenant_isolation_insert`, `superuser_bypass`, FORCE and their own `GRANT … TO app_user` in the creating migration, two-tenant tests in `ISOLATION_TESTED`; `rls-coverage`, `rls-migration-policies`, `schema-index-coverage` (Layer B: `parentId`, `goalId` on both tables, `leadUserId`, `initiativeId`; Layer C: the list reads) and `tenant-isolation-forward-lock` hold them. A catalogue test reads the CHECKs and the four column-scoped keys.
- **Encryption and sanitisation**: `Goal.description` and `Initiative.description` join `ENCRYPTED_FIELDS` and `RICH_TEXT_COVERAGE`'s plain-text list (`sanitizePlainText`). Titles and names are plaintext (shown in breadcrumbs, the roadmap and notifications), sanitised, classified in `encryption-manifest-coverage.test.ts`. Descriptions are **not** added to `SearchVector` (ADR 0008).

### Status, health, archive and delete (decisions 205, 208, 218)

- **Status** is set by a person who may change goals, any of the four values in any order (a Completed goal may be started again). It never changes health, progress or anything else.
- **Health is independent of status** (the rule recorded for decision 205). Health is a person's judgement of the outcome; it is never derived from the status, progress or "late", nothing sets or clears it when the status changes, and no combination is refused. A goal Started with health "Completed" and a goal Completed with health "Off track" are both stored and shown as set, each with who set it and when; the page shows them side by side with their names ("Status: Started · Health: Completed").
- **Archive** (kept from the map, narrowed by decision 218): `POST /goals/:id/archive { archived }` archives a goal whose status is Completed or Cancelled (409 `GOAL_NOT_FINISHED` otherwise; a CHECK holds `archivedAt` to those statuses) and unarchives it. An archived goal keeps its children, its links and its place in every ancestry (shown "Archived"); it leaves the lists, the pickers and the roadmap unless "Show archived" is ticked; it cannot be named by a new link (409 `GOAL_ARCHIVED`); its own fields, status and health are read-only until it is unarchived (409 `ARCHIVED`). Archiving a goal archives nothing below it. The same for initiatives (`INITIATIVE_NOT_FINISHED`, `INITIATIVE_ARCHIVED`).
- **Delete** (decision 218): `DELETE /goals/:id` soft-deletes any goal (archived or not) under `lockGoalTree`: the goal, every goal below it not already deleted and every initiative of all of them not already deleted get one new `deletionId`, `deletedAt` and `deletedByUserId` in one transaction. **Links are kept**: projects keep `initiativeId` and items keep `goalId`. `DELETE /initiatives/:id` soft-deletes one initiative; its projects keep the link. The browser sends it through the undo toast (`SITE_CONTRACTS`), and the Goals and Initiatives pages have a "Deleted" view with Restore for 30 days.
- **While deleted**, every read leaves the row out: lists (except "Deleted"), pickers, the roadmap, progress (no deleted goal or initiative is counted through, and none shows progress) and the ancestry (a project whose initiative is deleted shows no initiative; an item whose direct goal is deleted shows none; the walk stops at a deleted goal). A list filter naming a deleted goal or initiative is 404 `GOAL_NOT_FOUND` / `INITIATIVE_NOT_FOUND`, and so is every write naming one. Its row stays in the tree checks below, so a restore can never make a cycle or pass the depth cap.
- **Restore** (`POST /goals/:id/restore`, `POST /initiatives/:id/restore`) brings back the whole group the person deleted (its `deletionId`), links intact, before the purge. A goal or initiative deleted as part of a larger group is restored only through that group's root (409 `DELETED_WITH_PARENT`, naming the root). A restore whose own parent goal is deleted, or (for an initiative) whose goal is deleted, is refused until that one is restored (409 `PARENT_DELETED`, naming it).
- **The purge** (decision 219): `goal-purge`, nightly at 02:35 UTC (after the 02:30 UTC work-item purge, off the hour), in every live workspace as its system user, deletes goals and initiatives deleted more than `GOAL_PURGE_DAYS` (default 30, a new `src/env.ts` variable with `.env.example`, the compose file and `deploy/.env.prod.example`) days ago, in bounded pages in id order. The database clears every link to a purged row (the four `SET NULL` keys): a project loses its initiative, an initiative its goal, a goal its parent, an item its goal. The job counts what it will unlink in the same transaction and writes one counts-only `GOALS_PURGED` entry per workspace (goals, initiatives, projects unlinked, items unlinked), never a title. The unlink writes no item audit entry, bumps no `version` and fires no transition (the trigger does not watch `goalId`). A `JobPayloadMap` key, `JOB_DEFAULTS`, a registration naming its fields and a `SCHEDULED_JOBS` entry, as every job.

### The tree rules (ADR 0002 §2, made concrete)

- **One lock.** Every write that changes `Goal.parentId` or `Initiative.goalId`, and every goal delete and restore, takes `lockGoalTree(tx, tenantId)` first: `pg_advisory_xact_lock(hashtext('goal-tree:' || tenantId))`, in `src/app-layer/usecases/goal-tree-lock.ts`. A write that also touches projects or items takes it before any project or item row (decision 173's order: graph locks, then sequence rows, then item rows). Setting an item's `goalId` does not take it: it changes no tree link, and the goal is read `FOR KEY SHARE`.
- **Same workspace**: a parent goal, a goal named by an initiative and a goal named by an item are read `FOR KEY SHARE` in the caller's tenant (Paperclip's check), and the composite FK refuses another workspace's id anyway.
- **No cycle**: re-parenting a goal refuses a new parent inside its own subtree, deleted goals included (a bounded recursive read down, depth ≤ 3), 409 `GOAL_WOULD_CYCLE`.
- **Depth ≤ 3**: the new parent's depth plus the moved subtree's height, deleted goals included, must stay within three levels, 409 `GOAL_TOO_DEEP` naming the depth. A goal under an archived parent is allowed (archive hides, it does not cut).
- **A project has at most one initiative**, and **an item at most one direct goal** (single parent, ADR 0002). Moving a project to another initiative is one field write; its items, milestones and cycles do not move.
- **Nothing is inherited downward.** An item's direct goal is only what a person set on that item: a sub-item does not take its parent's, a new item does not take its project's initiative's goal, and moving a project to another initiative rewrites no item. Paperclip's goal fallback is not taken.

## The item's direct goal (decision 211; 3.2a)

- **What it is**: `WorkItem.goalId`, one goal of the item's workspace, nullable, set by a person. It may be a goal the item's project's initiative does not serve; the hierarchy above is unchanged, and a project still never names a goal.
- **Who sets it**: `PUT /api/t/:slug/work-items/:id/goal { goalId: string | null }`, `requirePermission('work_items.edit')`; the usecase (`setWorkItemGoal`, in `work-item-goal.ts`) also asserts `goals.view` to name a goal (clearing needs only the edit key), refuses a deleted goal (404 `GOAL_NOT_FOUND`) and an archived one (409 `GOAL_ARCHIVED`), passes `loadWritableItem`'s gates (404, 409 `PROJECT_ARCHIVED`) and the field set (`assertFieldWrite`, 400 `FIELD_HIDDEN`), and writes through the versioned `updateVersioned` as a single-field change (`If-Match` optional), as the milestone and cycle writes do. Closed items may be given or cleared a goal, as their milestone may. Audited `WORK_ITEM_GOAL_CHANGED` (the old and new goal ids); after the commit, the same effects as a milestone change. A create (dialog, API) accepts `goalId` too, under the same checks.
- **Field sets**: `goal` joins `CONFIGURABLE_BUILT_IN_FIELDS` (`src/lib/work-items/field-sets.ts`) beside `milestone` and `cycle`, so a type may require or hide it; the migration widens the stored CHECK. A REQUIRED goal is checked on every create path, like a REQUIRED milestone; `tests/integration/field-set-routes.test.ts` gains the route's row.
- **Moves keep it**: goals are workspace-wide, so a move to another project or team keeps `goalId` (unlike the milestone and cycle, which a move clears).
- **Copies**: a repeat copy takes its source's goal through `templateToCreateInput`, skipped and named (`goal:deleted`, `goal:archived`) when the goal is no longer linkable, never a refusal. Item templates and project templates do not carry a goal (no column; a template outlives the goals it would name).
- **Intake accept** creates through `createWorkItemInTx`, which takes `goalId` like any create field; the accept dialog offers it where it offers the milestone.
- **The transition record does not watch it** (`WorkItemTransition` has no `goalId`): progress is derived from today's rows, a goal's history through the project path is not per item, and 3.6 adds the column if it wants goal history.
- **Shown** on the detail (page and sheet) as a "Goal" picker (live, unarchived goals the caller may read) and in the create dialog; the breadcrumb (below); the `goalId` filter. No list column (decision 220).
- **Agents**: setting an item's goal is a candidate proposal kind for 6.2, approved by a person holding `work_items.edit` and `goals.view`.

## The ancestry query (3.2a)

One statement, three recursive terms, bounded by ADR 0002's caps (5 levels of sub-items, 3 of goals), carrying `tenantId`, `deletedAt` (the item's, each goal's and the initiative's) and `projectVisibilitySql` in its own `WHERE`:

```sql
WITH RECURSIVE item_up AS (
  SELECT w.id, w."parentId", w."projectId", w."goalId", 0 AS depth, ARRAY[w.id] AS path
  FROM "WorkItem" w
  WHERE w."tenantId" = $1 AND w.id = $2 AND w."deletedAt" IS NULL AND <projectVisibilitySql(w)>
  UNION ALL
  SELECT p.id, p."parentId", p."projectId", p."goalId", u.depth + 1, u.path || p.id
  FROM "WorkItem" p JOIN item_up u ON p.id = u."parentId"
  WHERE p."tenantId" = $1 AND p."deletedAt" IS NULL AND u.depth < 5 AND NOT p.id = ANY(u.path)
),
goal_up AS (                                   -- through the project's initiative
  SELECT g.id, g."parentId", 0 AS depth, ARRAY[g.id] AS path
  FROM item_up u
  JOIN "Project" pr    ON pr.id = u."projectId" AND pr."tenantId" = $1 AND u.depth = 0
  JOIN "Initiative" i  ON i.id = pr."initiativeId" AND i."tenantId" = $1 AND i."deletedAt" IS NULL
  JOIN "Goal" g        ON g.id = i."goalId" AND g."tenantId" = $1 AND g."deletedAt" IS NULL
  UNION ALL
  SELECT g.id, g."parentId", gu.depth + 1, gu.path || g.id
  FROM "Goal" g JOIN goal_up gu ON g.id = gu."parentId"
  WHERE g."tenantId" = $1 AND g."deletedAt" IS NULL AND gu.depth < 3 AND NOT g.id = ANY(gu.path)
),
direct_goal_up AS (                            -- the item's own goal (decision 211)
  SELECT g.id, g."parentId", 0 AS depth, ARRAY[g.id] AS path
  FROM item_up u JOIN "Goal" g ON g.id = u."goalId" AND g."tenantId" = $1 AND g."deletedAt" IS NULL AND u.depth = 0
  UNION ALL
  SELECT g.id, g."parentId", d.depth + 1, d.path || g.id
  FROM "Goal" g JOIN direct_goal_up d ON g.id = d."parentId"
  WHERE g."tenantId" = $1 AND g."deletedAt" IS NULL AND d.depth < 3 AND NOT g.id = ANY(d.path)
)
SELECT 'item' AS kind, … FROM item_up …
UNION ALL SELECT 'project', … UNION ALL SELECT 'initiative', … UNION ALL SELECT 'goal', … FROM goal_up …
UNION ALL SELECT 'direct_goal', … FROM direct_goal_up …
```

- **Path guards** stop on a repeat even if a cycle ever slipped past the lock (Paperclip's shape); the walk is at most 6 item rows, the project, the initiative and twice 3 goal rows.
- **Only the item's own `goalId`** starts the third term: a parent's direct goal is not the sub-item's (nothing inherited).
- **One round trip**: `WorkItemRepository.ancestry(db, ctx, visible, id)` (`$queryRaw`, bound parameters, the plan checked by H2's registry), returning `{ item, parents: root-first, project, initiative | null, goals: root-first, directGoals: root-first | null }`, each with id, key or title, status and archived flag; never a description.
- **Where it shows** (decision 220): a breadcrumb on the detail (page and sheet) above the title, "Goal › Initiative › Project › parents"; when the item names a goal directly that is not already one of the path's goals, a second line, "Also serves: Goal › Goal". `GET /api/t/:slug/work-items/:id/ancestry` (`requirePermission('work_items.view')`; 404 when the item is missing or invisible). The list's two filters `initiativeId` and `goalId`, in `WORK_ITEM_FILTER_KEYS`, the saved-view `FilterKey` and `LIST_FILTER_KEYS`, resolved in `resolveListFiltersInTx` by one bounded read each: `initiativeId` to a project-id set; `goalId` to the goal's live subtree and the projects under it, matching an item whose project is in that set **or** whose own `goalId` is in that subtree (`OR` on `projectId` and `goalId`). The list, board, timeline and (3.3) calendar share them.
- **A goal the caller may not read** (no `goals.view`, possible only for a custom role) is left out of the breadcrumb and counted ("1 level hidden"), never named.
- **Tests**: hand-written vectors (no initiative, no goal, three goal levels each way, five item levels, an archived goal, a deleted initiative, a deleted direct goal, a hidden goal, a direct goal equal to the path's goal); a property test over random trees that the walk returns exactly what a naive in-memory walk returns; the statement count (one); the plan uses the primary keys and `[tenantId, parentId]`.

## Progress and health (3.2b)

### Progress, derived (decision 212)

- **One pure function**, `domain/progress.ts` (`progressOf`, `isLate`, moved from `usecases/milestone.ts`, which then imports it): from `(stateCategory, count)` rows to `{ scope, done, started }`. **Scope** is every live item not cancelled; **done** the Done ones; **started** the Started ones. Milestones keep their meaning exactly.
- **An initiative** counts every live (`deletedAt IS NULL`), visible (`projectVisibilitySql`), non-cancelled item of every live project linked to it, **archived projects included** (an initiative's finished projects are usually archived, and leaving them out would make progress fall as work completes); deleted projects excluded; sub-items count as items. An item that names a goal directly does not count towards an initiative for that.
- **A goal** counts the same items for every live initiative of the goal and of every live goal below it, **plus every such item whose own `goalId` is the goal or a live goal below it** (decision 211). Each item is counted once: one grouped count over `WorkItem` with `projectId IN (…) OR goalId IN (…)`, so an item reached both ways is one row.
- **The reads**: `WorkItemRepository.countsByProjects(db, ctx, visible, projectIds)` for an initiative and `countsForGoal(db, ctx, visible, projectIds, goalIds)` for a goal, over the two progress indexes; the project and goal ids from one bounded read down the tree (depth ≤ 3; at most 1,000 projects per goal, refused above with the count, never cut).
- **"Late"**: a due date passed (today in the workspace calendar's zone, as 3.1) with `scope > done`, as a milestone's.
- **Zero is never complete**: a scope of 0 shows "No items", never "100 %"; a goal with no initiatives and no direct items says so.
- **Not stored, not cached, no job**: computed on every read of a goal, an initiative, the lists and the roadmap. 3.4's estimates may later weight progress by points; that is a later decision, recorded in `docs/goals.md`.

### Health, set by a person (decisions 205, 206, 207, 214)

- `PUT /api/t/:slug/goals/:id/health` and `PUT /api/t/:slug/initiatives/:id/health`, body `{ health: 'ON_TRACK' | 'AT_RISK' | 'OFF_TRACK' | 'COMPLETED' | null }`, `requirePermission('goals.manage')`, `If-Match` optional; usecases `setGoalHealth` / `setInitiativeHealth`.
- **A person only** (`assertActingAsPerson`): an API key (5.2), an agent session (6.3) and a job are refused, 403 audited. An agent may explain a goal's state in 7.4's digest, never set it, and no proposal kind sets health.
- **Never computed and never tied to the status**: progress, "late", the items' states and the goal's own status never change health, and health never changes them (the rule recorded for decision 205). A goal 100 % done with health "Off track", or Completed with health "At risk", shows both.
- **Recorded on the row** (`health`, `healthSetAt`, `healthSetByUserId`) and audited `GOAL_HEALTH_SET` / `INITIATIVE_HEALTH_SET` with the old and new values; the page's "Health history" reads those audit entries, newest first, bounded. **No written update** (decision 207) and no notification in 3.2.
- **Archived or cancelled** goals and initiatives keep their last health, shown greyed; setting health on an archived one is 409 `ARCHIVED`, on a deleted one 404.

## The roadmap (3.2c)

### Where it appears

`/t/<slug>/roadmap` (the Roadmap page under the Roadmap entry), and each initiative's page scoped to it. It is **not** a `ViewLayout` value: its rows are initiatives and projects, not work items, so it is a page with its own read rather than a saved view of items.

### Rows and bars (decision 215)

A bar is **derived, never stored**: no start date is added to any project, initiative or goal.

| Row | Bar | Marks |
|---|---|---|
| Goal (an optional grouping header, by `?groupBy=goal`) | the span of its initiatives' bars and of its direct items' dates | its due date as a diamond |
| Initiative (a summary row) | the span of its projects' bars | its due date as a diamond; health and progress in the grid columns |
| Project | the scheduling engine's span of its live, non-cancelled items' start and due dates | the project's due date and each milestone's due date as diamonds; "Late" when its due date has passed with open items, or its latest open item is due after it, or a milestone is late |
| "No initiative" (a summary row) | its projects, as above | — |
| A goal, initiative or project with no dated item | no bar; listed in the "No dates" list beside the chart (3.1b's pattern) | its due date diamond if it has one |

- **The engine's span, over stored dates** (the reading recorded for decision 215). A project's bar runs from the earliest to the latest day of its items' dates **as the engine reads an item's dates** (decision 185: an item with only a due date is one working day ending on it, only a start date one working day from it), on the project's team's working calendar, through the engine's summary span (the rule 3.1b draws a summary bar with; if the engine has no separately callable span function, 3.2c extracts `spanOf` into `src/app-layer/domain/schedule/` so there is still one implementation). The engine's forward and backward pass is not run on the roadmap: no "today" rule, no done items moved to their actual dates, no dependencies, no forecast. The roadmap shows plans, labelled "Planned dates", and each bar opens the timeline where the pass runs (decision 217).
- **Stored due dates are diamonds** (decision 210): goals', initiatives', projects' and milestones'. A goal's or an initiative's due date is not an engine deadline (ADR 0010 §3 is unchanged); a project finishing after its initiative's or goal's due date is flagged ("Finishes after the initiative's due date") and nothing else.

### The read

`GET /api/t/:slug/roadmap?goalId=&initiativeId=&teamId=&leadUserId=&health=&status=&archived=&groupBy=` (`requirePermission('goals.view')`; the usecase asserts `projects.view`), usecase `getRoadmap` in `src/app-layer/usecases/roadmap.ts`:

1. The live initiatives and goals in scope (bounded reads; archived only when asked; deleted never).
2. The visible, live projects in scope (`visibleProjects`), at most **500**; above it 422 `ROADMAP_TOO_LARGE` with the count ("Narrow the filter: 612 projects"), never a cut-off list.
3. One aggregate over `WorkItem` grouped by project: the four boundary dates the span needs (the earliest start, the earliest due date of an item with no start, the latest due date, the latest start of an item with no due date) and the counts by category; and, when grouped by goal, the same grouped by `goalId` for the direct items.
4. The milestones of those projects (at most 2,000, refused above with the count) with their progress and "late" (2.5b's read).
5. The working calendars of the projects' teams (`resolveWorkingCalendars`) and today in the workspace calendar's zone (3.1); the spans computed through the engine.

No description, comment or custom-field value is read. Every raw read carries `tenantId`, `deletedAt` and `projectVisibilitySql`; H2's plan registry gains the aggregate. Budget on H2's reference seed (500 projects, 100,000 items): p50 300 ms, p95 700 ms, at most 7 statements, response ≤ 400 KB uncompressed.

### The client (decisions 216, 217)

- **The chart**: SVAR React Gantt 2.8.3 through **the same adapter file** (`src/components/work-items/timeline/svar-adapter.tsx`; `tests/guards/svar-core-only.test.ts` allows SVAR imports there only), which gains a second row model, `roadmap-model.ts` (initiatives and goals as `summary` tasks, projects as tasks, due dates as `milestone` tasks), loaded with `next/dynamic` and `ssr: false`. `fonts={false}`, no PRO option, `readonly` set and every editing action intercepted to `false`: **the roadmap itself never changes a date** (decision 217).

The scope parameter is therefore the list filter of decision 220, which the timeline route already reads through `resolveListFiltersInTx`: no new timeline route or parameter. There dates change through 3.1c's "Change dates" confirmation, under every check it has, and the timeline's 5,000-item refusal applies with its count.
- **The accessible equivalent** (decision 203's rule, applied again): "Chart / Table", the table showing goal, initiative, project, start, finish, due date, progress, health, late flags and "Open timeline", with the same filters.
- **Today line, shading and the "No dates" list** reuse 3.1b's code; non-working days are not shaded at month and quarter zoom. Zoom by month and quarter (default quarter), stored per viewer in `localStorage` (a convenience, wrapped in try/catch).

## Agents, later (ADR 0003)

- **Reading** (6.2): read tools `get_goal`, `get_initiative`, `get_roadmap` and `get_work_item_ancestry` serve the same usecases under the agent's grants and the starter's permissions; an agent asked "why does this item exist" answers from the ancestry, citing the goal and initiative ids and the item's direct goal.

## What makes the prompt harder than it reads

- **"Every work item can answer its ancestry in one query"** stays one statement because each link is single-parent. With decision 211 an item can serve two goals, one through its project and one of its own; the statement returns both paths, and the breadcrumb shows the second only when it differs.
- **"A portfolio page per initiative"** uses a word ADR 0006 bans twice. The page is built; the word is not.
- **"Progress rolled up from child items"** must not count an item twice, must not drop finished work, and must not call an empty goal complete: one grouped count over the union of both paths, archived projects included, and "No items" answer the three.
- **"A roadmap view across projects"** cannot run the engine's pass at workspace scale without breaking 3.1's 5,000-item bound. The bars are the engine's span over stored dates, honestly labelled, and every bar opens the timeline where the pass runs.
- **"A health status set by a person"** rules out computing it, rules out agents setting it, and, with "Completed" as a health, rules out tying it to the status: it is a judgement beside the facts.

## Rules no decision names (3.2a, as built)

Where building 3.2a needed a rule no decision states, the choice and its reason; each is also in `docs/goals.md`. None contradicts an ADR.

1. **The ancestry's visibility.** The map's statement names `projectVisibilitySql`, which the search functions' fix (decision 245, #84) replaced with `projectVisibilityArgs` for the definer functions. The ancestry is not a definer function, so its anchor carries the visibility inline: a join to the item's live project and `(all OR projectId = ANY(granted))` as bind parameters, beside `tenantId` and `deletedAt` on every term.
2. **The goal chains' bound.** "At most 3" goal levels is read as three rows per chain (`depth < 2` from the nearest goal), and the item chain as six rows (`depth < 5`), the map's "at most 6 item rows … twice 3 goal rows".
3. **Hidden levels.** Without `goals.view` the initiative is withheld and counted too, not only the goals (`hiddenLevels`): an initiative is a goals-domain record.
4. **New links to an archived goal** include a new child goal and a re-parent under it (409 `GOAL_ARCHIVED`), as "it cannot be named by a new link" says; a goal already under an archived parent stays ("archive hides, it does not cut").
5. **A goal delete's group** holds the goal, its live descendants and the live initiatives of those goals; a goal or initiative already deleted keeps its own group. A group is at most 2,000 goals (409 `GOAL_TREE_TOO_LARGE` above), read one past the cap. An initiative deleted on its own restores only while its goal is live (`PARENT_DELETED`); one deleted with its goal answers `DELETED_WITH_PARENT` naming the group's root goal.
6. **A project's initiative on an archived project** is allowed: an initiative's finished projects are usually archived (the reason given for decision 212), and the project PATCH does not refuse archived projects either.
7. **The list filters ask `goals.view`** (403 otherwise), take comma-joined ids, answer 404 for a deleted or unknown id, and refuse a goal scope reaching more than 1,000 projects (422 `GOAL_SCOPE_TOO_LARGE` with the count, never cut), the cap 3.2b's progress uses; a goal subtree is read up to 2,000 goals.
8. **A repeat copy's goal** is skipped as `goal:unreadable` when the copy's owner may not read goals (the copy is made as them, and naming a goal asks `goals.view`), and as `goal:deleted` for a deleted or purged goal.
9. **Audit shapes.** `WORK_ITEM_GOAL_CHANGED` is `entity_lifecycle` naming `goalId`, the old and new ids in its metadata; `GOALS_PURGED` is `data_lifecycle` / `purged`, the counts-only shape `WORK_ITEM_PURGED` already uses; a lead cleared on member removal writes one `GOAL_UPDATED` or `INITIATIVE_UPDATED` per row (deleted rows included), with `reason: 'member_removed'`.
10. **The purge's pages** are 200 rows, initiatives first, each page in its own transaction under the goal-tree lock; the counts include deleted projects and items whose link the database clears; the sweep skips deleted workspaces. `GOAL_PURGE_DAYS` empty (as the compose file passes it unset) is the default 30.
11. **Lists without progress.** `GET /goals` and `GET /initiatives` order by status, due date and title in memory over at most 2,000 rows, filter health with `none` for "No health set", and carry no progress or "late" until 3.2b.
12. **The search-vector seam guard** is narrowed to work-item writes: a goal's or an initiative's description is not a search source (ADR 0008 unchanged), and the guard's `updateVersioned` heuristic would otherwise count `GoalRepository.updateVersioned`.
13. **The project-path progress index waits for 3.2b.** The model lists `202612xx120300_work_item_progress_indexes` among 3.2a's migrations, while the split puts "the grouped counts over both paths and their indexes" in 3.2b. Measured on the 10k seed, `[tenantId, projectId, stateCategory]` moved three existing plan checks off their indexes (the list by key, the templates' open items, a purged project's 410 buffers against its 400), and no 3.2a read uses it, so 3.2a ships three migrations (`20261215120000` to `20261215120200`) and 3.2b adds the index with the counts that need it, re-tuning those three checks. `[tenantId, goalId, stateCategory]` is in 3.2a: it is the item's goal key's index and serves the `goalId` filter.
14. **The pickers.** The detail's Goal picker lists the view configuration's live, unarchived goals (nested by their parents), keeps an item's archived goal shown as "a goal no longer offered", and is disabled without `work_items.edit` or `goals.view`; the create dialog offers it when there are goals and the field set does not hide it; the intake accept dialog only where the type requires it, as the milestone. Settings → Projects shows "an initiative no longer offered" for a project whose initiative is archived or deleted.

## Open questions for the owner (all decided, 2026-11-21)

Each question stands alone. The recommended option is first and marked; each option carries its main trade-off. Each carries the owner's decision.

**Words and states**

1. **What are the health values a person can set on a goal or an initiative?**
   - A (recommended). On track, At risk, Off track, plus "No health set" when nobody has set one. Three plain words most planning tools use; no colour names, so it reads the same in both themes and to screen readers.
   - B. Four values: add "Completed" as a health. Lets a finished goal show green; but it repeats the lifecycle status and a person could set a health that contradicts it.
   - C. A traffic light (Red, Amber, Green). Familiar to some teams; it names colours, which says nothing to a colour-blind reader or a screen reader, and ADR 0006 would need the colour words added.
   - **Decided:** **B, a departure from the recommendation** (decision 205, 2026-11-21): On track, At risk, Off track, Completed, plus "No health set". Reconciled with the lifecycle by one rule: health is an independent person's judgement, never derived from the status and never setting it; no combination is refused, and both are shown with their names ("Status, health and archive" above).

2. **Which records carry a health?**
   - A (recommended). Both goals and initiatives. The initiative's page (the roadmap's "portfolio page") is where most status conversations happen, and it needs its own health.
   - B. Goals only, as ADR 0002's sketch drew it. Smaller; initiatives would show progress but no human judgement.
   - C. Goals, initiatives and projects. Covers project status too; adds a column and a control to every project surface, beyond what the roadmap asks.
   - **Decided:** A, goals and initiatives (decision 206, 2026-11-21).

3. **When a person changes a health, do they also write why?**
   - A (recommended). No: the health, who set it and when are stored on the record, and the history is read from the audit entries (values only). Small, and nothing new to encrypt or moderate.
   - B. Yes: each change carries a short written update, kept as a history (encrypted, sanitised, a new table). Gives context ("vendor slipped two weeks"); adds a model, a word and a text that must be protected like a comment.
   - C. An optional line of text on the record itself, replaced at each change. Some context without a history table; earlier explanations are lost.
   - **Decided:** A, no written update; the history comes from the audit trail (decision 207, 2026-11-21).

4. **What lifecycle do goals and initiatives have?**
   - A (recommended). Both: Planned, Started, Completed, Cancelled. One set of words for both, and it avoids "Active", which ADR 0006 reserves for agents.
   - B. No lifecycle: only archive and restore, with health and progress telling the story. Simplest; a finished goal is indistinguishable from an abandoned one except by archiving.
   - C. Goals: Planned, Started, Achieved, Missed, Cancelled; initiatives: Planned, Started, Completed, Cancelled. Says whether a goal was met; two vocabularies to learn, and "Missed" overlaps the derived "Late".
   - **Decided:** A, Planned, Started, Completed, Cancelled for both (decision 208, 2026-11-21).

5. **Does a goal or an initiative name one accountable person, and what is that person called?**
   - A (recommended). Yes, optionally: the "Lead", one active member (Viewers included). "Owner" and "Responsible" are banned for this (ADR 0006), and a lead never becomes accountable for any work item.
   - B. No person field. Avoids another people column to clear when members leave; nobody is named as answering for a goal.
   - C. Yes, called "Sponsor". Fits executive-sponsored initiatives; reads oddly on a team-level goal.
   - **Decided:** A, an optional Lead, one active member, Viewers included (decision 209, 2026-11-21).

**Structure**

6. **Do goals and initiatives have due dates, and do they push on the schedule?**
   - A (recommended). Both may have an optional date-only due date, shown on their pages and as diamonds on the roadmap, flagged when a project finishes after it, but not a deadline for the scheduling engine. Useful dates with no change to 3.1's engine.
   - B. Goals only, as ADR 0002's sketch had (renamed from "target" to "due date"). Smaller; initiatives get their dates only from their projects.
   - C. Both, and an initiative's due date becomes an engine deadline for every item of its projects. Item timelines would show "Misses the initiative's due date"; amends ADR 0010 §3 and widens the timeline read.
   - **Decided:** A, optional due dates on both, not engine deadlines (decision 210, 2026-11-21).

7. **May a project link straight to a goal when it belongs to no initiative?**
   - A (recommended). No: the chain stays goal → initiative → project as ADR 0002 decided; a team that wants a project under a goal makes an initiative for it. One path, no exceptions.
   - B. Yes: a project may name a goal or an initiative, never both (a CHECK), so small workspaces skip the middle level. Ancestry stays single; ADR 0002 §2 is amended and every reader handles two shapes.
   - C. Yes, and work items may name a goal directly too, as Paperclip does. Most flexible; an item could then claim a goal its project does not serve, and "why" gets two answers.
   - **Decided:** **"Items may name a goal directly too", a departure from the recommendation** (decision 211, 2026-11-21). The hierarchy stays goal → initiative → project and a project never names a goal directly; in addition a work item may name one goal directly (`WorkItem.goalId`, nullable), which may be a goal its project's initiative does not serve. Its consequences are under "The item's direct goal": a work-item field under `work_items.edit` (and `goals.view`), a configurable built-in field, kept on moves, copied by repeats, counted once per goal, matched by the `goalId` filter, shown as a second ancestry line, cleared by the purge, not recorded by the transition trigger. ADR 0002 §2 is amended.

8. **Which items count towards an initiative's or a goal's progress?**
   - A (recommended). Every live, visible, non-cancelled item of every project under it, archived projects included, sub-items counted like any item (as milestones count). Finished projects keep counting after they are archived, so progress never falls as work completes.
   - B. The same, but archived projects excluded (as cycle counts are). Progress shows only current work; archiving a finished project lowers both the done count and the scope.
   - C. Leaf items only (items with no live sub-items). Avoids counting a parent and its children separately; differs from how milestones count, so the same items could show two different percentages.
   - **Decided:** A, archived projects included (decision 212, 2026-11-21); with decision 211, a goal also counts the items naming it directly, each item once.

**Permissions**

9. **Who may create and change goals and initiatives?**
   - A (recommended). A new permission domain, `goals` (view, manage): Owners, Admins and Members manage, Viewers read, guests nothing; custom roles can grant or remove it. Mirrors the cycles domain (decision 72); adds a domain to roles, the custom-role editor and the permission matrix.
   - B. No new domain: read under `projects.view`, change under `projects.edit`. Cheapest; a custom role cannot let someone edit projects without editing goals, or the reverse.
   - C. Read under `projects.view`, change under `admin.manage` only. Keeps strategy with admins; members cannot record their own team's goals.
   - **Decided:** A, a new `goals` domain (decision 213, 2026-11-21).

10. **Who may set a goal's or an initiative's health?**
    - A (recommended). Anyone who may change goals (question 9), as a person: never an API key, a job or an agent. Matches "set by a person" and keeps health a human judgement.
    - B. Only its lead, or an admin when it has no lead. Clear accountability; a stale health waits on one person.
    - C. Anyone who can read goals. Most current; a Viewer could change a status that others plan around.
    - **Decided:** A, anyone who may change goals, as a person only (decision 214, 2026-11-21).

**The roadmap**

11. **Where do a project's bar dates on the roadmap come from?**
    - A (recommended). Stored dates: from its items' earliest start date to their latest due date, with the project's due date and its milestones as diamonds and "late" flags derived. Fast at workspace scale and never shows a number nobody computed; it shows plans, not forecasts.
    - B. The scheduling engine run per project in the request, showing each project's computed finish. The real forecast; heavy (each project up to 5,000 items), so the roadmap would refuse large scopes much earlier.
    - C. A new stored start date on each project, with the bar from it to the project's due date. Simple and editable; a second set of dates that drifts from the items' own.
    - **Decided:** **"The span of its projects' items", a departure from the recommendation** (decision 215, 2026-11-21): a bar is derived, never stored; a project's bar is the span of its items' start and due dates through the scheduling engine's span, an initiative's the span of its projects; stored due dates show as diamonds; a scope with no dated item goes in a "No dates" list. Read here as the engine's span over the items' stored dates with decision 185's one-date rule on the project's calendar, not the engine's pass ("Rows and bars").

12. **Which chart draws the roadmap?**
    - A (recommended). The timeline library already in use (SVAR React Gantt, MIT core), through the same single adapter, read-only. One look and one accessibility layer for both timelines; zoom and virtualisation come with it.
    - B. inflect-compliance's visx chart (`gantt-chart.tsx`), copied. Lighter for a few dozen rows; no zoom or virtualisation, and a second chart to theme and keep accessible.
    - C. No chart: a table with dates and progress bars. Cheapest and fully accessible; not a roadmap people can scan at a glance.
    - **Decided:** A, SVAR through 3.1's adapter (decision 216, 2026-11-21).

13. **Can dates be changed from the roadmap?**
    - A (recommended). No: the roadmap is read-only in 3.2; a project's due date is changed on the project, item dates on the timeline. Nothing new to confirm or undo.
    - B. Dragging a project's due-date diamond opens a confirmation and changes the project's due date. Handy for planners; a second date-editing surface with its own confirmation and tests.
    - **Decided:** **"Open the item timeline on click", a departure from the recommendation** (decision 217, 2026-11-21): the roadmap stays read-only, but a bar opens 3.1's timeline scoped to that initiative's or project's items, where dates change through 3.1c's flow. The scope is the list filter of decision 220 (`initiativeId`, `goalId`, or the project's own page), which the timeline route already reads ("The client").

**Lifecycle, pages and delivery**

14. **Can goals and initiatives be deleted?**
    - A (recommended). No: archive and restore only, as recipient lists. Every ancestry and audit entry keeps pointing at something real; clutter is hidden by archiving.
    - B. Yes, through the undo toast; children are unlinked (projects lose their initiative, initiatives their goal, child goals move up). Tidier lists; ancestry history changes silently for every item underneath.
    - C. Yes, but only an archived one with nothing linked under it. Allows cleaning up mistakes; one more rule to explain.
    - **Decided:** **"Soft delete with restore", a departure from the recommendation** (decision 218, 2026-11-21): goals and initiatives are soft-deleted (`deletedAt`, `deletionId`) and restored like projects; deleting a goal soft-deletes its child goals and its initiatives as one group; projects and items keep their links while deleted, the rows are hidden from every read and picker, and a restore brings the group and its links back. Archive stays, for Completed and Cancelled goals and initiatives ("Status, health, archive and delete").

15. **Where does a work item show its ancestry?**
    - A (recommended). A breadcrumb on the item's detail, an ancestry route for agents and integrations, and two list filters ("Initiative", "Goal") that the list, board, timeline and calendar share. Answers "why" where people look, and lets them slice work by goal.
    - B. The breadcrumb and the route only, no filters. Smaller; slicing work by goal has to go through projects.
    - C. All of A plus Initiative and Goal columns in the list. Most visible; two more columns to compute on every list page.
    - **Decided:** A, the breadcrumb, the ancestry route and the `goalId` and `initiativeId` filters (decision 220, 2026-11-21); `goalId` matches items through both paths (decision 211).

16. **What is the roadmap's "portfolio page per initiative" called?**
    - A (recommended). It is simply the initiative's page, titled with the initiative's name, under Goals → Initiatives. ADR 0006 bans "Portfolio" for both initiatives and the roadmap, so no new word is needed.
    - B. Amend ADR 0006 to allow "Portfolio" as this page's label. Matches the roadmap's wording; reintroduces a word the vocabulary deliberately banned, beside "Initiative" for the same thing.
    - **Decided:** A, the initiative's page (decision 221, 2026-11-21), under Roadmap → Initiatives (decision 222).

17. **How do people reach goals, initiatives and the roadmap?**
    - A (recommended). One sidebar entry, "Goals", whose page has three tabs: Goals, Initiatives, Roadmap. Keeps the sidebar short; the roadmap is one click deeper.
    - B. Three sidebar entries. Each is one click away; three more rows in a sidebar that already holds nine.
    - C. "Goals" in the sidebar, and the roadmap as a tab on the Projects page. Puts the roadmap beside the projects it draws; splits the planning pages across two places.
    - **Decided:** one sidebar entry with three pages, **named "Roadmap", not "Goals"**, a departure from the recommendation in its name (decision 222, 2026-11-21). The owner's words: "One roadmap entry, three pages - goals, initiatives, roadmap". Goals, Initiatives and Roadmap are each their own route under `/roadmap`; a goal's and an initiative's pages are subpages with canonical parents ("The pages and navigation").

18. **How is 3.2 delivered?**
    - A (recommended). Three pull requests: 3.2a the model, routes and ancestry; 3.2b progress, health and the goal and initiative pages; 3.2c the roadmap. Each reviewable alone, and the ancestry lands first for 6.2.
    - B. Two: the model with progress and pages, then the roadmap. Fewer reviews; the first is large.
    - C. One. Simplest to track; about 9,000 lines in one review.
    - **Decided:** A, three pull requests (decision 223, 2026-11-21), re-cut so the item's direct goal (decision 211) and soft delete and the purge (decisions 218 and 219) land in 3.2a ("Split").

19. **How are these decisions recorded in the ADRs?**
    - A (recommended). Amend ADR 0002 (§2 made concrete and a new §17 on goals, initiatives and progress) and ADR 0006 (the new words); no new ADR. The chain was already ADR 0002's decision.
    - B. A new ADR 0011 "Goals, initiatives and the roadmap", with ADR 0002 pointing to it. One place to read the whole design; splits the parent chain across two ADRs.
    - **Decided:** A, amend ADR 0002 and ADR 0006, no new ADR; 3.3 keeps ADR 0011 (decision 224, 2026-11-21). Applied.

20. **What happens to soft-deleted goals and initiatives in the end?** (asked with question 14's answer)
    - **Decided:** purged after 30 days (decision 219, 2026-11-21): `GOAL_PURGE_DAYS`, default 30, and a nightly purge job; when a row is purged, links on projects and items are cleared with `SET NULL` (and so are an initiative's goal and a child goal's parent).
