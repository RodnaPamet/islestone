## Summary

- **Nothing is copied.** inflect-compliance has no cycles in this sense (its "audit cycles" are an audit-readiness period over a framework), no milestones, no work-item templates (its templates are compliance catalogues installed into controls, policies and risks) and no recurring tasks (its recurrence is a cron schedule on a control test plan). What it has, and what 2.5 takes as patterns, is its scheduling discipline: the conditional claim on a stamped next-run date, the deterministic BullMQ job id, the bootstrap-first scan order, the deleted-workspace skip (`src/app-layer/jobs/control-test-scheduler.ts`), the one-home month-end clamp (`src/app-layer/utils/cadence.ts`), and the "one projection from template to item" lesson (`src/app-layer/usecases/control/task-from-template.ts`). Kaneo's recurrence (MIT) gives the rule shape and the next-date algorithm, pattern only. Everything is written fresh.
- **Names follow ADR 0006.** The roadmap's "`Module` or milestone" is a **Milestone** (ADR 0006 bans "Module" and says so in its own "Consequences"; open question 1 asks the owner to confirm, because ADR 0006 is still Proposed). Templates are `WorkItemTemplate` and `ProjectTemplate` (ADR 0006's Template row). Four words are new and need ADR 0006 rows (open question 13): cycle schedule, repeat, transition, burn-up. ADR 0006 bans "Target" in the schema (the Goal row), so the milestone's date is `dueDate` in the schema and "Due date" in the UI, as 2.1 renamed `WorkItemLink.target` to `location`.
- **Cycles** belong to a team and do not overlap. A team may turn on a **cycle schedule** (length in weeks, a first start date, how many upcoming cycles to keep), and a daily job keeps that many upcoming cycles created; a person may also create a one-off cycle or move a future cycle's dates (importers in 5.5 need arbitrary dates). An item has at most one cycle, of its own team (ADR 0002 §2 and §10).
- **Rollover** (proposed default): the night after a cycle's last day, the daily job closes it and moves its unfinished items (Backlog, Unstarted, Started) to the team's next cycle, creating it from the schedule if needed; a per-team switch sends them to no cycle instead. The job writes as the workspace system user (it acts for nobody). Open question 3.
- **Burn-up** (proposed default): a new append-only table, `WorkItemTransition`, written only by a database trigger whenever an item's state, cycle, milestone, project or deletion changes. The burn-up is computed from it by a pure, property-tested module, so the chart is exact from the first minute, never misses a night, and does not depend on the worker. 3.6's cumulative flow and throughput need the same record (the jira-auto-scheduler study asked for it "before 3.6"). Open questions 4 and 5.
- **Milestones** belong to a project (ADR 0002, ADR 0006): a name, a due date and a place in the project's order. Progress is derived from their items. An item has at most one milestone, of its own project.
- **Templates** (proposed default): item templates per project (title, description, type, priority, labels, assignee, due-in-days, custom-field values, sub-items), used from the create dialog; project templates per workspace (fields, milestones, item templates and starter items), used from the project create dialog and made from an existing project with "Save as template". Template descriptions and project-template content are encrypted under the workspace key. Open questions 10 to 12.
- **Recurrence** (proposed default): a "Repeat" rule on an item (every N days, weeks, months or years; chosen weekdays; month ends clamp; an optional end), generated by a daily job a few days before each due date, copying the item where the repeat was set. The job acts for the person who set the repeat and re-resolves them each time; if they left or lost permission, the series pauses and says why. Open questions 6 to 9.
- **Size.** About 6,000 lines of source and 3,000 of tests. That is more than the roadmap's M, so the map proposes four pull requests in order (open question 14): 2.5a cycles, transitions, burn-up and rollover; 2.5b milestones; 2.5c templates; 2.5d recurrence.

What the migrations add beyond Prisma's DDL, each with a catalogue test (in the style of `tests/integration/custom-fields-and-views.test.ts`):

- **No overlapping cycles in a team.** Proposed: an exclusion constraint, `EXCLUDE USING gist ("tenantId" WITH =, "teamId" WITH =, daterange("startsOn", "endsOn", '[]') WITH &&)`, which needs `CREATE EXTENSION btree_gist` (contrib, present in the `pgvector/pgvector:0.8.7-pg17-trixie` image used locally and in production; migrations run as the superuser). The usecases also check under the team's advisory lock (`cycle-team:<teamId>`) so a refusal is a clear 409, not a database error. If the extension breaks `npm run db:check-schema-drift`, the lock-and-check stays and the constraint is dropped, recorded in the pull request.
- `CHECK ("endsOn" >= "startsOn")`; `CHECK` ranges on `lengthWeeks`, `upcomingCount`, `interval`, `leadDays`, `weekdays` (each element 0..6, and only for `WEEKLY`).
- `COLLATE "C"` on the three new `sortKey` columns (ADR 0002 §5).
- **The transition trigger.** `work_item_transition_record`, `AFTER INSERT OR UPDATE OF "stateId", "cycleId", "milestoneId", "projectId", "deletedAt" ON "WorkItem"`, with a `WHEN` clause so an update that changes none of them writes nothing. It inserts the row's state after the change. The function is `SECURITY DEFINER` with a pinned `search_path`, and `app_user` holds only `SELECT` on `WorkItemTransition`: the trigger is its single writer, held by privilege, and nothing can update or delete a row. Every row is a full picture of the five properties, so the state at any moment is the latest row at or before it, and no back-fill is needed (the first row of an existing item is written when it first changes).
- **The occurrence unique.** A partial unique index `("recurrenceId", "occurrenceOn") WHERE "recurrenceId" IS NOT NULL`: one item per occurrence, whatever retries or races do.
- `ON DELETE SET NULL ("recurrenceId")` on the item's rule link (Postgres 15+ column-list form; Prisma cannot declare it): removing a repeat leaves its earlier items in place.
- **Cycle and milestone references are RESTRICT, cleared by usecases.** A composite FK's plain `SET NULL` would also null `teamId` or `projectId`; the delete usecases clear the items first (bumping their versions, audited) and then delete.
- Row-level security (`tenant_isolation`, `tenant_isolation_insert`, `superuser_bypass`, FORCE) and the grants on every new table: `SELECT, INSERT, UPDATE, DELETE` for `CycleSchedule`, `Cycle`, `Milestone`, the two template tables and `WorkItemTemplateLabel`, `WorkItemRecurrence`; `SELECT` only for `WorkItemTransition`.

Every new table gets its two-tenant test in `tests/integration/work-tables-isolation.test.ts` and its `ISOLATION_TESTED` entry.

## The transitions and the burn-up

**Why a table** (proposed default, open question 4). The burn-up needs, for each day of a cycle, how many items were in it and how many were done at the end of that day. Islestone stores no state history (ADR 0002; the jira-auto-scheduler study flags it for 3.6). The options are a nightly count per cycle, a reconstruction from the audit trail, or a record of each change. The audit trail is a security record: its `detailsJson` would become an analytics format, every write path would have to carry the cycle and category in it, and a burn-up read would scan audit rows per item. A nightly count is simple but cannot be repaired for a night the worker missed. A trigger-written record is exact, cannot be bypassed by a write path that forgets it, and is what 3.6 needs for cumulative flow and for throughput that survives reopened items.

**The computation** is a pure module, `src/app-layer/domain/burn-up.ts`: given the transitions of every item that was ever in the cycle, the cycle's dates, "now" and the zone (`NOTIFICATIONS_TZ`), it returns one point per day from `startsOn` to `min(endsOn, today)`: `scope` (in the cycle, not deleted, not cancelled), `started` (Started or Done) and `completed` (Done). Today's point is "now"; earlier points are the end of that day. Cancelled items leave scope (open question 4). Property tests: the same transitions in any order give the same series; adding a transition after the cycle ended changes nothing; `completed <= started <= scope` on every day; an item that leaves and re-enters is counted only while in.

**The read** is two bounded queries: the distinct item ids with a transition naming the cycle (`[tenantId, cycleId, workItemId]`), then their transitions up to the cycle's end, plus each item's last one before its start (`[tenantId, workItemId, at]`). Only items whose transition's project is in `visibleProjects(ctx)` count, so a deleted project's items leave every chart, as they leave every metric (`docs/work-items.md`, "Project visibility"). A cycle with more than 5,000 items is drawn from the first 5,000 and says so (never silently truncated).

**Retention** (proposed default, open question 5): transitions carry no text and no person, only ids, a category and a time. They have no foreign key to `WorkItem`, so they outlive an item's 30-day purge and a closed cycle's chart never changes; they are removed with the workspace (8.3). Purging them with the item is the alternative.

**Estimates.** `WorkItem` has no estimate column (ADR 0002's sketch had one; 2.1 did not build it, and 3.4 adds "estimates in points or hours per team"). The burn-up counts items; 3.4 adds a points mode by reading the estimate into the same module.

## Jobs

Each job is a `JobPayloadMap` key with its `JOB_DEFAULTS` entry, one `executorRegistry.register` naming its payload fields, and a `SCHEDULED_JOBS` entry (`tests/guards/runtime-wiring-coverage.test.ts`, `executor-payload-forwarding.test.ts`). Both sweep every live workspace (`runWithoutRls({ reason: 'cross-tenant-sweep' })` for the list, then `runInTenantJobContext` per workspace, as `work-item-due-notification` does), skip deleted workspaces, page their reads, and are durably idempotent at the data layer, not through the BullMQ job id (`schedules.ts`, "Idempotency").

| Job | Schedule | Identity | What | Idempotency |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| `cycle-rollover` | `10 0 * * *` in `NOTIFICATIONS_TZ` | The workspace system user (`buildTenantSystemJobContext`): a scheduled consequence of the team's schedule, acting for nobody | Per team: (1) close each cycle whose `endsOn` is before today and `completedAt` is null; (2) move its unfinished items to the next cycle (or out of cycles, per the team's `rollover`); (3) create cycles from the schedule so `upcomingCount` future cycles exist | The close is a conditional `updateMany … WHERE completedAt IS NULL` that sets `completedAt`, in the same transaction as the move: a second run finds nothing to close. Cycle numbers and dates are deterministic from the schedule, `[tenantId, teamId, number]` is unique and overlaps are refused, so a second run creates nothing. |
| `work-item-recurrence` | `30 0 * * *` in `NOTIFICATIONS_TZ` (before the 08:00 reminders) | The person who set the repeat, **re-resolved** each run (`resolveMemberContext`): a job doing what a person asked. `null` pauses the rule (`OWNER_NOT_ACTIVE`); a context without `work_items.create`, or a project it cannot see, pauses it (`OWNER_NOT_PERMITTED`). Never a fallback to the system user (`CLAUDE.md`, "Job identity"; open question 9) | Per rule with `nextOccurrenceOn - leadDays <= today`, not paused: create the occurrence through the create usecase as that person, then advance `nextOccurrenceOn` (or pause `ENDED` at the end date or count) | The claim is an `updateMany` whose `WHERE` names the stamped `nextOccurrenceOn` (inflect's control-test-scheduler pattern), in the same transaction as the create; the occurrence unique is the backstop. A rule further behind than one occurrence (the worker was down) creates only the latest due occurrence and records how many it skipped (open question 7). |

**The rollover in detail.** One transaction per cycle, under the team's advisory lock: claim the cycle; find or create the next cycle (the first one starting after `endsOn`); then one `updateManyAndReturn` moves every item with `cycleId = closed`, an open category, `deletedAt IS NULL`, in a live and unarchived project, setting `cycleId` and `version = version + 1`. Items in an archived project and deleted items stay in the closed cycle (their writes are refused anyway). Each moved item gets a `WORK_ITEM_CYCLE_CHANGED` entry (`reason: 'rollover'`, from, to), written in pages of 500 with the transaction client; the cycle gets one `CYCLE_COMPLETED` (counts only: completed, rolled over, left behind). No bells and no mail (open question 3). The automation event (`emitAutomationEvent`, still the 5.1 stub) fires after the commit.

**When the next cycle cannot be made** (the team has no schedule and no later cycle exists), `NEXT_CYCLE` behaves as `NO_CYCLE` and the `CYCLE_COMPLETED` entry says so.

**The recurrence job in detail.** It calls the same create path a person's request takes (`createWorkItemInTx`, extracted from `createWorkItem` so the claim and the create share one transaction), with `recurrenceId`, `occurrenceOn` and the copied fields; the create's own side effects keep their order (`tests/unit/usecases/work-item-sequence-order.test.ts`). The source item is read as it is at that moment. A source that is deleted pauses the rule (`SOURCE_DELETED`); an archived project pauses it (`PROJECT_ARCHIVED`). Each pause writes `RECURRENCE_PAUSED` as the system user, naming the reason and `ownerUserId`, never naming a departed person as the actor.

**Concurrency (ADR 0002 §9).** Setting an item's cycle or milestone is a single-field change: `If-Match` optional, the version increases, a stale version is a 409 with the current row. The rollover and the cycle and milestone deletes bump every item they change. Cycle, milestone, template and schedule rows carry no `version`: their writes are small and last-writer-wins, audited. A recurrence rule change takes no `If-Match`.

**Who sees counts.** Cycle and milestone counts and the burn-up are computed over `visibleProjects(ctx)` only, so 8.4's guests will see the counts of the items they can see and no more.

## Usecase rules

- **Cycles.** The cycle's team is fixed. An item joins only a cycle of its own team (composite FK) that is not completed (409 `CYCLE_COMPLETED`); its sub-items are independent. Numbers are the team's next number under the team lock. Status is derived, never stored: upcoming (`startsOn` after today), current (today within the dates, `completedAt` null, or ended but not yet closed by the job), completed (`completedAt` set). "Today" is the date in `NOTIFICATIONS_TZ` until workspaces have their own time zone (2.4 open question 6 deferred that). Turning a schedule on creates the cycle containing today (if the team has none there) and the upcoming ones, in the person's request, audited `CYCLE_SCHEDULE_SET` and `CYCLE_CREATED`.
- **Milestones.** Progress is derived (closed of total, cancelled excluded from both, over visible items). "Late" is derived (a due date before today with open items). No stored status: a milestone with every item closed shows as complete. Deleting one clears its items (versions bumped, audited) and goes through the undo toast in the UI.
- **Moves** (ADR 0002 §10, already decided). Moving an item to a project of another team clears its cycle; moving to another project clears its milestone. `moveWorkItemToProject` sets both in the same `UPDATE`, so the composite FKs never see a stale pair; the transition trigger records the change.
- **Item templates.** A template's type, labels, custom-field values and sub-items are validated at save against the project as it is then, and again at use: an archived type is refused (`TEMPLATE_TYPE_ARCHIVED`), an archived field or option and a person who is no longer an active member are skipped and listed in the response; deleted labels have already left the template (the join cascades). The request body wins over the template for every field it names. `dueInDays` sets the due date from today. Removing a member clears their template assignee, as their items are unassigned (`removeTenantMember`).
- **Project templates.** Content is a versioned, Zod-validated document (`ProjectTemplateContentSchema`): custom fields with options, milestones with `dueInDays`, item templates, and starter items with sub-items, labels by name, due offsets and a milestone by index. Caps: 50 fields, 50 milestones, 50 item templates, 200 starter items, 50 sub-items each, 256 KB. Applying runs in `createProject`'s transaction: the project, its key prefix and sequence, then fields, options, milestones, item templates and items, numbered in order. Types and labels resolve by name against the workspace and the project's team; a name that resolves to nothing is reported, never created, and never silently dropped (the response and the `PROJECT_CREATED` entry list what was skipped). "Save as template" captures a project's fields, milestones, item templates and, if asked, its open items, with due dates turned into offsets from today.
- **Recurrence.** Setting a repeat needs a due date on the item (the series' first date); the dialog offers to set one. One rule per item. The rule's owner is whoever last set it. Changing the rule recomputes `nextOccurrenceOn` from `firstOn`; it never creates an occurrence in the request. Resuming a paused rule makes the caller its owner and skips occurrences that fell due while it was paused. A generated item is an ordinary item: editing it does not change the series, and it carries "Occurrence of <source key>" on its detail.
- **Sanitising.** Cycle names, milestone names, template names and titles are `sanitizePlainText`; template descriptions too, before encryption; project-template content is sanitised field by field before it is serialised and encrypted.
- **Member removal** (`removeTenantMember`) also clears template assignees. Recurrence rules owned by the member are not touched: the next run pauses them, which is the record of why.

## Audit, automation and notifications

- Audit actions, all through `logEvent` with the transaction client and a structured `detailsJson`: `CYCLE_CREATED`, `CYCLE_UPDATED`, `CYCLE_DELETED`, `CYCLE_COMPLETED`, `CYCLE_SCHEDULE_SET`, `CYCLE_SCHEDULE_REMOVED`; `WORK_ITEM_CYCLE_CHANGED`, `WORK_ITEM_MILESTONE_CHANGED` (both in the item's Activity, which reads `WORK_ITEM_*`); `MILESTONE_CREATED`, `MILESTONE_UPDATED`, `MILESTONE_DELETED`; `WORK_ITEM_TEMPLATE_CREATED`/`_UPDATED`/`_DELETED`; `PROJECT_TEMPLATE_CREATED`/`_UPDATED`/`_DELETED`; `RECURRENCE_SET`, `RECURRENCE_REMOVED`, `RECURRENCE_PAUSED`; `WORK_ITEM_CREATED` gains `templateId` or `recurrenceId` and `occurrenceOn`. No entry carries a description or template content (encrypted text never enters the trail).
- Automation (5.1 defines the contract; `emitAutomationEvent` stays a stub): `WORK_ITEM_CYCLE_CHANGED`, `WORK_ITEM_MILESTONE_CHANGED`, `CYCLE_COMPLETED`, emitted after the commit. 5.1's prompt names cycles in its event contract.
- Watcher bells ring for a cycle or milestone change a person made (material changes, `docs/work-items.md` "Notifications"), not for the rollover (open question 3). A generated occurrence rings the create path's own bells (assignment, due).

## Ordering and risks

1. **2.5a** schema for cycles and the transition trigger, the burn-up module, the cycle usecases, routes and the rollover job, the Cycles and cycle pages, the item's cycle picker, column, filter and lane. **2.5b** milestones end to end. **2.5c** templates (item, then project). **2.5d** recurrence and its job. Each is a pull request with its migration, tests and docs.
2. **Risk: the trigger is a second writer of meaning.** It writes only a derived record and is the single writer of that table; its catalogue test and a behaviour test hold it. A future column that should count (an estimate in 3.4) is added to its `UPDATE OF` list in the same migration.
3. **Risk: `btree_gist` and the drift check.** Prisma may report the extension as drift. Fallback in the constraint bullet above.
4. **Risk: rollover transaction size.** One `updateManyAndReturn` per cycle plus paged audit appends; a cycle of thousands of items holds the per-workspace audit chain lock for the duration. Bounded by H2's measurements; if it is too long, the audit entries move to `appendAuditEntryOrQueue` after the commit.
5. **Risk: the create path inside the recurrence claim.** `createWorkItem` must split into a transaction-taking core without changing its side-effect order; the sequence-order test holds it.
6. **Risk: counts across visibility.** Every count goes through `visibleProjects`; 8.4 must keep that true for guests.
7. **Risk: the chart's domain** (see "The chart").
8. **Later sub-phases that lean on this:** 3.1 (milestones and cycles on the timeline), 3.3 (cycles and milestones on the calendar), 3.4 (estimates into the burn-up and the trigger), 3.6 (cumulative flow, throughput and burn-up for any scope from `WorkItemTransition`), 5.1 (cycle events), 5.5 (importers map sprints to cycles with their own dates and epics or versions to milestones), 6.2 (MCP read tools for cycles), 7.1 (plans propose milestones), 8.2 (the template gallery and sample project build on project templates; ADR 0002's "templates should create [a project] by default" lands there), 8.3 (retention table entries for every new model).

## Open questions for the owner

Each question says what is being decided, the options, and the recommendation. "Recommended" is what the map applies if the owner accepts all.

1. **What do we call a scoped group of work with a date: "Milestone" or "Module"?** The roadmap says "`Module` or milestone". The vocabulary ADR (0006, not yet signed) already picks **Milestone** and bans "Module", and the work-item ADR (0002, signed) builds the item's `milestoneId` on it.
   - A. One thing called **Milestone**, belonging to one project, with a name and a due date. *Recommended.*
   - B. One thing called **Module**. Means amending ADRs 0002 and 0006.
   - C. Both: a Module (a group with no date) and a Milestone (a dated checkpoint). Two concepts for users to learn, and an item could be in both.

2. **How are cycles created?**
   - A. Each team can switch on a **cycle schedule** (cycle length 1 to 8 weeks, a first start date); Islestone keeps the next two cycles ready, and anyone allowed can still add a one-off cycle or move a future cycle's dates. Default length two weeks. *Recommended* (importers from Jira or Linear bring cycles with their own dates).
   - B. Only hand-made cycles, each with its own dates.
   - C. Only the schedule; no hand-made cycles and no date changes.

3. **What happens to unfinished items when a cycle ends?** "Unfinished" means in a Backlog, Unstarted or Started state; Done and Cancelled items stay where they are.
   - A. **Moved automatically** to the team's next cycle the night after the last day (just after midnight in the deployment's reminder time zone, `NOTIFICATIONS_TZ`, until workspaces have their own time zone), with a per-team switch to send them back to "no cycle" instead. Nobody is notified; each item's Activity shows the move. Items in archived projects and deleted items stay behind. *Recommended.*
   - B. Always removed from the cycle (back to the backlog), never moved on.
   - C. Nothing automatic: the cycle shows "ended, N unfinished" and a person chooses to move or remove them.
   - D. Moved to a Cancelled state. Not recommended: it closes work nobody decided to drop.
   - Also: should assignees get a notification when their item rolls over? Recommended: no (it would arrive for every unfinished item every cycle).

4. **Where do the burn-up chart's numbers come from, and what do they count?**
   - A. A **small record of each change**, written automatically by the database whenever an item's status, cycle, milestone, project or deletion changes. Exact from the first minute, cannot miss a night, and the same record serves the analytics and forecasting of roadmap 3.6. *Recommended.*
   - B. A **nightly count** per cycle. Simpler, but a night the background worker misses is lost, and 3.6 needs its own record later anyway.
   - C. **Rebuilt from the audit trail.** No new table, but it turns the security log into a reporting source and is slow on large workspaces.
   - What counts: recommended, items (not points, until estimates arrive in 3.4); cancelled items leave the scope; "completed" means a Done state.

5. **How long do we keep that change record?** It holds no text and no names, only ids, a status category and a time.
   - A. Keep it until the workspace is deleted, so a closed cycle's chart never changes, even after its deleted items are purged 30 days later. *Recommended.*
   - B. Delete an item's records when the item is purged. Charts of old cycles then lose those items.

6. **How is a repeat written?**
   - A. A **simple built-in rule**: every N days, weeks, months or years (N up to 99); weekly on chosen weekdays; monthly on the same day of the month (on short months, the last day: a series on the 31st lands on 28 or 29 February, then 31 March); an optional end date or number of times. *Recommended* (the same shape Kaneo uses, minus its time zone, which date-only due dates do not need).
   - B. Full **calendar rules** (iCalendar RRULE, through the `rrule` library): "the last Friday of every quarter" and the like. More power, more to explain, a new dependency.
   - C. **Cron expressions**. Precise but unfriendly for people.
   - Also: should monthly repeats offer "the second Tuesday" or "the last weekday"? Recommended: not in 2.5; add when asked.

7. **When is the next copy of a repeating item made?**
   - A. **On a schedule**: a nightly job makes each copy a set number of days before it is due (default 3, adjustable 0 to 30), whether or not the previous copy is done. If the job could not run for a while, it makes only the latest missed copy, not one for every missed day. *Recommended* (the roadmap says "generated by a job").
   - B. **On completion**: the next copy is made when the current one is marked Done (Kaneo's way). A copy never piles up, but nothing appears if nobody finishes the last one.
   - C. Either, chosen per repeat.

8. **What does each copy take, and from where?**
   - Source, A: the item where the repeat was set, as it is when the copy is made (edit it to change future copies). *Recommended.* B: the most recent copy (edits carry forward from copy to copy). C: a saved item template.
   - Taken: title, description, type, priority, labels, assignee (only if still an active member), custom-field values, milestone (if it still exists) and sub-items; the due date moves on, a start date keeps its distance from it, and the copy starts in the team's default state. Not taken: comments, watchers, relations, links, cycle. *Recommended.*

9. **Whose name is on a repeating item, and what if that person leaves?**
   - A. The person who set the repeat. Before each copy, Islestone checks again that they are still an active member allowed to create items in that project; if not, the repeat **pauses** and the item shows why, and anyone allowed can resume it, becoming its new owner. *Recommended* (this is the rule the codebase already applies to every job acting for a person).
   - B. The workspace's system user ("Islestone"). Repeats never pause when someone leaves, but items appear in nobody's name and keep coming after their creator has gone.

10. **What can templates hold, and who manages them?**
    - A. **Item templates belong to a project** (title, description, type, priority, labels, assignee, "due in N days", custom-field values, sub-items), managed by anyone who can edit the project (Members can). **Project templates belong to the workspace** and create, with the new project, its custom fields, its milestones (dates counted from creation), its item templates and a set of starter items with sub-items; they do not create a team or a workflow, and labels are matched by name. Admins manage project templates, made from an existing project with "Save as template"; anyone who can create projects can use them. *Recommended.*
    - B. Item templates workspace-wide (any project), managed by admins.
    - C. Project templates hold settings only (fields, milestones, item templates), no starter items.
    - Also: editing a project template's contents in the browser (rather than saving a project again) waits for a later step. Recommended: yes, wait.

11. **Which permissions guard the new things?** The permission set already has `cycles.view` and `cycles.manage` (Members hold both); milestones and templates have no keys of their own.
    - Cycles and the team's cycle schedule: A. `cycles.manage` (Members can run their team's cycles). *Recommended.* B. The schedule under `admin.manage`, like the rest of a team's configuration (decision 52), cycles themselves under `cycles.manage`.
    - Milestones: A. `projects.edit` (a milestone is part of its project, as custom fields are). *Recommended.* B. `cycles.manage` (planning). C. New `milestones.view`/`milestones.manage` keys (a change to the permission set and to every custom role).
    - Templates: A. As in question 10 (`projects.edit` for item templates, `admin.manage` to manage and `projects.create` to use project templates). *Recommended.* B. A new `templates.manage` key.
    - Repeats: setting one needs `work_items.create` (it creates items in your name) and `work_items.edit` on the item. *Recommended.*

12. **What is encrypted?** Work-item descriptions are encrypted today; titles are not (they are searched and shown in notifications).
    - A. Encrypt template descriptions and the whole content of project templates (which holds descriptions) under the workspace's key; keep template names, titles, cycle names and milestone names in plain text, like item titles. *Recommended.*
    - B. Encrypt nothing new (template descriptions would then be the only readable copy of description-like text).

13. **May we add these words to the vocabulary ADR (0006)?** "Cycle schedule" (a team's rule for making cycles), "Repeat" (the rule on an item; never "recurring task"), "Transition" (the change record of question 4; not shown to users), and "Burn-up". Also: the milestone's date is called "Due date" because the ADR bans "Target". *Recommended:* yes to all.

14. **One pull request or four?** 2.5 is larger than the roadmap's estimate.
    - A. Four, in order: cycles with the burn-up and rollover; milestones; templates; repeats. Each is usable on its own. *Recommended.*
    - B. One.
