## Summary

- **Three things, all written fresh.** A member's **capacity** (hours a week, the workspace's default unless set for them, from a Monday; decision 284), a member's **time off** (hours off, or the whole day, on each date, with a type; decisions 286 and 287), and a **Workload** page: one row per person, one column per week, the hours of their assigned open work placed in each week against the hours they can work, and the weeks where the first is larger flagged "Over capacity" (decisions 296 and 297).
- **Nothing ports from inflect-compliance**: it has no capacity, time off, absence, workload or allocation (its HRIS sync tracks employment status, not absence). **Cal.diy** (MIT, a roadmap port candidate) is read only through its study, **pattern only** (decision 301): capacity as weekly hours over working weekdays, holidays and time off replacing a day's hours (study §4.3), and a per-period duration rule turned into a report, never a block (§4.6). No AGPL or GPL product was read (Worklenz and OpenProject have workload views; neither was opened).
- **Capacity meets 3.1's working calendars**, but a member has no team (`Team` has no members at `15a9abb`), so each person counts on the workspace's calendar unless their capacity names a team's calendar (decision 285). Weekly hours are shared over that calendar's working weekdays; a holiday removes its day's share; time off removes its hours. Every working-day count goes through `domain/schedule/calendar.ts` (ADR 0010 §1: one engine, never a second count).
- **Time off is a grid of dates** (decision 286): one row per person and date, holding the hours off or "whole day", entered as a range and stored date by date. **Each date has a type**, Vacation, Sick or Other (decisions 287 and 303): "Sick" is health data, so the type is stored encrypted, padded to one length, never audited, logged, indexed, searched or notified, and absent from every read but the person's own and a workload manager's. Time off is **kept for the workspace's life** (decision 289), needs no approval (decision 288), shapes no schedule (decision 290, ADR 0010 unchanged) and reaches no external calendar (decision 291, ADR 0011 unchanged).
- **Allocation is computed on every read, never stored** (principle 4): each counted item's remaining hours (its hours estimate less the time spent on it, at least zero) spread over its remaining working days on **the item's own** calendar, from the engine's `plannedSpanOf`, then summed by person and ISO week (decisions 292, 293, 295). Points are never converted (ADR 0012; decision 294): a person's points work is named beside the hours. Undated and unestimated work is counted and named, never guessed into a week.
- **"Read-only suggestions" are code, not proposals**, and "Suggestion" is a banned word (ADR 0006's Proposal, Change dates and Intake request rows; the schema guard refuses it in every Prisma identifier): an over-capacity week opens "Ways to rebalance" (decision 283): the items that fill it and options computed by a pure module ("Assign to Ana: 14h free that week", "Move ISL-12's dates to 14 Dec"), each opening the existing assign picker or 3.1c's "Change dates" confirmation. Nothing is written until a person confirms there; no agent, no levelling (decision 299).
- **A new `workload` permission domain** (decision 298): `view`, `time_off`, `manage`. Only a signed-in person writes capacity or time off; never an agent, an API key or a job.
- **Three pull requests** (decision 302): 3.5a capacity and time off, 3.5b allocation and the Workload view, 3.5c the ways to rebalance. About 5,200 lines of source and 4,800 of tests. ADR 0013 "Workload and capacity" records the rules (decision 282).

## What makes the prompt harder than it reads

- **"Hours per week"** has to meet 3.1's working calendars and holidays, which belong to the workspace and to teams, while a person belongs to no team. A person's capacity names a calendar, or counts on the workspace's (decision 285).
- **"Time off"** is a word ADR 0006 already reserves for 3.5 (the Holiday row), and with a type the product records why a person is away: "Sick" is health data, a special category of personal data (decision 287). The type is the one field in Islestone that must be kept from colleagues who can see everything else about the same row.
- **"Allocation"** needs hours. Points teams have none, and ADR 0012 and decision 257 forbid converting them; recorded time is visible as a person's weekly total only to `time.approve` (decision 266), so the view cannot show "time spent this week" for everyone (decision 295).
- **"Read-only suggestions"** cannot be called suggestions, and cannot be proposals, which are agents' (ADR 0003, ADR 0006's Proposal row). They are numbers computed in code and opened by a person into confirmations that already exist.
- **"Per person per week"** across 200 people and 26 weeks means bounded reads and integer arithmetic that sums exactly: an estimate of 7 hours spread over 3 days must place 420 minutes, not 419 or 421.

## Capacity (3.5a; decisions 284 and 285)

### The model

```prisma
/// One row per workspace; no row means the defaults (roadmap 3.5a).
model TenantWorkloadSettings {
  tenantId             String   @id
  /// The capacity of a member with no MemberCapacity row in force: 0 to 6,000
  /// minutes (100 hours) a week (CHECK). 2,400 (40 hours) by default.
  defaultWeeklyMinutes Int      @default(2400)
  version              Int      @default(1)
  updatedAt            DateTime @updatedAt
}

/// A member's capacity from a Monday until the next row (decision 284).
model MemberCapacity {
  id              String   @id @default(cuid())
  tenantId        String
  /// FK (tenantId, userId) → TenantMembership(tenantId, userId), ON DELETE CASCADE:
  /// a removed member's capacity goes with the membership.
  userId          String
  /// A Monday (CHECK extract(isodow from "effectiveFrom") = 1), date-only.
  effectiveFrom   DateTime @db.Date
  /// 0 to 6,000 (CHECK). 0 means "works no hours" (a member who is never assigned work).
  weeklyMinutes   Int
  /// The team whose working calendar the person counts on (decision 285); NULL: the
  /// workspace's. FK (calendarTeamId, tenantId) → Team, ON DELETE SET NULL ("calendarTeamId").
  calendarTeamId  String?
  /// An actor stamp, no FK.
  createdByUserId String
  createdAt       DateTime @default(now())
  version         Int      @default(1)

  @@unique([tenantId, userId, effectiveFrom])
  @@unique([id, tenantId])
  @@index([tenantId, calendarTeamId])
}
```

- **The row in force for a week** is the person's latest row with `effectiveFrom` on or before that week's Monday; none, the workspace's default on the workspace's calendar. `effectiveFrom` is a Monday so a week has one capacity. Past rows are kept as history.
- **Which calendar** (decision 285): `calendarTeamId` names a team, and `resolveWorkingCalendars(...).forTeam(teamId)` gives that team's calendar, else the workspace's; a team deleted later clears the column (`SET NULL`) and the person falls back to the workspace's. A team without its own calendar already resolves to the workspace's, so naming it changes nothing until the team gets one.
- **Deactivated members** count zero capacity while deactivated (their rows are kept); their assigned open work then shows as over capacity, which is the point: it needs a new assignee (ADR 0003 §1: "A deactivated member stays assigned and is shown as inactive"). A GUEST membership has no row in the view before 8.4 (decision 204).
- **Tenant-table rules** for both tables: `tenantId`, a tenant-leading index (`TenantWorkloadSettings`' primary key is `tenantId`; `MemberCapacity` leads its unique with it), the four RLS policies with FORCE, `GRANT … TO app_user` in the creating migration (`SELECT, INSERT, UPDATE` on the settings, no DELETE; `SELECT, INSERT, UPDATE, DELETE` on capacity rows, since a future row may be removed), two-tenant tests in `ISOLATION_TESTED`, Layer B of `schema-index-coverage` (the `[tenantId, calendarTeamId]` index for the team FK; the membership FK is led by the unique), and a catalogue test for the CHECKs and the `SET NULL` column list.
- **A write names an active member.** As every insert of a row whose cascading key names the membership (CLAUDE.md, "A row that names a member"), a capacity or time-off write locks the person's membership `FOR KEY SHARE` (`lockActiveUserIds`) first and refuses a non-member with a 400; the membership's `CASCADE` means a removal never fails on these rows.

### Capacity in a week (decisions 285 and 286)

The pure, client-safe `src/app-layer/domain/workload/capacity.ts` (property-tested), over epoch days and `CompiledCalendar`:

1. **Day shares.** The week's minutes are shared over the calendar's working weekdays (`perWeek`): each gets `floor(W / perWeek)`, and the remainder goes one minute at a time to the earliest working weekdays, so the shares sum to `W` exactly. A 40-hour week on Monday to Friday is 480 minutes a day; 37.5 hours is 450.
2. **Holidays.** A holiday on the person's calendar keeps its share at zero (`isWorkingDay` is false). Its hours are lost, not moved to another day: a week with a holiday has less capacity.
3. **Time off, date by date** (decision 286). For each working day of the person's calendar that has a time-off row: a whole-day row removes the day's whole share; a row of `m` minutes removes `min(m, share)`. A row on a weekend, a non-working weekday or a holiday removes nothing more (no double counting). The type plays no part.
4. **Deactivated**: zero. **No capacity row**: the workspace's default on the workspace's calendar.

`capacityByWeek(person, weeks, calendars, timeOffDays)` returns, per ISO week, the capacity, the minutes time off removed and the dates that carry time off. It never reads a clock, a zone or a type.

## Time off (3.5a; decisions 286 to 289)

### The model

```prisma
/// One person's time off on one date (decision 286): hours off, or the whole day,
/// with a type (decision 287).
model TimeOff {
  id              String   @id @default(cuid())
  tenantId        String
  /// FK (tenantId, userId) → TenantMembership(tenantId, userId), ON DELETE CASCADE:
  /// a removed member's time off goes with the membership.
  userId          String
  /// Date-only.
  day             DateTime @db.Date
  /// NULL: the whole day, whatever the day's capacity share is. Otherwise the hours
  /// off, 1 to 1,439 minutes (CHECK).
  minutes         Int?
  /// VACATION, SICK or OTHER (decisions 287 and 303). ENCRYPTED under the workspace's data key
  /// (manifest); the code is padded to 8 characters before encryption (VACATION is exactly 8), so every
  /// ciphertext has the same length; NOT NULL; CHECK ("type" ~ '^v[12]:'), so no
  /// path stores it in plaintext. Read only for the person and holders of
  /// workload.manage.
  type            String
  /// Who wrote it: the person, or a holder of workload.manage. An actor stamp, no FK.
  createdByUserId String
  /// Optimistic concurrency for a date's change.
  version         Int      @default(1)
  createdAt       DateTime @default(now())
  updatedAt       DateTime @updatedAt

  @@unique([tenantId, userId, day])
  @@unique([id, tenantId])
}
```

- **One row per person and date** (decision 286), unique by `(tenantId, userId, day)`; no range and no exclusion constraint. A range typed in the browser ("from 7 to 18 December, whole days, Vacation") is stored date by date on the working days of the person's calendar, and each date may then be changed or removed on its own ("Friday 11th: 4h"). A date already holding time off is replaced, and the dialog names which.
- **A whole day is stored as such** (`minutes` NULL), not as the day's share at the time: a later change of the person's capacity or calendar keeps it a whole day.
- **Time off is not a holiday and not a time entry.** A holiday is a calendar's day for every item and person on it; time off is one person's, and moves no item's working days (decision 290). A time entry records work done (3.4); time off records work not available.
- **The type** (decision 287) is the only special-category field in Islestone. See "The type, held apart" below.
- **Kept for the workspace's life** (decision 289): no job deletes time off. The person, or a holder of `workload.manage`, changes or deletes any date, past or future, and the audit trail keeps that the date existed (its date and hours; never its type).
- **Member removal** (a rule, recorded with decision 289): the membership key cascades, so a removed member's capacity and time off are deleted in `removeTenantMember`'s own transaction, audited by the removal's entry. Planning data about someone who has left serves no reader, and the type may be health data, so it does not outlive the membership; this differs from time entries on purpose (decision 277 keeps a record of work done). A deactivated member keeps their rows.
- **Index**: the unique `[tenantId, userId, day]` serves every read (the people in scope, a window of dates) and leads with `tenantId`.
- **Tenant-table rules** as for `MemberCapacity`; `GRANT SELECT, INSERT, UPDATE, DELETE` (a deletion is for good), a two-tenant test, a catalogue test for the unique, the CHECKs and the cascade.

### The type, held apart (decision 287)

"Sick" is health data, a special category of personal data under the GDPR (general knowledge, not legal advice: Islestone processes it on the workspace's behalf, and the workspace decides whether to use the type). So the type is treated as the most sensitive value in the product:

- **Encrypted under the workspace's data key**, in place, as a manifest field (`ENCRYPTED_FIELDS.TimeOff = ['type']`), not as a Postgres enum, which would sit in plaintext in every dump. Both key sweeps rewrite it like any manifest field. `sanitize-rich-text-coverage` classifies `TimeOff` as a closed code, not free text: the usecase accepts only the three codes (`TimeOffTypeSchema`), so there is nothing to sanitise.
- **One length for every value.** AES-GCM ciphertext has its plaintext's length, so `SICK` would be told from `VACATION` by length alone. The repository pads the code to 8 characters before it reaches the encryption extension and trims it after decryption (`TIME_OFF_TYPE_WIDTH`); a unit test asserts the three ciphertexts have equal lengths. The type is required on every row, so its presence says nothing either.
- **Never in plaintext**: the CHECK `TimeOff_type_ciphertext` (`"type" ~ '^v[12]:'`) refuses a write that bypassed the extension.
- **Never audited, logged, indexed, searched, notified, exported or counted.** `TIME_OFF_SET` and `TIME_OFF_REMOVED` carry dates and hours only; the Prisma audit extension names `type` in an update's `undiffableFields` and never records its value (`buildDiffJson`'s rule for every encrypted field); no log line carries it; no index covers it (a random-IV ciphertext cannot be indexed usefully anyway); ADR 0008 is not amended, so no search source reads it; the bell names who and the dates; no metric has it as an attribute; no export exists in 3.5, and a later one (5.2, 7.4) treats it as the person's and workload managers' only.
- **Absent from every read for a caller who may not see it.** Who may is `mayReadTimeOffType(ctx, userId)` (`workload.policies.ts`): the person themselves, or a holder of `workload.manage`. The repository's workload read never selects `type`, so it is never decrypted there (`tests/unit/repositories/time-off-repository-predicates.test.ts` holds the select lists); the time-off read selects it only when the policy allows, and the answer then omits the key, never sending `null` (the permission matrix's time-off rows assert the key's absence for every role that may not see it). Everyone holding `workload.view` sees the dates and hours.
- **Written by those who may read it**: the person for their own dates, a holder of `workload.manage` for anyone's. A write never echoes another person's type back to a caller who could not read it.

### Who records it (decision 288)

- **The person** records, changes and deletes their own time off (`workload.time_off`); **a holder of `workload.manage`** records, changes and deletes anyone's (time away already known, entered for the person). Nobody approves it: Islestone is not an HR system, and approval of time away stays in a workspace's HR tools.
- **When someone else writes a person's time off**, the person gets one bell (`TIME_OFF_CHANGED_BY_OTHER`, decision 300), naming who and the dates, never the type; the dates show "Added by Ana" on the person's own view.
- **Only a signed-in person writes** (the health and time rule, `setsHealthAsPerson` / `recordsTimeAsPerson`: no API key, agent, job or system user; refused with an audited 403 `NOT_A_PERSON`). Agents never record anyone's time off or capacity (ADR 0003 §4).
- **Concurrency.** Every time-off write takes the person's advisory lock (`time-off:<workspace>:<person>`) first, then the membership `FOR KEY SHARE`, then reads the dates it names; each date it changes carries the version the browser read (or none for a new date), and any mismatch refuses the whole write, 409 `VERSION_CONFLICT` naming the dates. The unique is the backstop.

## Allocation (3.5b)

### What counts (decision 292)

An item counts toward its **assignee's** allocation when it is live (`deletedAt` NULL), open (Backlog, Unstarted or Started), in a live, unarchived project the caller can see (`visibleProjects`, the read fails closed), assigned to a person, and its team estimates in hours with a counted `estimateMinutes` (`countedEstimate`). Each item counts its own estimate: estimates do not roll up in 3.4a, and a parent and its sub-items each count what they hold (a team that estimates both is shown both). Waiting on and reviewers carry no allocation: the assignee is the accountable person (ADR 0003 §1).

Beside the hours, each person's row names what could not be placed: **No dates** (counted estimate, neither date; their count and hours), **Not estimated** (open assigned items with no counted estimate, in hours teams and in teams that do not estimate; their count), and **Points work** (open assigned items of points teams; their count and points, decision 294). Unassigned work with hours is one row of its own ("Unassigned"), shown and never compared with a capacity.

### Placing hours into weeks (decision 293)

The pure `src/app-layer/domain/workload/allocation.ts` (property-tested), over epoch days, each item on **its own** calendar (ADR 0010 §5: the team's, else the workspace's), "today" in that calendar's zone through `todayClock` (decision 191):

1. **Remaining**: `R = max(0, estimateMinutes − timeSpentMinutes)`, the time spent being every finished entry on the item (timers excluded, as everywhere in 3.4b) when the caller may see an item's time (decision 295); otherwise `R` is the estimate and the answer says so.
2. **The span** is the engine's `plannedSpanOf(dates, calendar)`: start to due; one date, one working day on it; start after due, one day at the due date. Never a second span rule.
3. **From today**: the placement starts at the later of the span's start and today's next working day. An item whose span ended before today (overdue) places all of `R` on today's next working day and is marked overdue.
4. **Into weeks**: the minutes are shared over the placement's working days by week, each week's share proportional to `workingDaysInclusive(calendar, max(a, monday), min(b, sunday))`, by largest remainder with ties to the earlier week, so the weeks sum to `R` exactly. A week outside the window adds to the row's "Later" figure.

A week is Monday to Sunday by date (decision 268, `WEEK_STARTS_ON = 1`), never passed through a zone. The module imports only `domain/schedule/calendar.ts` and `span.ts`, so the timeline, the calendar view, the roadmap and the workload read count working days the same way. An item's hours land on its own calendar's working days even when its assignee is off: the person's week then shows the conflict as over capacity (decision 290), which the ways to rebalance address.

### Over capacity (decision 296)

A person's week is **over capacity** when its allocated minutes exceed its capacity; "Over by 6h" is the difference. A week with zero capacity (all time off, a deactivated member) and any allocation is over. Nothing else is flagged and nothing is enforced: assigning work into a full week is never refused (the roadmap: "flagged", "no automatic levelling"; Cal.diy's duration rule reported, never blocking).

## The Workload view (3.5b)

### Where it appears (decision 297)

- **A sidebar entry "Workload"** in the Workspace section (`NAV_PATHS.workload = '/workload'`, visible to `workload.view`), a `MAIN_PAGES` entry, a `common.sections` key and a catalogue key. Not a layout of the Work items page: its rows are people, not items.
- **Filters** (the shared `FilterToolbar`): people (any active members), a project, a team (people with counted work in that team's projects in the window), a recipient list (resolved by `resolveListRecipients` at read time), "Over capacity only". A filter narrows **the people**; each person's allocation always counts all their visible work, and a project or team filter shows that share in the cell as a second figure ("12h of 38h in Website").
- **Weeks**: twelve from the current week by default (today in the workspace calendar's zone), previous and next, "This week", at most 26 per read.

### The read

`GET /api/t/:slug/workload?from=YYYY-MM-DD&weeks=1..26&userId=&projectId=&teamId=&recipientListId=&over=` (`requirePermission('workload.view')`), usecase `getWorkload` (`src/app-layer/usecases/workload.ts`):

1. `from` is a Monday, not before the current week (400 `WORKLOAD_RANGE`; decision 295), `weeks` at most 26.
2. **The people**: ACTIVE members (any role but GUEST) and deactivated members still assigned open items, narrowed by the filters; at most `WORKLOAD_PERSON_LIMIT` (500), read one past and refused 422 `WORKLOAD_TOO_LARGE` with the count, never cut.
3. **Capacity**: the settings row, the people's capacity rows, their time-off dates in the window (`TimeOffRepository.daysForPeople`: `userId`, `day`, `minutes`; never `type`), and the calendars (`resolveWorkingCalendars` for every team named by a capacity row or an item).
4. **Work**: one read of the people's live, open, assigned items in visible, unarchived projects with a due date on or after the window's start or a start date on or before its end, or neither (`WorkItemRepository.listForWorkload`, on `WorkItem_tenantId_assigneeId_stateCategory_dueDate_idx`), at most `WORKLOAD_ITEM_LIMIT` (20,000) read one past (422 `WORKLOAD_TOO_LARGE`); the fields `id`, `key`, `title`, `projectId`, `teamId`, `stateCategory`, `startDate`, `dueDate`, both estimate columns and the team's unit. No description, comment or field value.
5. **Time spent**: when the caller may see an item's time (`mayViewTime` or `mayTrackTime`, decision 266), one grouped read of finished entries for those items (`TimeEntryRepository.totalsForItems`, on `TimeEntry_tenantId_workItemId_workDate_idx`); otherwise `timeSpentCounted: false`.
6. **The answer**: `weeks`, and per person `capacity[]`, `allocated[]`, `over[]`, `timeOff[]` (minutes removed and the dates with time off, per week; never a type), `later`, `noDates`, `notEstimated`, `pointsWork`, `active`; the "Unassigned" row; the calendars' ids and zones; `today`; `timeSpentCounted`. Computed by the pure modules on the server. Not stored, not audited, never logged per person (Cal.diy study §7 row 14).

`GET /workload/cell?userId&week` (`workload.view`): the items that fill one person's week, each with its minutes there, its dates and the reason it is there ("due 9 Dec", "overdue", "spread over 12 working days"), the week's time-off dates with their hours (never a type), at most 200 items, and from 3.5c the ways to rebalance. One more read of the same shape, bounded to one person.

### The page

- **The grid** (`src/components/workload/WorkloadGrid.tsx`): a real `role=grid` on a `div`, rows people, columns weeks, Islestone's own roving tabindex with arrow, Home and End keys (the pattern of My time's `week-grid-model.ts` and `CalendarMonth`), the pure layout in `workload-model.ts`. Each cell's label: "Ana Petrova, week of 7 December: 46 hours allocated of 32, over by 14; time off on 2 days". A row ends with No dates, Not estimated, Points work and Later.
- **A cell opens a sheet** with the week's items in the shared `DataTable` (key, title, hours this week, dates, why), each opening the item's sheet; from 3.5c, "Ways to rebalance" under it.
- **"My capacity and time off"** at the top of the page for the signed-in person: their capacity ("40h a week, Workspace calendar") and their time off as a grid of dates (decision 286), a month at a time, each date showing its hours or "Whole day" and its type (it is their own). Removing a future capacity row or someone's time off goes through the undo toast.
- **The data** is `src/components/workload/data/use-workload.ts` (and `use-time-off.ts`, `use-member-capacity.ts`), through `use-tenant-swr.ts`; a work-item write anywhere re-reads the mounted workload (registered with `work-item-cache.ts` for re-read, not placement: a change of assignee, dates, estimate or state moves hours between rows and weeks that only the server's read recomputes). The shared-query guard holds the folder; every change sends its versions.

## Ways to rebalance (3.5c; decisions 283 and 299)

The pure `src/app-layer/domain/workload/options.ts` (property-tested), computed on the server for one over-capacity cell, from the same inputs as the read:

- **The items that fill the week**, by their minutes there, most first.
- **"Assign to …"**, for each of the five largest items: at most three members who (a) are ACTIVE, (b) already hold open assigned work in the item's project in the window (so the names are people on that project, not the whole workspace), and (c) would stay within capacity in every week of the item's placement if it were theirs; ranked by their free minutes in the cell's week, then by name. "Assign to Ana: 14h free that week." It opens the assign picker with Ana chosen; the existing assign write (`work_items.assign`, field sets, audit, bells) does the rest.
- **"Move dates"**, for each of the five largest items: the earliest dates, keeping its working-day duration on its own calendar and moving later only, at which the person is within capacity in every week the item would cover, searched over at most 26 weeks. "Move ISL-12 to 14–18 Dec." It opens 3.1c's `ChangeDatesDialog` over the item's project's timeline with those dates (as the calendar view does, decision 241), so the preview, the push set, the bound of 100 and every rule are 3.1c's.
- **"Due during time off"**: an item due on a date the person has a whole day off, named (never the type).

Nothing is applied by an option, and nothing is stored: a person opens one, sees the existing confirmation and confirms or not. An option is offered only when the viewer could make that write (`work_items.assign`, `editableRows` for dates, asked on the server). No agent computes or applies them; 7.5's agent rescheduling, and its Timefold evaluation for levelling, will read the same modules and propose through the proposal queue (ADR 0003).

## Agents (ADR 0003)

- **An agent is never a row** (it has no membership) and never writes capacity or time off; the person-only check refuses an agent, an API key and a job on every write.
- **Delegated items** (6.3's `delegateAgentId`) count toward their assignee: the person stays accountable for the work an agent does on it.
- **An agent never reads a time-off type**: its authority is narrowed to its authorising person's (ADR 0003 §4), and no tool returns the type to a model. 7.4 and 7.5 read dates and hours only.
- **7.4** names over-allocation as a risk signal computed in code: it calls `domain/workload`, never a second computation. **7.5** evaluates Timefold for levelling and writes an ADR then; any agent reassignment or date change it produces is a proposal a person approves.

## H3 and 3.5

The roadmap's H3 names nothing for workload. The pull requests carry these, and H3's gate adds them to its list:

- **Properties**: a week's capacity equals the day shares' sum, never negative, never above `W`; adding a time-off date or a holiday never raises it; a whole-day row removes exactly its day's share and a row on a non-working day removes nothing; an item's placed minutes sum to its remaining minutes exactly, every week's share non-negative, none before today; a later due date never moves minutes earlier; results byte-equal under five process time zones (each in its own process, as the engine's); points never appear in an hours sum.
- **The type**: the three codes' ciphertexts have equal lengths; no response of the workload, cell or time-off routes carries `type` for a caller other than the person or a holder of `workload.manage`, for every role and both custom roles; no audit row, log line or notification of a time-off write holds the code (an integration test reads them back); a raw insert of a plaintext type is refused by the CHECK.
- **Options**: an "Assign to" option never names someone who would go over in any week of the placement; a "Move dates" option moves later only and brings the person within capacity in every week it covers; nothing an option offers is written by computing it.
- **Concurrency**: two writes of one person's same date (one wins, the other 409 `VERSION_CONFLICT`); a capacity or time-off write racing the member's removal (the removal never fails; the write is refused, and the removed member's rows are gone).
- **Person-only**: an API-key, agent and job context refused on every write route.

## Open questions for the owner (all decided, 2026-11-27)

Each question stands alone. The recommended option is first and marked; each option carries its main trade-off. Each carries its decision, numbered 281 + its question.

**Words and record**

1. **What are the new things called, and where are the rules recorded?**
   - A (recommended). "Capacity" (`MemberCapacity`, hours a week), "Time off" (`TimeOff`), "Workload" (the page), "Allocated", "Over capacity", "Free", "Workload settings" (`TenantWorkloadSettings`, Settings → Workload); a new ADR 0013 "Workload and capacity", and ADR 0006 rows. Uses the word ADR 0006 already reserves for a person's time off and avoids every banned word ("Leave", "Allowance", "Slack", "Schedule", "Report").
   - B. The same words, no new ADR: the rules go in ADR 0010 (calendars) and `docs/workload.md`. One fewer document; personal data and permission rules sit in the scheduling ADR.
   - C. "Availability" for capacity and "Absence" for time off. Familiar from HR tools; "Availability" is a booking word, and ADR 0006's reservation of "Time off" would be undone.
   - **Decided:** A, the names as proposed and a new ADR 0013 (decision 282, 2026-11-27). Applied: ADR 0013 (Accepted), ADR 0006 "Workload words of sub-phase 3.5", ADR 0003 §4, ADR 0010's notes.

2. **What are the read-only suggestions called?** "Suggestion" is banned (ADR 0006's Proposal, Change dates and Intake request rows) and refused in every identifier by the schema guard.
   - A (recommended). "Ways to rebalance" as the heading, each line an option named by its act ("Assign to Ana", "Move dates"). Says what they are for; no new noun to learn.
   - B. "Recommendations". A familiar word; implies the product chooses, close to what proposals do.
   - C. "Hints". Short; reads as help text, and undersells that each opens a real change.
   - **Decided:** A, "Ways to rebalance" (decision 283, 2026-11-27).

**Capacity**

3. **Where does a member's capacity come from, and can it change over time?**
   - A (recommended). A workspace default (40 hours a week unless an Owner or Admin changes it), and a per-member capacity from a chosen Monday ("from 1 February, 24h"), with its history. Every member has a number from day one; part-time and future changes are planned without editing the past.
   - B. Per-member only, current value, no default: a member with none set shows "No capacity set" and is never flagged. Nothing assumed; the view is empty until someone fills every member in.
   - C. A workspace default only, no per-member value. Simplest; wrong for every part-time member.
   - **Decided:** A, a workspace default and a per-member capacity from a Monday with its history (decision 284, 2026-11-27).

4. **How do weekly hours meet working calendars and holidays?** A member belongs to no team.
   - A (recommended). Weekly hours are shared evenly over the working weekdays of the person's calendar, which is the workspace's unless their capacity names a team's; a holiday on it removes that day's hours; time off removes its days or hours. Reuses 3.1's calendars and the one working-day count; a person working with a team abroad gets its holidays.
   - B. Per-person hours for each weekday ("Mon–Thu 8h, Fri 4h") on the workspace's calendar. Fits uneven weeks; seven numbers per person, and no team's holidays.
   - C. Weekly hours only; calendars and holidays ignored, time off still subtracted. Simplest; a week with a public holiday shows full capacity.
   - **Decided:** A, weekly hours over the person's calendar, the workspace's or a named team's (decision 285, 2026-11-27).

**Time off**

5. **What can time off be?**
   - A (recommended). A range of whole days, or a range with the same number of hours off each working day (a part day: "Friday afternoon, 4h"). Covers half days and short absences with one shape.
   - B. Whole days only. Simplest; a half day must be entered as a full day or not at all.
   - C. Free hours per date (any pattern). Most precise; a grid of numbers to enter for a week away.
   - **Decided:** **C, free hours per date, a departure from the recommendation** (decision 286, 2026-11-27): time off is one row per person and date holding the hours off or "whole day", unique per person and date; a range is entered as a range and stored date by date on the working days of the person's calendar, each date changeable on its own. Applied: the range-and-exclusion model is replaced ("Time off (3.5a)"), capacity removes each date's hours ("Capacity in a week"), and the routes set and remove dates in batches.

6. **What does time off record about why?** Illness is health data, which needs special care under GDPR (general knowledge, not legal advice).
   - A (recommended). Nothing: dates and hours only. Nobody can learn the reason from Islestone; nothing to encrypt or restrict.
   - B. A type from a fixed list (Holiday, Sick, Other), shown only to the person and workload managers. Helps planning conversations; a "Sick" row is health data held by Islestone.
   - C. An optional free-text description, encrypted, shown only to the person and managers. Flexible; people will write medical details in it.
   - **Decided:** **B, a fixed type, a departure from the recommendation** (decision 287, 2026-11-27): each date carries a type (Vacation, Sick or Other since decision 303; the owner first named Holiday), visible only to the person and to holders of `workload.manage` (confirmed with decision 298: everyone with `workload.view` sees the dates and hours, never the type). Applied: the type is an encrypted manifest column holding the code, padded to one length, required and CHECKed to ciphertext; never audited, logged, indexed, searched, notified or exported; absent from every read for a caller who may not see it ("The type, held apart"). Decision 303 renamed the first value Vacation, so "Holiday" keeps its one meaning; "Vacation", "Sick" and "Other" collide with nothing.

7. **Who records time off, and is it approved?**
   - A (recommended). The person records their own; a holder of `workload.manage` may record anyone's, and the person gets a bell when someone else does; no approval. Islestone plans around time away without becoming an HR system.
   - B. As A, with approval: time off counts only once a manager approves it. Mirrors HR tools; a second approval flow beside timesheets', and HR tools already approve time away.
   - C. Only workload managers record time off. One place of truth; people cannot say they are away without asking someone.
   - **Decided:** A, the person or a workload manager, a bell when someone else does, no approval (decision 288, 2026-11-27).

8. **How long is time off kept?**
   - A (recommended). Deleted 90 days after it ends by a nightly job (the period settable by the operator). The view looks forward; old absences are personal data with no use.
   - B. For the workspace's life, like time entries. Lets a later past-weeks view show old capacity; holds years of absence dates.
   - C. Until the person or a manager deletes it. No job; nobody will.
   - **Decided:** **B, kept for the workspace's life, a departure from the recommendation** (decision 289, 2026-11-27): no purge job and no retention variable; the person or a workload manager changes or deletes any date. Applied: the job, its index and `TIME_OFF_RETENTION_DAYS` are dropped. Recorded with it as a rule: a removed member's capacity and time off go with the membership, by its cascading key.

9. **Does time off change schedules?** ADR 0010 lists this as a reason to revisit it.
   - A (recommended). No: time off lowers capacity only; the scheduling engine, the timeline, the calendar view and review steps' due days keep counting team and workspace calendars. ADR 0010 unchanged; the over-capacity flag shows the conflict instead of moving dates silently.
   - B. Yes for the engine: an item's working days skip its assignee's time off. Timelines reflect absence; a per-person calendar in the engine, an ADR 0010 amendment, and a reassignment changes an item's computed dates.
   - C. As B, and review steps' due days skip the reviewer's time off. Most faithful; due days depend on private absence and move with it.
   - **Decided:** A, time off lowers capacity only (decision 290, 2026-11-27).

10. **Does time off reach external calendars?**
    - A (recommended). No: time off stays in Islestone in 3.5. ADR 0011 unchanged; no new event kind or scope.
    - B. Push a person's own time off to their Islestone calendar (3.3b's writer) as private, busy, all-day events. Helpful to colleagues booking meetings; a third event kind and its teardown.
    - C. Read "out of office" events from the connected calendar as time off. No double entry; a new read beyond the Islestone calendar, which ADR 0011 forbids, and other people's events read.
    - **Decided:** A, no calendar sync of time off (decision 291, 2026-11-27).

**Allocation**

11. **What counts as allocated?**
    - A (recommended). The assignee's open (Backlog, Unstarted, Started), live items with an hours estimate, in visible, unarchived projects; each item its own estimate (a parent and its sub-items each count what they hold); undated work, unestimated work and points work named per person, not placed. Every open commitment counts once per item it is on; nothing is guessed.
    - B. As A, but Backlog excluded (only Unstarted and Started). Counts only committed work; dated backlog items vanish from planning.
    - C. As A, and a parent with live estimated sub-items counts nothing of its own. Avoids double counting for teams estimating both levels; a parent's own work disappears.
    - **Decided:** A (decision 292, 2026-11-27).

12. **How are an item's hours placed into weeks?**
    - A (recommended). The remaining hours (estimate less time spent, at least zero) spread evenly over the item's remaining working days on its own calendar, from today (or its start, if later) to its due date; one date, one working day on it (the engine's rule); overdue work on today, marked. Matches how the timeline reads dates; past days absorb nothing.
    - B. The whole estimate over the whole planned span, past days included. Stable numbers week to week; finished-on-time work still shows as remaining, and overdue work vanishes.
    - C. All of it in the due date's week. Simplest; a three-week item lands as one spike.
    - **Decided:** A (decision 293, 2026-11-27).

13. **What happens to points?** ADR 0012 and decision 257 say points and hours are never converted.
    - A (recommended). Not converted: a person's points work is named beside their hours ("3 items, 13 points"), never compared with capacity. Keeps ADR 0012; points teams see their items but no over-capacity flag from them.
    - B. Each points team sets "hours per point", used only by the workload view. Points teams get flags; an ADR 0012 amendment, and a ratio that is a guess dressed as a number.
    - C. Ignored: points work is not shown at all. Simplest; a points team's people look idle.
    - **Decided:** A, points never converted, named beside the hours (decision 294, 2026-11-27).

14. **How does time already recorded count?** A person's weekly total is visible only to `time.approve` (decision 266).
    - A (recommended). Only to shrink each item's remaining hours (its time spent, which every holder of `time.view` already sees on the item); the view looks forward from the current week and never shows a person's recorded week. Respects decision 266; no "actuals" column.
    - B. As A, and earlier weeks show recorded time against capacity, only to the person and to `time.approve` holders. Lets managers compare plan and actual; two kinds of cell, and the view differs by viewer.
    - C. Not at all: allocation is the whole estimate. Simplest; an item 90 % done still counts in full.
    - **Decided:** A (decision 295, 2026-11-27).

15. **When is a week over capacity?**
    - A (recommended). When allocated hours exceed capacity, shown as "Over by 6h"; a week with no capacity and any allocation is over. One rule, no setting.
    - B. Above a workspace threshold (100 % by default, settable from 50 % to 200 %). Teams that plan with slack can flag earlier; a setting that makes the same week red in one workspace and not another.
    - C. Two bands: "Nearly full" at 85 % and "Over capacity" above 100 %. Earlier warning; more colour to read.
    - **Decided:** A (decision 296, 2026-11-27).

**The view**

16. **Where is the workload view, and what does it cover?**
    - A (recommended). A sidebar page, "Workload", over the whole workspace: one row per person, filters for people, a project, a team or a recipient list narrowing who is shown, each person's cell always counting all their visible work (a filter's share shown as a second figure). Capacity is a person's, so the row is the person whatever the filter.
    - B. A layout on the Work items, project and cycle pages (`WORKLOAD` in saved views). Fits the layout switcher; a person's cell then shows only that scope's work, so "over capacity" means different things on each page.
    - C. Both. Most reach; two surfaces for one read, and the scope ambiguity of B.
    - **Decided:** A (decision 297, 2026-11-27).

17. **Which keys, and who sees what?**
    - A (recommended). A new `workload` domain: `view` (everyone's capacity, time-off days and allocation; Owners, Admins, Members, Viewers), `time_off` (record one's own; the same four), `manage` (the default, anyone's capacity and time off; Owners, Admins); Guests nothing (decision 204). Planning needs to see who is away; custom roles can grant each.
    - B. The same keys, but capacity hours and time-off days of others shown only to `manage` holders; everyone else sees allocated hours and the over-capacity flag. More private; a colleague cannot tell why a week is full.
    - C. No new domain: viewing rides on `work_items.view`, managing on `admin.manage`, one's own time off on no key (like notification preferences). Fewer keys; a custom role cannot grant workload management without workspace administration.
    - **Decided:** A, the `workload` domain as proposed (decision 298, 2026-11-27); with decision 287, `workload.view` shows the dates and hours of time off and never its type, which only the person and holders of `workload.manage` see.

18. **What are the read-only suggestions?**
    - A (recommended). For a full week: the items that fill it, and for the largest five, "Assign to …" (up to three people already working on that project who would stay within capacity) and "Move dates" (the earliest later dates that bring the person within capacity), each opening the existing assign picker or "Change dates" confirmation; items due during time off named. Concrete and safe: nothing changes until a person confirms in a dialog that already exists.
    - B. Only the items that fill the week, each opening its sheet. Smallest; the person works out the fix alone, which the roadmap's "suggestions" asks the product to help with.
    - C. As A, and "Assign to" considers every active member, not only the project's people. More candidates; names people with no context on the project.
    - **Decided:** A (decision 299, 2026-11-27).

19. **What else shows workload, and who is notified?**
    - A (recommended). One line on My Work ("This week 34 of 40h · next week over by 6h") linking to the Workload page, and one bell to a person when someone else adds, changes or removes their time off; no over-capacity notifications (7.4 will explain over-allocation). People see their own load where they start, and learn when their absence is recorded for them.
    - B. Neither: the Workload page only. Quietest; people do not see their own load unless they look.
    - C. As A, and a Monday bell to a person whose week is over capacity. Prompts action; a notification every week for people who are always busy.
    - **Decided:** A, My Work's line and the bell when someone else changes one's time off (decision 300, 2026-11-27); the bell never names the type.

**Delivery**

20. **What is taken from Cal.diy?**
    - A (recommended). Patterns only, through its study (§4.3 capacity from weekly hours with holidays and time off; §4.6 the duration rule as a report); no file of the clone opened; a `PROVENANCE.md` row "pattern only", no `NOTICE`. Nothing is copied, so the study's open relicensing question (its §9, question 1) does not arise.
    - B. As A, and translate Cal.diy's date-range test cases (inputs and expected outputs) into Islestone's tests, carrying its MIT notice into `NOTICE` (study §8). More cases; needs the owner's answer to the study's relicensing question first.
    - C. Nothing, no row: written fresh from the roadmap alone. No provenance to keep; ignores a useful, already-reviewed design.
    - **Decided:** A, patterns only, through the study (decision 301, 2026-11-27).

21. **How is 3.5 delivered?**
    - A (recommended). Three pull requests: 3.5a capacity and time off (with the page's capacity grid and "My capacity and time off"), 3.5b allocation and the Workload view with My Work's line, 3.5c the read-only options. Each reviewable alone; people can enter capacity and time off before allocation lands.
    - B. Two: capacity and time off; then allocation, the view and the options together. Fewer reviews; the options' rules mixed with the allocation arithmetic.
    - C. One. Fastest to track; about 5,100 lines in one review.
    - **Decided:** A, three pull requests: 3.5a, 3.5b, 3.5c (decision 302, 2026-11-27).
