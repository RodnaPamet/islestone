The roadmap prompt asks for: ordered review steps on a work item; each step names reviewers (members or a recipient list), all or any, a due date in working days; while a step is open the item waits on its reviewers who have not responded, the next step opens when it completes, and after the last the item waits on its assignee again; two outcomes and an optional comment; a returned step closes the round and opens a new round of the same steps, the earlier round read-only; default steps per type set by an admin, copied at create; only people respond, never an agent (a delegate at most drafts a review comment as a proposal); every response audited, every route a literal `requirePermission`, notifications through the shared writers; conditional steps wait for 5.1.

## Summary

- **Nothing is copied from inflect-compliance; four patterns are re-used.** inflect has no multi-step review. What it has: a single-row request and decision (`PolicyApproval`, `prisma/schema/policy.prisma` lines 105–129, decided in `usecases/policy.ts` `decidePolicyApproval`, lines 776–880; `EvidenceReview`, `evidence.prisma` lines 179–193, an append-only action log), a reviewer column on its task (`Task.reviewerUserId`, `tasks.prisma` line 31, never a flow), and its access-review campaigns, whose decision rows are one-way stamps. Patterns taken, none of their code: (1) the claim on a still-pending row (`PolicyApprovalRepository.decide`'s `status: 'PENDING'` predicate) so two responses never both land; (2) the stale-decision refusal (a decision on a request that is no longer the open question is a 409, not an overwrite); (3) the one-way stamp trigger (`access_review_connected_decision_one_way`, migration `20261018130100_agent_recertification_campaigns` lines 232–277: a recorded verdict and the frozen subject cannot change); (4) "zero is never complete" (inflect `CLAUDE.md`, legacy access invariant 5: a step with no reviewers left never completes by an empty `every`). Separation of duties from `decidePolicyApproval` (the requester may not approve their own request) is offered as an option in open question 11, not assumed. Its UI (`src/components/ui/ApprovalBanner.tsx`) is not in the mirror and is not taken: it names Approve and Reject, proposal words here.
- **The model is four tables on the item and two for type defaults.** A round (`WorkItemReviewRound`) holds ordered steps (`WorkItemReviewStep`); a step names members and recipient lists before it opens (`WorkItemReviewStepList` for lists) and holds one reviewer row per person once it opens (`WorkItemReviewer`, with the response on the row). Default steps per type are `WorkItemTypeReviewStep` and `WorkItemTypeReviewStepReviewer`. A round that has ended is frozen by trigger; a response is a one-way stamp.
- **The hard part is waiting on.** 2.4b's waiting on names at most one actor (`WorkItem.waitingOnUserId`, and from 6.3 a session reference, decision 106, with a CHECK that at most one is set). A step names several reviewers. This map recommends keeping the column one actor and deriving "waiting on the reviewers" from the open step's pending reviewer rows, with the column cleared while a round is open and set to the assignee when it ends (open questions 5 and 6).
- **Agents cannot review by construction.** A reviewer is a person with an ACTIVE membership; an agent has none (ADR 0003 §3). The respond usecase also refuses an API key and, from 6.1, an agent context (`assertActingAsPerson`). 2.8 builds no agent path; 6.2 adds a proposal kind for a drafted review comment that never records a response.
- **Size.** About 4,500 lines of source and 4,000 of tests, so three pull requests (open question 18).
- **The roadmap's four open questions** are refined below (1–4), with fourteen new ones the code raised (5–18).

## Vocabulary (ADR 0006)

Rule 1: each concept gets a row before its first model or label. Proposed (open question 1):

| Concept | Model | UI | Note |
|---|---|---|---|
| Review step | `WorkItemReviewStep`; enum `ReviewStepMode` (`ALL`, `ANY`), `ReviewStepStatus` | Review step; "All must sign off", "Any one signs off" | Not "Stage" (banned for workflow states), "Approval step" or "Approval workflow" (Approve is the proposal's verb; Workflow is rule 6), "Review" alone (6.2's proposal review page), "Gate". "Step" is free: ADR 0006 uses it only in a workflow state's description, and `AgentRunStep` is qualified. |
| Round | `WorkItemReviewRound`; enum `ReviewRoundStatus` | Round; "Round 2" | Not "Revision" (reads as a version of the item's content, which 4.1's pages will have), "Pass" (banned for the sender check since 2.6c), "Cycle" (taken), "Iteration" (banned for cycles). |
| Reviewer | `WorkItemReviewer` (one row per person per opened step) | Reviewer(s) | Not "Approver", "Signatory", "Assignee" (for this role). |
| Review outcome | enum `ReviewOutcome`: `SIGNED_OFF`, `RETURNED` | Sign off, Return; "Signed off", "Returned" | Not Approve, Edit and approve, Reject (proposals), Accept, Decline (intake's verbs, and banned synonyms of Approve for proposals), Pass/Fail (4.5's checks). |
| Default review steps | `WorkItemTypeReviewStep`, `WorkItemTypeReviewStepReviewer` | A type's "Review steps" | Not "Review template" ("Template" names 2.5c's item and project templates), "Review workflow". |
| Response text | `WorkItemReviewer.responseText` (encrypted) | Shown under the outcome; "Why it is returned" | Never `note` or `message` (banned in identifiers). Open question 10 decides whether it is this column or a locked `Comment`. |

The area has no noun of its own in the UI beyond "Review steps" on the item and the type, and "To review" in My Work. The proposal queue keeps "Review" (6.2); a reviewer's list is "To review", never "Reviews".

**The guard trap.** `tests/guards/schema-vocabulary.test.ts` turns an unqualified ban into a schema-wide ban. "Approve", "Reject", "Accept" and "Decline" are already banned for their concepts; the new rows must qualify "Stage" ("for a review step"), "Gate" and "Template" ("as this model"), or `WorkItemTemplate` and every comment that says "stage" break.

## Model

New file `prisma/schema/reviews.prisma` (one owner per declaration, `prisma/schema/README.md`), enums in `enums.prisma`. Every table follows the tenant-table rules: `tenantId`, a tenant-leading index, the four RLS policies with FORCE and its own `GRANT … TO app_user` in the migration that creates it, and a two-tenant test in `ISOLATION_TESTED`.

### On the item

- **`WorkItemReviewRound`**: `id`, `tenantId`, `workItemId` (composite FK to `WorkItem(id, tenantId)`, CASCADE so the 30-day purge removes it), `number` (1, 2, …; unique per item), `status` `ReviewRoundStatus`: `PLANNED` (steps editable, nothing open), `OPEN`, `COMPLETED` (every step signed off or skipped), `RETURNED` (a step was returned), `WITHDRAWN` (ended unfinished: the item closed, or a person withdrew it); `startedAt`, `startedByUserId`, `endedAt`, `endedByUserId` (NULL when the system ended it), `endReason` (text CHECKed: `ITEM_CLOSED`, `ITEM_DELETED`, `WITHDRAWN_BY_PERSON`, `NO_STEPS` …), `version` (optimistic concurrency for step edits on this round, ADR 0002 §9's rule applied to the round). A partial unique index allows at most one `PLANNED` or `OPEN` round per item.
- **`WorkItemReviewStep`**: `id`, `tenantId`, `roundId` (FK `(roundId, tenantId)`), `workItemId` (denormalised, FK to the item, for the indexes), `position` (int, unique per round: steps are few and ordered, so no fractional key), `name` (plaintext, sanitised, ≤ 120, like a title), `mode` `ReviewStepMode` (`ALL`, `ANY`), `dueInWorkingDays` (CHECK 1..60), `status` `ReviewStepStatus`: `PENDING` (not yet open), `OPEN`, `COMPLETED`, `RETURNED`, `SKIPPED`, `WITHDRAWN`; `openedAt`, `dueOn` (date-only, set once when it opens; never passed through a time zone), `endedAt`, `skippedByUserId`, `skipReason` (text CHECKed: `PERSON_SKIPPED`, `NO_REVIEWERS_LEFT`). Index `(tenantId, status, dueOn)` for the reminder job.
- **`WorkItemReviewStepList`**: the recipient lists a step names (`stepId`, `recipientListId`, FK to `RecipientList(id, tenantId)` RESTRICT: lists are archive-only, decision 141). Resolved when the step opens (open question 7).
- **`WorkItemReviewer`**: `id`, `tenantId`, `stepId`, `roundId`, `workItemId` (denormalised for "To review" and the reminders), `userId` (FK to `User`, as `Comment.authorUserId`: no membership FK, because a departed reviewer's response must outlive their membership), `viaRecipientListId` (NULL when named directly), `outcome` `ReviewOutcome?`, `respondedAt`, `responseText` (ENCRYPTED, sanitised, ≤ 4,000; open question 10), `itemVersionAt` (the item's `version` when they responded, so the record says what was reviewed; open question 16), `withdrawnAt`, `withdrawnReason` (text CHECKed: `MEMBER_REMOVED`, `STEP_ENDED`, `REMOVED_BY_PERSON`). Unique `(stepId, userId)`. Index `(tenantId, userId, outcome, withdrawnAt)` for "To review". A reviewer named directly on a `PENDING` step is a row with no response; a list's people get rows when the step opens.
- CHECKs: `outcome IS NULL` ⇔ `respondedAt IS NULL`; `outcome` and `withdrawnAt` never both set; `responseText IS NOT NULL` when `outcome = 'RETURNED'` if open question 10 makes it required.

### Defaults per type

- **`WorkItemTypeReviewStep`**: `tenantId`, `typeId` (FK to `WorkItemType(id, tenantId)` CASCADE), `position`, `name`, `mode`, `dueInWorkingDays`. Per type across the workspace (open question 12).
- **`WorkItemTypeReviewStepReviewer`**: `stepId`, and exactly one of `userId` (an ACTIVE member when written; FK to `TenantMembership(tenantId, userId)` CASCADE, so a removed member leaves every default) or `recipientListId` (a workspace list only: a type spans projects; CHECK exactly one).
- Copied, never linked: a create copies the type's defaults into round 1 `PLANNED`; a later change to the defaults changes no item (the 2.4b rule, decision 109).

### What the database holds (migration SQL, with a catalogue test)

- **A round that has ended is frozen.** A trigger `work_item_review_round_frozen` refuses any `UPDATE` or `DELETE` of a step, a step's list or a reviewer row whose round is `COMPLETED`, `RETURNED` or `WITHDRAWN`, and any move of a round out of those statuses. The one admitted change is the key-rotation sweeps' re-wrap of `responseText` (ciphertext to ciphertext, every other column unchanged), as CLAUDE.md requires of every write-once trigger on an encrypted column.
- **A response is a one-way stamp.** The same trigger refuses changing `outcome`, `respondedAt`, `responseText` or `itemVersionAt` once `outcome` is set, in any round (open question 15 can relax this for an open step).
- **Steps of an open round** may be deleted only while `PENDING` (trigger), and `app_user` holds no `DELETE` on rounds or reviewer rows: a reviewer named by mistake is withdrawn (`REMOVED_BY_PERSON`), not deleted, so the record keeps who was asked.
- The item's purge still removes everything: referential actions run as the table owner, past the missing `DELETE` grant and a `BEFORE UPDATE` trigger; `tests/integration/work-item-purge.test.ts` gains the case.
- No change to `work_item_transition_record`: the round and reviewer rows carry their own times, and the column changes the steps make (below) are recorded as any waiting-on change is.

Migrations: one per enum value added to `NotificationType` / `EmailNotificationType` (`tests/guards/migration-enum-isolation.test.ts`), then one for the new enums and tables. About `20261208120000` to `20261208120400`.

## The lifecycle

Pure rules first: `src/app-layer/domain/review-steps.ts` (client-safe, property-tested) holds `stepOutcome(mode, reviewers)` (open, completed or returned), `nextStep(round)`, `roundOutcome(round)` and `reviewAffordances`; the usecases and the detail read it, as `planAnswerMark` and `stateTransitionEffects` are read. Every write below runs in one transaction that locks the item's open round first (`SELECT … FOR UPDATE` on the round row), so two responses, a response and a skip, or a response and a step edit serialise.

1. **Planned.** A create copies the type's defaults into round 1, `PLANNED`; a person may add, change, reorder or remove steps and reviewers (open question 3). An item with no steps has no round.
2. **Start.** "Send for review" (open question 8) moves the round to `OPEN` and opens its first step. An empty round cannot start (`NO_STEPS`), and a step with nobody named and no list cannot open (`NO_REVIEWERS`).
3. **A step opens**: its lists are resolved through `resolveListRecipients` (`may: work_items.view`, the item's project; open question 7) and their people get reviewer rows beside the named ones (each person once); every reviewer must be an ACTIVE member who sees the project at that moment, the rest are dropped and named in the audit entry. A step left with nobody is refused at open (`NO_REVIEWERS`), never opened empty. `dueOn` = today in `NOTIFICATIONS_TZ` plus `dueInWorkingDays` working days (open question 4). The item now waits on its pending reviewers (below). Each reviewer gets the "review requested" bell (and email by preference).
4. **A response.** Only the named reviewer, acting as a person, on the round's open step: the reviewer row is claimed with one `updateMany` naming `outcome: null`, `withdrawnAt: null` and the step `OPEN` (the inflect claim pattern); a lost claim is 409 `REVIEW_RESPONSE_TAKEN`, a step that is no longer open 409 `REVIEW_STEP_NOT_OPEN` (the stale-decision refusal). Then `stepOutcome`:
   - `ANY`: the first sign-off completes the step; the rest are withdrawn (`STEP_ENDED`).
   - `ALL`: the step completes when every reviewer not withdrawn has signed off **and at least one has** (zero is never complete).
   - A return returns the step (open question 9 decides whether at once or after everyone has answered).
   - A completed step opens the next; the last completes the round.
5. **A returned step** ends the round `RETURNED`; its other open and pending steps end `WITHDRAWN`. A new round (`number + 1`, `PLANNED`) is created at once with the same steps: names, modes, working days, named members and named lists, never the previous round's resolved people. Nothing in the returned round is rewritten or deleted. The item waits on its assignee. The new round opens when a person sends it again (open question 8).
6. **Skip.** An open or pending step may be skipped by a person who may (open question 3); it ends `SKIPPED` with who and why, never as a response, and the next step opens.
7. **A reviewer leaves.** `removeTenantMember` withdraws their pending reviewer rows (`MEMBER_REMOVED`), audited, in its transaction, before the membership row goes; then each affected open step is re-evaluated by `stepOutcome`. A step whose last remaining reviewer left with no sign-off does not complete: it ends `SKIPPED`, reason `NO_REVIEWERS_LEFT`, only if a person skips it; until then it stays open and is flagged "No reviewers left" on the item and in the assignee's bell (open question 3). Their removal from `WorkItemTypeReviewStepReviewer` cascades.
8. **The item closes or is deleted.** Every path into `DONE` or `CANCELLED` (single, bulk, board move, duplicate, a state's delete, the answer) ends an `OPEN` round `WITHDRAWN` (`ITEM_CLOSED`) in the same transaction, unless open question 2's gate refuses the move first; a `PLANNED` round stays `PLANNED`. The withdrawal is one function, `endOpenRoundOnClose`, called beside `stateTransitionEffects` by every state path; a guard pins the callers as `work-item-sequence-order.test.ts` pins side effects. A reopen does not reopen the round. A soft delete withdraws it (`ITEM_DELETED`); a restore does not reopen it.
9. **A move to another project** keeps the rounds; a reviewer who cannot see the new project stays asked (their read is refused until they can; 8.4's guests are the only case) — recorded as a known limit, revisited in 8.4.
10. **A type change** does not touch steps (the 2.4b rule for field sets).

## Waiting on

2.4b's column names one actor; a step names several. Recommended (open questions 5 and 6):

- **The column stays one actor.** `WorkItem.waitingOnUserId` keeps its meaning, its CHECK, its index, its lane, its filter and its transition record. 6.3's session reference (decision 106) sits beside it unchanged.
- **While a round is open, the item waits on the open step's pending reviewers**, read from the reviewer rows (`outcome IS NULL AND withdrawnAt IS NULL` in the `OPEN` step of the `OPEN` round). It is a read model, not a column: the detail's "Waiting on" shows the step's name and its pending people; the list's waiting-on column shows them (or "3 reviewers"); due reminders go to them through the review-due type (below); My Work shows the item under "To review". The `waitingOnId` filter, the "Waiting on me" section and the waiting-on lane keep reading the column, and a new filter `reviewerId` (with `me`, resolved on the server as `waitingOnId=me` is) reads the reviewer rows.
- **Opening a round clears the column**, in the same versioned write, audited `WORK_ITEM_WAITING_ON_CHANGED` with `reason: 'review_round_opened'`; the transition record notes the change, so 3.6 counts the time before it against whoever was named. **Ending a round (completed or returned) sets the column to the assignee** when there is one, the item is open and the assignee is an ACTIVE member, otherwise leaves it empty; audited with `reason: 'review_round_completed' | 'review_round_returned'`, and the 2.4b bell to the assignee is replaced by the review notifications (no second bell). A withdrawn round (the item closed) sets nothing: a closed item waits on nobody.
- **A person setting waiting on by hand while a round is open** is refused, 409 `REVIEW_ROUND_OPEN` (recommended), so there is one answer to "whose move" at a time; the person adds a reviewer, skips the step or withdraws the round instead (open question 6). The same rule binds 6.3's session reference when it arrives: the review owns "waiting on" while a round is open.
- **Decision 106 holds.** Nothing here makes anyone accountable: the assignee stays the one accountable person (ADR 0003 §1), and an agent session can never be a reviewer. A delegate's session that is `AWAITING_INPUT` during a round reads as waiting on the assignee only after the round ends; while it is open the reviewers are whose move it is.
- **3.6.** Time waiting per party reads the column's recorded changes and the reviewer rows (`openedAt` of the step to `respondedAt` or `withdrawnAt`). The 3.6 map names both sources; no transition column is added.

## Due dates in working days

- `addWorkingDays(dateOnly, n, calendar)` in `src/app-layer/domain/working-days.ts`: pure, date-only, property-tested (never lands on a non-working day; monotone in `n`; `n` working days ahead of a Friday is the next week's). Written fresh: inflect-compliance has none, and date-fns's `addBusinessDays` takes a `Date` and a time zone question that date-only values must never meet.
- Until 3.1 the calendar is Monday to Friday, no holidays (open question 4, recommendation A), held as a constant 3.1 replaces with the team's working calendar. `dueOn` is computed once when the step opens and stored; a later calendar does not move it.
- **Reminders.** `work-item-due-notification` (08:00 in `NOTIFICATIONS_TZ`) also rings each pending reviewer of an open step whose `dueOn` is today (window 0) or tomorrow's working day (window 1), and once when overdue, as `WORK_ITEM_REVIEW_DUE`; deduplicated per reviewer, step and window; ACTIVE members of a live workspace only (the liveness census). The daily digest (2.7c) lists a reviewer's open steps when they chose DAILY.

## Recipient lists as reviewers

- A step names members and lists; a list is resolved **when its step opens** (recommended, open question 7): its people at that moment become reviewer rows, through `resolveListRecipients` (the one reader; never `RecipientListMember` directly), with `viaRecipientListId` kept for display ("via Legal"). Until the step opens the list is shown by name and its current size.
- Why not follow the list as it changes (decision 139 copied for watchers; reviews differ in one way that argues for copying even harder): an `ALL` step needs a fixed set to be complete against, and a returned round must stay as it was. A person added to the list after the step opened is not asked; a person removed from the list but still a member stays asked (their membership, not the list, is what makes them able to respond).
- A list archived after it was named on a `PENDING` step resolves to nobody (the resolver's rule); the step then cannot open (`NO_REVIEWERS`) and the person sending the round is told which step and list.
- Default steps per type name workspace lists only; an item's step may name the workspace's lists and its project's.

## Agents never count as reviewers

- **What holds it now (2.8).** A reviewer row names a `User` with an ACTIVE membership when it is written (named, or resolved from a list); an agent has no `User` row and no membership (ADR 0003 §3), so it cannot be named. The respond usecase calls `assertActingAsPerson` (refuses an API key now; 6.1 adds the agent term, as its docblock says) and requires `ctx.userId` to equal the row's `userId`; the system user (`sys_<tenantId>`) holds no membership by CHECK and is refused by the same equality. No job responds: the reminder job and the round withdrawal write as the system user and never set `outcome` (a unit test reads every writer of `outcome` and pins it to the respond usecase alone, as the search write-seam guard pins its writers).
- **Who may never respond** is also written into the ADR (below) beside ADR 0003's "agents propose": an agent, an automation rule (5.1: "an automation never responds to a review step"), an API key, an import (5.5), and a person on someone else's behalf, an admin included.
- **What 6.2 adds, not 2.8.** A proposal kind `REVIEW_COMMENT`: a delegate drafts the text of a review comment for a named reviewer; approving it runs `addComment` as that reviewer and records **no** response (the reviewer then signs off or returns themselves). The kind is listed in this map so 6.2's map carries it; 2.8 builds no proposal, no agent path and no placeholder.

## Audit, notifications, keys and routes

### Audit

`logEvent` inside each transaction, `detailsJson.category: 'entity_lifecycle'`, never the response text or a step name a person typed:

| Action | When |
|---|---|
| `WORK_ITEM_REVIEW_STEPS_CHANGED` | Steps or reviewers of a `PLANNED` round, or `PENDING` steps of an open one, added, changed, reordered or removed (ids and changed fields). |
| `WORK_ITEM_REVIEW_ROUND_STARTED` | A round opened (round number, step count). |
| `WORK_ITEM_REVIEW_STEP_OPENED` | A step opened (step id, reviewer ids, lists resolved, people dropped and why, `dueOn`). |
| `WORK_ITEM_REVIEW_RESPONDED` | A response (step id, outcome, `itemVersionAt`; never the text). |
| `WORK_ITEM_REVIEW_STEP_SKIPPED` | A skip (by whom, reason). |
| `WORK_ITEM_REVIEW_ROUND_ENDED` | A round completed, returned or withdrawn (reason). |
| `WORK_ITEM_REVIEWERS_WITHDRAWN_ON_MEMBER_REMOVAL` | In `removeTenantMember` (count), beside the waiting-on clear. |
| `WORK_ITEM_TYPE_REVIEW_STEPS_SET` | A type's defaults replaced. |

Each response is also visible in the item's Activity (it merges `WORK_ITEM_*` entries), with the outcome and, for the people who may read the item, the response text read from the reviewer row, not from the audit entry.

### Notifications

| Type | To | Default |
|---|---|---|
| `WORK_ITEM_REVIEW_REQUESTED` | each reviewer when their step opens | bell on; email IMMEDIATE offered (joins `IMMEDIATE_EMAIL_TYPES`) |
| `WORK_ITEM_REVIEW_DUE` | pending reviewers on window 0, 1 and overdue (the daily job) | bell on; email DAILY |
| `WORK_ITEM_REVIEW_RETURNED` | the assignee and whoever sent the round | bell on; email IMMEDIATE offered |
| `WORK_ITEM_REVIEW_COMPLETED` | the assignee and whoever sent the round | bell on; email OFF |

### Keys (open question 13, recommendation A: no new domain)

### Routes

Under `/api/t/:slug/work-items/:id/review/` (`review` as a route segment is the item's sub-resource, not 6.2's page; open question 1 can rename it), each wrapped in `withApiErrorHandling`, each with a `route-permissions.ts` rule and a `tests/guards/tenant-crud-authz-coverage.test.ts` pin:

- `GET …/review` — the item's rounds, newest first, with steps, reviewers and responses (the response text decrypted for readers of the item).
- `PUT …/review/steps` — replace the planned round's steps (or the open round's `PENDING` steps) in one versioned write; `If-Match` on the round.
- `POST …/review/send` — open the planned round.
- `POST …/review/withdraw` — end the open round `WITHDRAWN` (`WITHDRAWN_BY_PERSON`), its steps not rewritten; a new `PLANNED` round with the same steps follows, as after a return.
- `POST …/review/steps/:stepId/respond` `{ outcome, text? }` — the reviewer's response.
- `POST …/review/steps/:stepId/skip` `{ reason? }` — skip.
- `DELETE …/review/steps/:stepId/reviewers/:userId` — withdraw a pending reviewer (`REMOVED_BY_PERSON`), through the undo toast.
- `GET`/`PUT /work-item-types/:typeId/review-steps` — a type's defaults.
- `GET /work-items?reviewerId=me` — the list filter (the existing route, a new key in `WORK_ITEM_FILTER_KEYS` and the saved-view `FilterKey`).

Every mutation input is a Zod schema in `src/app-layer/schemas/review.ts`; step names and response text go through `sanitizePlainText` in the usecase; `responseText` joins the encryption manifest and `tests/guards/sanitize-rich-text-coverage.test.ts`'s classified models.

## What makes the prompt harder than it reads

1. **Waiting on is one actor; a step is several.** Either 2.4b's column changes shape (and 6.3's CHECK with it) or waiting on gains a second, derived source. This map keeps the column and derives (open question 5).
2. **"Nothing is rewound or deleted" fights every ordinary edit path.** Steps are edited before a round, reviewers leave, items close, lists change and items are purged; each needs a rule that never touches an ended round, and a trigger so a forgotten path fails instead of rewriting history.
3. **"All must respond" over a set that can shrink.** A reviewer who leaves, a list that changes, a step that loses everyone: zero is never complete, and a step must never complete by an empty `every`.
4. **The comment.** A `Comment` can be edited and deleted (decision 53) and moderated by an admin, which would rewrite a closed round's record; a write-once text cannot be moderated (open question 10).
5. **Eight state paths** must all end an open round on close (and, with the gate, ask first); one forgotten path leaves reviewers waiting on a closed item. One function, called beside `stateTransitionEffects`, and a guard.
6. **Working days before a calendar exists**, and date-only values that must never meet a time zone.
7. **Agent exclusion has to hold by construction**, before agents exist, without building any agent code.

## ADR amendments (applied with decisions 148 to 165)

Recommended (open question 17, A): a new ADR, **0009 — Review steps**, which ADR 0002 §1, §3 and §14 point to, so ADR 0002 stays the model of one table and ADR 0009 carries the one feature with its own lifecycle.

### New: ADR 0009 — Review steps (draft)

> Status: Proposed (roadmap 2.8; owner decision 80)
>
> **Decision.**
> 1. A work item may carry review rounds; a round holds ordered review steps; a step names reviewers (members, or recipient lists resolved when it opens) and says whether all or any must sign off; it is due a number of working days after it opens. Steps run in order; reviewers within a step respond in parallel.
> 2. A reviewer responds once, Signed off or Returned, with text (required to Return). A response is a one-way stamp.
> 3. A Return ends the round. Its steps, reviewers and responses stay as they were, read-only, held by a trigger; a new round with the same steps follows. Nothing is rewound or deleted.
> 4. While a round is open the item waits on the open step's pending reviewers; ADR 0002 §14's column is cleared when it opens and names the assignee when it ends. A person does not set waiting on by hand while a round is open.
> 5. An open round never makes a reviewer accountable for the item: the assignee stays the one accountable person (ADR 0003 §1).
> 6. **Only people review.** A reviewer is a person with an active membership, responding as themselves. An agent, an automation rule, an API key, an import, the system user, and a person acting for another (an admin included) never respond to a step and never count towards one. A delegate may at most draft a review comment as a proposal (roadmap 6.2) that the named reviewer approves as their own comment; approving it records no response. This stands beside ADR 0003 §4's "agents propose, people approve" and is not changed by any autonomy rung.
> 7. Default steps belong to a work item type across the workspace and are copied onto an item when it is created; changing them changes no item.
> 8. A type may require its review to finish before an item moves to Done; otherwise review is an indicator. Cancelling is never held.
> 9. A review step is not a proposal and does not use the proposal queue (6.2): a proposal reviews an agent's change, a review step reviews a person's work.
>
> **Consequences.** Every state path ends an open round on close; member removal withdraws pending reviewers; the purge removes rounds with the item. Conditional steps arrive with 5.1's conditions. Working days are Monday to Friday until 3.1's calendars.

## Open questions for the owner

Each gives options with the recommendation first and a one-line trade-off. 1–4 are the roadmap's own, refined; 5–18 are new.

**The roadmap's four**

1. **Words.** A. Review step / Round / Reviewer / outcomes **Signed off** and **Returned** (verbs Sign off, Return); My Work "To review". *Recommended*: plain, no collision with Approve, Reject, Accept, Decline, Workflow or the proposal page's "Review". B. Sign-off step / Round / Reviewer / Cleared and Sent back: "sign-off" says what is wanted, but "Cleared" reads as erased. C. Review step / Revision / Looks good and Changes needed: friendly; "Revision" suggests the item's content was versioned, and the outcomes read as chat, not a record. *Decided: A (148): Review step, Round, Reviewer, Signed off and Returned, "To review".*
2. **Gate or indicator.** A. Indicator by default; a type may require review to finish before Done (`REVIEW_INCOMPLETE`); Cancelled never held; closing withdraws an open round. *Recommended*: the gate where a team wants it, one more check on every state path. B. Indicator only: simplest; nothing stops unreviewed work reaching Done. C. A gate whenever an item has steps: strict; every board drag of a reviewed item can fail. *Decided: A (149): an indicator by default, a type may require review before Done (2.8c), cancelling never held, closing withdraws an open round.*
3. **Who may change, skip and close steps.** A. Anyone with `work_items.edit` edits a planned round and an open round's pending steps; skipping a step, or closing one whose reviewers left, takes the item's assignee, whoever sent the round, or an admin (`admin.manage`), audited. *Recommended*: the people responsible for the item can unblock it; others can only add work. B. Anyone with `work_items.edit` may also skip: fewer stuck items, but a review anyone can skip asserts little. C. Admins only skip and close: strongest record; every departed reviewer waits for an admin. *Decided: A (150).*
4. **Working days before 3.1.** A. Monday to Friday, no holidays, computed in `NOTIFICATIONS_TZ` when a step opens and stored. *Recommended*: predictable; 3.1's calendars replace the constant for new steps only. B. A workspace setting of working weekdays now: right for a Sunday–Thursday week; a setting 3.1 then has to absorb. C. Calendar days until 3.1: simplest; "5 days" over a weekend is 3 working days. *Decided: A (151).*

**Waiting on**

5. **How waiting on holds several reviewers.** A. The column stays one actor; while a round is open the item waits on the open step's pending reviewers, read from the reviewer rows and shown wherever waiting on is shown; the column is cleared when the round opens and names the assignee when it completes or is returned; My Work gets "To review" and a `reviewerId` filter. *Recommended*: 2.4b's column, lane, CHECK and 6.3's plan stay as they are. B. One union read: `waitingOnId` and "Waiting on me" also match pending reviewers; no new section; lanes cannot place a card with two people. C. Waiting on becomes a set (a table replacing the column): one model, but rewrites 2.4b, its transition record and decision 106's CHECK. D. The column names the step's next pending reviewer in turn: no new reads, but an "all" step is no longer parallel. *Decided: A (152). The API filter `reviewerId` lands in 2.8a; My Work's "To review" in 2.8b.*
6. **Waiting on set by hand while a round is open.** A. Refused, 409 `REVIEW_ROUND_OPEN`; the same rule binds 6.3's session reference. *Recommended*: one answer to "whose move" at a time. B. Allowed, shown beside the reviewers, and replaced by the assignee when the round ends: flexible ("waiting on the supplier"), two answers at once. C. Allowed and kept when the round ends (the assignee is named only if the column is empty): the hand value wins, but a stale one can outlive the review. *Decided: A (153).*

**Steps and lists**

7. **Recipient lists as reviewers.** A. Resolved when the step opens: its people then become reviewers ("via Legal"); later list changes ask nobody new. *Recommended*: an "all" step has a fixed set, and an ended round stays as it was. B. Followed while the step is open: a person added to the list is asked at once; "all" chases a moving set, and a removal from the list silently drops a reviewer. C. Resolved when the step is added to the item: simplest; a list changed between adding and opening asks the wrong people. *Decided: A (154).*
8. **When a round starts, and when the next one after a Return starts.** A. Explicitly: "Send for review" by anyone with `work_items.edit`; after a Return the new round waits until it is sent again. *Recommended*: the assignee revises first; reviewers are not asked about unchanged work. B. Round 1 on creation, and the next round at once after a Return: no click, but reviewers are asked before there is anything to review. C. On a move into a chosen workflow state (such as the seeded "In review"): fits teams that use that state, but couples review to a team's workflow, which ADR 0002 §3 keeps apart. *Decided: A (155).*
9. **What one Return does in an "all" step.** A. Returns the step at once; the round ends and the others' pending responses are withdrawn. *Recommended*: the assignee hears at once and nobody reviews work already sent back. B. The step waits for every reviewer, then returns if any returned: all comments arrive in one round, but a known return waits on the slowest reviewer. *Decided: A (156). In an "any" step the first response decides it either way (ADR 0009 §3).*
10. **The response's text.** A. Its own encrypted, write-once text on the reviewer row, required to Return, optional to sign off; shown in the review section and Activity; not searched, no mentions; an admin cannot edit or remove it. *Recommended*: the round's record cannot change. B. A `Comment` linked from the response and locked like the marked answer (decision 113): searchable and mentionable; an admin moderating it rewrites a closed round. C. As A, plus an admin may redact the text (the trigger admits only setting it to a "removed by an admin" marker, audited): moderation with an honest trace, one more exception in the trigger. *Decided: A (157), with its mutability following decision 162.*
11. **Who may be a reviewer.** A. Any ACTIVE member who sees the project, Viewers included (responding is not editing the item); the item's assignee may be named too. *Recommended*: stakeholders without edit rights can sign off; the step says who is asked. B. As A, but the assignee may not respond to a step on their own item (inflect's separation of duties): stronger sign-offs; a one-person team cannot review its own work. C. Only roles holding `work_items.edit`: no Viewer reviewers; a read-only stakeholder cannot sign off. *Decided: **B (158), not the recommendation:** any active member who sees the project may be a reviewer, Viewers included, but the item's assignee may not respond to a step on their own item. Refused at both points: naming the assignee when steps are edited (`REVIEWER_IS_ASSIGNEE`) and responding; a list's resolution drops the assignee; an assignee change that makes a pending reviewer the assignee withdraws their row (`BECAME_ASSIGNEE`), audited.*
12. **Default steps per type.** A. Per type across the workspace (`admin.manage`), naming members and workspace lists; copied by every create path, including template sub-items, starter items, intake accept and repeat copies, never opened. *Recommended*: the prompt's "set by an admin", one place, the 2.4b rule for machine-made items. B. Per type and project (`projects.edit`), able to name project lists: closer to the people who review; a type behaves differently per project. C. As A, but machine-made items (repeats, intake) get no steps: fewer surprise reviews; a recurring item that needs sign-off each time loses it. *Decided: A (159; built in 2.8c).*
13. **Keys.** A. No new domain: steps and sending under `work_items.edit`, responding under `work_items.view` plus being named, defaults under `admin.manage`. *Recommended*: fits decisions 58 and 104; a custom role controls it through the existing keys. B. A new `reviews` domain (`reviews.respond`, `reviews.manage`, `reviews.skip`): finer for custom roles; one more domain in every role, the permission set and the matrix. *Decided: A (160).*

**Notifications and records**

14. **Notifications.** A. Four types (review requested, review due, returned, completed); immediate email offered for requested and returned, default immediate for requested; due through the 08:00 job (windows 0, 1 and overdue). *Recommended*: a reviewer hears at once and is reminded; widens decision 132's immediate list by two. B. Reuse `WORK_ITEM_WAITING_ON` for the request and `WORK_ITEM_DUE` for reminders: no new preferences; a reviewer cannot mute reviews apart from waiting on. C. As A without immediate email (daily digest only): fewer emails; a reviewer may learn of a request a day late. *Decided: A (161): requested and returned default to immediate email, due to the daily digest, completed off.*
15. **Changing a response.** A. Never: a response is a one-way stamp; a reviewer who signed off by mistake asks for the step to be returned or a new round. *Recommended*: the record is exactly what was said. B. Changeable until the step completes (each change audited, the last counts): forgiving; a step can flip after others read it. *Decided: **B (162), not the recommendation:** a response may change until its step completes (by every required sign-off, a Return, a skip or a withdrawal); then the step and its responses are frozen, and an ended round is never rewritten. The trigger refuses any change to a reviewer row of a completed step or an ended round (the key-rotation re-wrap and the purge excepted); a change while open is audited with ids, never text, under the round's lock.*
16. **What a sign-off covers.** A. The item stays editable during a round; each response records the item's version (`itemVersionAt`), and the review section marks "changed since signed off". *Recommended*: honest without blocking work. B. Title and description are locked while a round is open (409 `REVIEW_ROUND_OPEN`): a sign-off certifies exactly what was read; edits wait for a Return or Withdraw. *Decided: A (163).*
