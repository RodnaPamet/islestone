# Analytics

Sub-phase 3.6a: the transition record's `fromStateCategory`, the measures and the Analytics page; sub-phase 3.6b: time waiting; sub-phase 3.6c: forecasting and its calibration (see "Forecasting", below). The decisions are owner decisions 309 to 334 (`docs/HANDOFF.md`), recorded in [ADR 0014](adr/0014-analytics-and-forecasting.md) and the porting map [`docs/porting/maps/3.6.md`](porting/maps/3.6.md), whose "Rules no decision names (3.6a, as built)", "(3.6b, as built)" and "(3.6c, as built)" list the choices the build made. Dashboards (3.6d) are not built yet.

The words are ADR 0006's (decision 309): Analytics, Throughput, Cycle time, Lead time, Age, Cumulative flow, Burn-up, Time waiting, Dashboard, Widget; "Lead time" and "Cycle time" are always written whole. Never Velocity, Burn-down, Insights, Reports, Stats, Confidence, Projection, Card, Stage or Pipeline for these, and never Idle time, Blocked time, Response time or Wait (as a model) for time waiting.

## The record is the source

Every measure reads `WorkItemTransition` (decision 310), never a stored series; nothing computed is stored, cached or audited. Each row is an item's picture right after an insert or a change of state, cycle, milestone, project, deletion, waiting on or estimates, written only by the SECURITY DEFINER trigger `work_item_transition_record`; `app_user` holds SELECT alone, and rows outlive the purge (decision 66).

- **`fromStateCategory`** (migration `20261226120000_transition_from_state_category`): the item's state category before the change, `OLD."stateCategory"` on an update and NULL on the insert row. The trigger writes it; the migration backfilled existing rows once with `LAG("stateCategory")` in the record's order (`at`, then `id`), an item's first row keeping NULL. The trigger's `UPDATE OF` list is unchanged.
- **A completion is one row**: `"stateCategory" = 'DONE' AND "fromStateCategory" IS DISTINCT FROM 'DONE'` (an insert straight into Done counts at creation). A reopen is a row from Done to another category; a deletion, a restore, a move or an estimate change of a Done item stays Done and is not a completion.
- **The partial index** `WorkItemTransition_completions` on `("tenantId", "projectId", "at")` with exactly that predicate serves throughput and durations. Prisma cannot declare it (the drift check does not see it); a query spells the predicate exactly. `tests/integration/analytics-record.test.ts` holds the column, the trigger body and the index.
- **The only module with SQL against the record** is `WorkItemTransitionRepository` (the cycle burn-up's two reads moved into it from `CycleRepository`).

## Definitions

The pure, client-safe module `src/app-layer/domain/analytics/` holds every rule; the SQL reads implement the same rules, each with a property test against the pure module on random histories (reopens, deletions and restores, cancels, moves, inserts into Done, a deleted project, ties at one instant) in five zones.

- **A picture at an instant** is the item's latest row at or before it, ties broken by the row's id (`record.ts`).
- **Completion** (decision 311; `completion.ts`). An item is completed as of a moment `R` when its picture at `R` is in Done and not deleted; it counts once, on the day of its last entry into Done at or before `R`. A reopened item counts at its later completion; a deletion retracts it from every past day and a restore returns it; a purged item (its last row its deletion) never counts; Cancelled is never completed. A read sees only rows at or before `R`, so a past week's throughput can change after a reopen or a deletion: history is shown as it stands.
- **Cycle time** (decision 312; `durations.ts`) runs from an item's first entry into Started (at or before its completion) to its completion; an item done without starting has none and is counted as "Done without starting". **Lead time** runs from its creation (its first row) to its completion. **Age** is an open item's time in Started now, from its first entry into Started to the moment of reading.
- **Elapsed working time** (decision 313; `elapsed.ts`): the time between two instants less every whole non-working day (a weekend day or a holiday of the item's own team's calendar, else the workspace's) lying inside the span, each removed at its own length (23 or 25 hours across an offset change), in days of 24 hours to one decimal. A day only partly inside the span is never removed, so the result is never negative. Friday 16:00 to Monday 10:00 on a Monday-to-Friday calendar is 18 hours, 0.8 days to one decimal. Working days come from the scheduling module (`isWorkingDay`); a `WorkingTimeMeter` keeps a running sum per calendar so thousands of durations cost one walk of the year.
- **Percentiles** are nearest rank everywhere (`percentiles.ts`): the value at index `ceil(p × n) − 1` of the sorted values, so every value shown happened.
- **Throughput** (`throughput.ts`): completions per day in the scope's zone, summed per ISO week (Monday to Sunday); the median is over whole weeks.
- **Cumulative flow** (`flow.ts`): at the end of each day (now for today) the items in scope by category: Backlog, Unstarted and Started at that instant, Done counted only for items that entered Done on or after the window's first day; Cancelled and deleted items out.
- **The burn-up for any scope**: scope (every in-scope item, Done included), started (Started or Done), completed (Done), in items, or in points or hours when every team in scope estimates in that unit (3.4a's `burnUpMeasuresFor`, intersected; ADR 0012: never mixed or converted), unestimated items counted and named.

The flow and the burn-up are one event sweep (`flowSweep`): each item's rows are walked once and every change of bucket or weight becomes a −1 and a +1 on its day; a running sum gives each day's end. 2.5a's cycle burn-up is re-expressed over it; its per-day scan is kept in `tests/unit/domain/analytics-flow.test.ts` as the oracle of a property test. **The instant of midnight belongs to the day it begins**, for throughput and flow alike; 2.5a's scan also counted a row at exactly that instant in the day it ended, and nothing else changed.

## Time waiting (3.6b)

Owner decisions 76, 313, 317 and 329 to 332; `domain/analytics/waiting.ts` (pure, property-tested) and `usecases/analytics-waiting.ts`. Time waiting measures how long items waited on each person: the one person named in Waiting on, and the reviewers of an open review step.

- **A field wait** is a run of an item's consecutive recorded rows naming the same person in `waitingOnUserId` while the item is live and in the read's scope. It starts at the run's first row and ends at the next row naming someone else or no one, at the item's deletion, when it leaves the scope (a move to a project outside it or out of sight), or runs on at the moment of reading `R`. A close always ends one (closing clears waiting on, and 2.4b-a's CHECK refuses a closed item that names someone); a reopen restores nothing, so it starts none. A restore of an item that still names the person starts a new wait. A wait of no length that ended (two rows at one instant) is no wait.
- **A review wait** (decision 317): while a review step is open, each reviewer asked waits from the step's opening (or from being added to the open step, when later) until their latest response, their withdrawal or the step's end, read from the review rows (`WorkItemReviewStep`, `WorkItemReviewer`; never rewritten once ended, ADR 0009 §4). It counts only while the item is live and in scope, judged on the record at each instant like a field wait. These cover exactly the time waiting on is cleared (decisions 152 and 153). One person's overlapping spans on one item would be merged (the review rows never give one person two open steps of an item at once).
- **Measured** as elapsed working time on the item's own calendar (decision 313), the stretches of one wait on one team merged first, so a weekend inside a wait is removed even when the record has a row in it (an estimate changed on a Saturday). Over the window, each wait counts its overlap with the window; a wait's length is the whole wait.
- **Per party**: the time waited in the window and how much of it as a reviewer ("Waited on Ana 3.5 days, 2 of them as a reviewer"), the items waited on (a wait reaching into the window), the waits that ended in the window with their median and 85th percentile length (nearest rank, whole waits), and the items waiting on them at `R`, the oldest ten listed with their age (at most 2,000 listed across the rows shown, `waitingNowCut` when cut). A wait wholly before the window gives no row. An active or deactivated member is named from the membership; every removed member's waits are one row, "Former members", never a name or an id.
- **Totals across everyone**: the time waited (summed over parties), of it as a reviewer, the open item time in the window (live, in scope, Unstarted or Started; Backlog time is out of both the share's denominator and its numerator, decision 336), the time open items spent waiting on someone (each instant of an item once, whoever it waited on: two reviewers at once count the item once) and its share of open item time, the items, the ended waits' figures and the items waiting now. Elapsed working time is not monotone in its span (a whole weekend inside a longer span is removed; one cut by a shorter span's end is not), so the waiting time is held to the open time and the share never exceeds one.
- **Who sees what** (decisions 329 and 335): every holder of `analytics.view` reads their own row (zeros when nobody waited on them); every party's row, theirs included and Former members, and the totals only with `analytics.people` (Owners and Admins by default; a custom role may grant or remove it), in any scope: in a small scope the totals less one's own row would be another person's figure. Without the key the answer has no `people` and no `totals` member at all, and other parties are never looked up or named. A caller who is not a person, an API key (5.2), the system user or an agent (6.x), reads no row and no totals, only the answer's frame; nothing is read for it (decisions 332 and 335, `readsWaitingAsPerson`). Waits are grouped by whom an item waited on, never by its assignee (decision 330).

## What counts

| Case | Rule |
|---|---|
| Parents and sub-items | Each item counts once (decision 314), as progress counts. A type filter narrows to one level. |
| Moved to another project | Judged by the recorded project at each instant (decision 315): counted in the old project before the move and the new one after; a completion counts in the project it was completed in. |
| Archived projects | Their history counts; their open items are out of the figures about now (open, started, age) unless the list's "include archived" is set, as on the list. |
| Deleted projects | Never counted (their items are deleted with them). |
| Projects the caller cannot see | Never counted, now or in history: a row counts only while its recorded project is visible (before 8.4 every role but Guest sees every live project). |
| Items before 2.5a | Counted from their first recorded row; one with no Started row has no age. |

## Filters

The reads take the list's filters through `resolveListFiltersInTx`, never a second filter path (decision 315).

- **Set aside**: the state, category and due filters describe status, which each measure owns. A read ignores them and names those set in `setAside`; the page does not offer them.
- **Recorded fields** are judged on each row as recorded: project, team, cycle, milestone, and an initiative through its projects' current links (judged against each row's recorded project).
- **Every other filter judges current values**: type, labels, assignee, waiting on, priority, custom fields, goal, created by, reviewer, watching, `q`. The usecase resolves them to the items they select now, deleted ones included (a deletion is judged by the record), at most 50,000 (`ANALYTICS_FILTER_ITEM_LIMIT`; above, 422 `ANALYTICS_TOO_LARGE` with `what: 'filter'`), and the record is read for those items only. A purged item has no current values, so such a filter excludes it. "Label is Bug" counts the items labelled Bug now, at every past instant; the page says so.
- **No measure is grouped by assignee** (decision 330); an "assignee is" filter still narrows any chart.

## Days and zones

Day boundaries are the scope's working calendar's zone (decision 316), by the calendar view's rule: one team, project, cycle or milestone, that team's calendar; anything wider, the workspace's. A calendar's zone defaults to `NOTIFICATIONS_TZ`. "Today" is read in that zone through `todayClock`; a window ends no later than today, and the moment of reading is the earlier of now and the end of the window's last day. Durations use each item's own calendar. Dates are epoch days, never passed through a zone. **The cycle burn-up** (`GET /cycles/:id/burn-up`) cuts its days in its team's calendar zone too, so the cycle page and an Analytics burn-up of the same cycle agree.

## Reads, routes and keys

**The `analytics` permission domain** (decision 329): `view` (every analytics read over the items one can see; from 3.6c forecasts; from 3.6d one's own dashboards and shared ones), `share` (shared dashboards, 3.6d), `people` (time waiting per person beyond one's own row, 3.6b). Owners and Admins hold all three, Members `view` and `share`, Viewers `view`, a Guest nothing (decision 204). `view` guards every route; since 3.6b `people` decides, in the usecase, whether time waiting answers other people's rows and the totals (decision 335); `share` guards nothing until 3.6d. A custom role stored before 3.6 takes its base role's analytics keys. Pages read `analyticsAffordances` (`analytics.policies.ts`) on the server.

| Route | Answer | Bounds |
|---|---|---|
| `GET /analytics/counts?<filters>&from&to` | Open and started items now; completed and created in the window (created: an item whose first row lies in the window, its recorded scope then, live now) | the window |
| `GET /analytics/throughput?<filters>&from&to&bin=week\|day` | The bins (`start`, `end`, `count`, `whole`), the total, the median whole bin | 50,000 completions |
| `GET /analytics/durations?<filters>&from&to&measure=cycle\|lead` | Cycle time and lead time percentiles over every completion, "Done without starting", the newest 2,000 completions as points (key, title, day, both durations), and for cycle time the oldest 2,000 open, started items' age with their total | 50,000 completions |
| `GET /analytics/flow?<filters>&from&to&burnUp=0\|1&measure=items\|points\|hours` | Cumulative flow per day (per ISO week's last day beyond 92 days), the items followed, and with `burnUp=1` the burn-up and the measures the scope offers (`MEASURE_UNAVAILABLE` otherwise) | 20,000 items (every item with a row in scope up to the window's end) |
| `GET /analytics/waiting?<filters>&from&to` (3.6b) | The caller's own row (`self`), and with `analytics.people` the totals and every party's row (`totals`, `people`, absent otherwise; decision 335), the items followed | 20,000 items, as the flow; 50,000 reviewers' spans in the window (`what: 'reviews'`) |

Every route is `requirePermission('analytics.view')` with a rule in `route-permissions.ts`; none is under `/admin/`. A window is at most 366 days, inclusive, and never reversed (400 `ANALYTICS_RANGE`). Over a bound the answer is a 422 `ANALYTICS_TOO_LARGE` with `{ what, count, limit }`, never a cut answer. Every answer carries its frame: the window, today, the zone, the calendar's team and the filters set aside. Reads only, never audited; no description, comment, field value or review response is read, and the points' and the waiting-now items' keys and titles are read by id under row-level security.

Time waiting reads in two statements beside the scope's: `ReviewRepository.reviewSpans` (the review rows' one seam, 2.8a: a window's open steps on `WorkItemReviewStep_tenantId_status_dueOn_idx` and those that ended in or after it on `WorkItemReviewStep_tenantId_endedAt_idx`, migration `20261227120000_review_step_ended_index`, with their reviewers), then `WorkItemTransitionRepository.waitSpans`: in one statement the items followed (the flow's rule, the count alone above the bound) and of them the segments the pure module needs: each live, in-scope row to its next row (or `R`), with its position among the item's rows, when it names someone in waiting on, is open and reaches into the window, or belongs to an item with a review span. The pure module's `relevantSegments` is the same rule; a property test holds that it gives the same figures as every segment, and `tests/integration/analytics-waiting.test.ts` holds the SQL to it on random histories.

## The Analytics page

The **Analytics** sidebar entry (Workspace section, `analytics.view`; Viewers too, never a guest) opens `/analytics`: the counts and five fixed charts over a scope set with the list's shared filter toolbar (the status filters not offered) and a window of the last 4, 8, 12, 26 or 52 weeks (12 by default, ending today in the workspace calendar's zone). Throughput as weekly bars and cumulative flow as stacked areas on the mirrored chart platform; cycle time as a scatter of the newest completions with the 50th, 85th and 95th percentile lines and open items' age on today's column, and lead time as a distribution in whole working days, both small visx components on the same tokens; the burn-up through the cycle page's `BurnUpChart` with its measure switch. Below them, **Time waiting** (3.6b, `TimeWaitingChart`): the totals in its summary line when the server gives them, and a bar per party the server gave, split into waiting on and as a reviewer, with the table of the same figures and of what waits now; a reader without `analytics.people` sees their own row, zeros included, and a sentence saying other people's figures and the totals are shown to those allowed to see them (decision 335). The component draws the rows it was given and never asks who may see what. Every chart has its table (the accessible fallback, always in the document), is its own read (`src/components/analytics/data/use-analytics.ts`), and says when it is over its bound, with the count, while the others draw. From 3.6d these charts are the dashboard's default composition.

## Forecasting

Sub-phase 3.6c (owner decisions 318 to 325; ADR 0014 §3 to §5). "When will these open items be done?" A forecast gives the dates by which a scope's open items are **50, 85 and 95 % likely** to be finished (never "confidence", ADR 0006), the chance of finishing by the scope's due date, the distribution and a "Chance finished by" curve, or a refusal with its counts. Schedules and forecasts come from code (principle 4): nothing is stored, cached, audited or logged per scope, and no description, comment, field value or estimate is read.

### The scope and the history

- **The scope** is the list's filters (`resolveListFiltersInTx`; the status filters set aside and named, as every analytics read does). Its **remaining** items are its live, open (Backlog, Unstarted, Started) items in visible, unarchived projects, counted now; units are items only (decision 320).
- **Whose completions** (decision 321): those of the projects the remaining items belong to (a cycle's: its team's), named on the answer and changeable to a subset of them, to the team when every scope project is one team's (a cycle: its own), or to the workspace. A team asked for a scope over two teams, or a history project outside the scope, is 400 `FORECAST_HISTORY`. A team's history is its rows' recorded team; its first recorded row is read over the team's current projects.
- **The window** (decision 318): the last 4, 8, 12 (the default) or 26 weeks ending yesterday (today's partial day is left out), never starting before the history's first recorded row.
- **The sample**: the completions (decision 311, as of now, through the 3.6a completions read) on each working day of the scope's calendar in the window, in date order. A completion on a non-working day is added to the next working day in the window, or, at the window's end, to its last working day: nothing is lost.
- **The calendar and today** follow decision 316: one team, project, cycle or milestone, the team's calendar; anything wider, the workspace's; today in its zone through `todayClock`.
- **The share** (decision 322): all of that throughput unless the person picks 75, 50 or 25 %; no new items are assumed. Both are printed with the dates.
- **The due date**: a cycle's end, a milestone's, goal's, initiative's or (with none of those) one project's due date. A due date of today or earlier has passed (the forecast's first day is tomorrow) and gets no chance.

### The simulation

`forecastCompletion` (`src/app-layer/domain/forecast/simulate.ts`), pure and client-safe (no database, clock, zone or environment):

1. **Refusals first** (decision 319), each returning no date: nothing open ("Nothing left to forecast: no open items."), fewer than 20 completions in the window, fewer than 20 working days of recorded history (each sentence names the counts), and a 50th percentile past the cap ("No forecast within two years at the recent pace"). Below 50 completions the answer carries "Based on few items; the range is wide."
2. **10,000 trials.** Each trial walks the working days after today; on each it draws one day of the sample uniformly and subtracts its count times the share, in whole hundredths of an item (a 50 % share of 3 is 150), so no floating-point sum enters a count. A trial ends on the day nothing is left, or beyond the cap of 520 working days (about two years).
3. **Random numbers**: mulberry32 (`rng.ts`, copied from inflect-compliance), each trial its own stream seeded by the forecast's seed and the trial's number (`trialSeed`, murmur3's finaliser), so with one seed more remaining items never finish earlier and a larger count never later.
4. **The seed** (decision 323) is FNV-1a (32-bit) over the canonical JSON of the scope's key (its filters, the history's projects or team, the window), the day it is read on, the sample, the remaining count, the share and the trial count (`seed.ts`). The same data on the same day gives the same dates on every reload and machine; it is shown under "How this was computed".
5. **Percentiles** are nearest rank over the outcomes, counted per day (no sort). A percentile beyond the cap is "Later than" the cap's date. **Dates** are the `d`-th working day after today (`addWorkingDays`), holidays ahead skipped (ADR 0010 §1). **The chance by the due date** is the share of trials finished on or before it, shown as a whole percent that reads 0 or 100 only when none or all finished.
6. **The distribution** is a histogram of finishing days, by ISO week when the first and last lie more than 92 days apart, and the cumulative curve; trials beyond the cap are counted, not drawn.

The worst case (10,000 trials to the cap, 5.2 million draws) is about 20 ms in Node (`tests/unit/domain/forecast-performance.test.ts`, budget 150 ms, failing at four times it). The trial loop reads `Math.imul` and `Math.floor` through module constants: a global read in a sandboxed context (Jest's `vm`) is about twenty times slower.

### The read

`GET /analytics/forecast?<the list's filters>&historyWeeks=4|8|12|26&share=25|50|75|100&basedOn=projects|team|workspace&historyProjectIds=<ids>` (`analytics.view`; `getForecast` in `usecases/analytics-forecast.ts`). A refusal is a 200 with `refused: { reason, … }` and no dates; more than 50,000 completions in the history is 422 `ANALYTICS_TOO_LARGE` (`what: 'completions'`). The answer carries the frame (today, the zone, the calendar's team, the filters set aside), the scope's kind and due date, the remaining count, what it is based on and what else is offered, the window with its completions and working days, the share, the trials and the seed, the dates, the chance, the histogram, the curve and the scope's own record. Eight statements a read (a cycle's nine).

### Calibration

**The back-test** (`backtest.ts`, pure; decision 325, H3): from weekly origins (the first working day of each ISO week, from the first day with a full window), forecast "the date of the next N completions" for N of 10, 25 and 50 with the history as it stood when the origin began (completions as `completionSpans`: a reopen or a deletion after the origin is not seen), and score it against the day the N-th completion from the origin on happened, as the record stands now. Each percentile is scorable at an origin when the actual day is known, or when its date has passed without it (a miss); a hit is an actual day on or before the date; each rate has its scorable count and a 95 % Wilson band. Actuals never feed a forecast.

**The scope's own record** (decision 325): beside every forecast that gives dates, the same question replayed over the last 52 weeks from the same history, window and calendar, at the whole share for the history's own next `ceil(remaining × 100 / share)` completions (a forecast of R items at share s is the forecast of R × 100 / s of the history's), 2,000 trials an origin. Shown with at least 10 scorable origins of the 85th percentile ("Of 34 earlier forecasts here, the 85% date held 29 times"); below that, "Not enough history to show how forecasts held here"; more than 20,000 completed items in its 64 weeks, not shown (the forecast still is).

**Published** (H3): `npm run forecast:calibration -- --shape <10k|100k> --write` runs the back-test over every project of the seeded workspace (`backtestWorkspaceForecasts`, `usecases/forecast-calibration.ts`) and writes the table below. `tests/perf/forecast-calibration.test.ts` holds the 85 % date's hit rate, pooled over the three sizes, between **80 and 90 %** over at least 200 scorable origins on the 10k seed, and the percentiles ordered; the band is never widened to pass. The seed's history is synthetic and non-stationary on purpose (growth across the year, a slow quarter, a holiday dip), so the forecasts run late at the 50th percentile; the real calibration is 9.1's weekly review of real workspaces.

### Where forecasts appear

A **Forecast** section on a project's, a milestone's, a cycle's (beside the burn-up), a goal's and an initiative's page, and a **Forecast** action in the Work items page's and a project page's toolbar that opens the sheet for the current filters (decision 324), each offered on the server to a caller `mayViewAnalytics` admits. The section shows the three dates, the chance by the due date, what it is based on and assumes, and the caution or the refusal (on a project's and a milestone's page, one line under the filter toolbar: the dates and the chance, or the refusal, so the board keeps its height); "Details" opens the sheet with the window, share and basis a person may change, the distribution and the curve each with its table, and "How this was computed" with the seed and the record. The charts are inflect-compliance's ALE histogram (its accessible buckets) and loss-exceedance curve, copied and adapted (`ForecastHistogram`, `ChanceByDateCurve`; `@visx/curve` 4.0.0). Copy is `forecast.*`.

### Agents

An agent reads a forecast only through 6.4's read tools within its authority, and states no date the module did not compute (decision 332, ADR 0003 §4); 7.5's explanation reads the result and its inputs (the sample, the share, the seed).

## Privacy

Time waiting per person is the one figure about a person (decision 76), a measure of how quickly someone responds: each person sees their own row, and others' rows and the totals only `analytics.people` holders (decisions 329 and 335, the totals because in a small scope they less one's own row are another person's figure); removed members are one anonymous row. No measure is grouped by assignee (decision 330). An agent never reads per-person time waiting, whatever its authorising person holds (decision 332): the read gives a caller that is not a person no row and no totals (decision 335). Nothing new about people is stored: waits are computed from the record (kept for the workspace's life, decision 66) and the review rows on every read, never cached, logged or audited. Logs carry ids and counts only, never a person's figures, a scope's numbers or filter values.

## Performance

Live reads with stated bounds (decision 331); the forecast's reads are in [`docs/performance.md`](performance.md), "Forecasting (3.6c)"; pre-aggregation is the fallback only if the nightly 100k run misses the flow budget. The plan checks, statements per request and latency scenarios are in [`docs/performance.md`](performance.md), "Analytics (3.6a)" and "Time waiting (3.6b)".

The scale seed writes a back-dated year into the record (`scripts/seed-scale/history.ts`): every item is created, leaves the backlog, is mostly estimated after creation, may wait on a member (about 30 %, a quarter of those twice), joins its cycle, is started, goes to review (about 60 % of done items, a quarter of those sent back once), is reopened once (about 8 % of done items) and moves from another project (about 3 %), and ends on its current picture, which the verifier checks for every item, with each row's `fromStateCategory` its item's previous row's category. About 44,000 rows per 10k workspace. Closings follow a non-stationary year (growth across the year, a slow quarter, a holiday dip in late December), so a back-test (3.6c) is not calibrated by construction.
