# Calendar sync

Roadmap 3.3b and 3.3c ([ADR 0011](adr/0011-external-calendar-sync.md); owner decisions 225 to 244 in [`docs/HANDOFF.md`](HANDOFF.md); the porting map [`docs/porting/maps/3.3.md`](porting/maps/3.3.md)). A member puts their open items and their projects' milestones into their own Google or Outlook calendar. Islestone is the source of truth: it pushes, and reads back exactly one thing, a day the member moved on one of their item events, applied as their own edit (3.3c). The calendar view is a separate thing ([`docs/work-items.md`](work-items.md), "The calendar view (3.3a)"); "Calendar" alone is the view's word, and the sync's things are a Calendar connection, a Synced event and the Calendar sync settings (ADR 0006).

## Who and when

- **The operator's switch.** `CALENDAR_SYNC=1` opens it; `0` closes it; unset, closed in production and open otherwise (`isCalendarSyncOpen`, `src/lib/calendar-sync/switch.ts`). Closed, every calendar sync route answers 404 before its permission check and no calendar job does anything. Production refuses `CALENDAR_SYNC=1` without a complete sign-in app pair, in `src/env.ts` and at startup of the app and the worker (`src/lib/security/startup-calendar-sync-check.ts`). Operator steps: [`docs/deployment.md`](deployment.md), "Calendar sync".
- **The workspace's switch** (decision 234): Settings → Calendar sync, "Allow members to sync their calendars" (`PUT /api/t/:slug/calendar-sync/settings`, `integrations.manage`: Owners and Admins). Off, every connection of the workspace is torn down. The page shows how many members are connected per provider, never who.
- **Microsoft 365's admin consent** (decision 237): "Authorise Microsoft 365" (`POST …/calendar-sync/microsoft-consent`, `integrations.manage`) answers the v2 `/adminconsent` URL for the sign-in app registration with the calendar scope (`https://graph.microsoft.com/Calendars.ReadWrite`), and `/api/integrations/calendar/admin-callback` records the customer directory's id (a GUID, CHECKed) and who granted it, audited `CALENDAR_MICROSOFT_CONSENT_GRANTED`. "Withdraw" (`DELETE`, through the undo toast) stamps the withdrawal and tears down every Microsoft connection, audited `CALENDAR_MICROSOFT_CONSENT_REVOKED`. Google needs no workspace consent.
- **The member** (decision 235): anyone who holds `work_items.view` connects their own calendar, Viewers included as a read-only feed (nothing is read back from it: see "The read-back"); a guest connects nothing (decision 204). Every member route is keyed off the caller (`ctx.userId`), never a user id in the request. The section is on Inbox → Notification preferences, offered by the server (`calendarSyncSectionOffered`).

## Connecting a calendar

`POST /api/t/:slug/calendar-sync/connect?provider=google|microsoft` (`work_items.view`) refuses 409 `CALENDAR_SYNC_OFF`, `PROVIDER_NOT_CONFIGURED`, `MICROSOFT_NOT_AUTHORISED`, `CALENDAR_DISCONNECTING` (a teardown still running) or `NO_PUBLIC_ORIGIN`, and otherwise answers `{ authorizeUrl }`, which the browser navigates to (a top-level navigation: the CSP is unchanged), with the sealed state cookie.

- **The apps are the sign-in apps** (decision 236): `GOOGLE_*` and `MICROSOFT_*`. The flow is hand-written (`src/app-layer/integrations/providers/calendar/token.ts`, `src/app-layer/usecases/calendar-connection.ts`) and imports nothing from the auth library (ADR 0004 A3); it writes no `Account`, links no identity, redeems no invite and reads no address. Its redirect URIs are its own (`/api/integrations/calendar/callback`, and for Microsoft `/admin-callback`), built on `NEXT_PUBLIC_APP_URL` through `appLink`, never from the request's host.
- **Scopes** (decision 228), constants in `token.ts`: Google `https://www.googleapis.com/auth/calendar.app.created` (only calendars the app created), Microsoft `offline_access Calendars.ReadWrite`. Never `openid`, `email` or `profile`; Google's URL sets `access_type=offline` and `prompt=consent` and never `include_granted_scopes`. Microsoft's authority is the consented directory.
- **The state** (`src/lib/calendar-sync/oauth-state.ts`): `<base64url JSON>.<HMAC-SHA256>` under `deriveSubkey('islestone-calendar-oauth-state-v1')`, carrying a random nonce (the `state` the provider echoes), the workspace's slug, the user's id, the provider and the PKCE verifier (S256); an HttpOnly, `SameSite=Lax` cookie scoped to `/api/integrations/calendar`, 10 minutes (`isl_cal_connect`, and `isl_cal_admin` for the consent).
- **The callback** (`/api/integrations/calendar/callback`, a signed-in path, neither public nor a machine caller): nothing is stored unless the cookie verifies, its nonce equals the returned `state`, the state's user is the session's (a state minted for another person or workspace is refused), and the nonce was never used (claimed once in Redis for 10 minutes; an unreachable Redis refuses). It asks `requirePermission('work_items.view')` for the state's workspace (an audited 403 otherwise), records a provider `error` by its code only, then the usecase asks the switch and the consent again, exchanges the code with the verifier, refuses a consent without a refresh token, creates the "Islestone" calendar (a reconnect reuses the one it has), stores the connection (a reconnect replaces the token and clears a revocation) and queues the first run. Audited `CALENDAR_CONNECTED` with the provider and the granted scope count, never a token. The browser returns to the preferences page with `?calendar=<outcome>` from a closed list.

## The model

`prisma/schema/calendar-sync.prisma`; migrations `20261218130000_calendar_sync_enums` and `20261218130100_calendar_sync`.

- `TenantCalendarSettings`: one row per workspace (no row: sync off): the switch and Microsoft's consent. `app_user` holds no DELETE on it.
- `CalendarConnection`: one per member, workspace and provider, keyed to the user, not the membership (a removed member's events are still deleted with the token this row holds). `tokenEncrypted` is in the encryption manifest under the workspace's DEK (decision 238), so both key rotations re-encrypt it; it is present while CONNECTED, NULL once REVOKED (CHECKs), and destroyed with the row after a teardown. `revokedReason` is `ACCESS_WITHDRAWN` (a refused call) or `GRANT_INVALID` (a refused refresh). `syncCursor` is Google's `syncToken` for the read-back. States: CONNECTED, REVOKED, DISCONNECTING, CALENDAR_REMOVED (the Islestone calendar was deleted at the provider, 3.3c: the token kept, the calendar's id and cursor cleared).
- `CalendarSyncedEvent`: one per connection and work item or milestone (two partial uniques; the kind names exactly one, by CHECK). No foreign key to the item or the milestone: the record outlives an item's purge until its remote event is gone. The connection's key is NO ACTION, so a connection is deleted only after its events. `clientEventId` is 40 lowercase hex (CHECK); `contentHash` 32 hex over exactly what is pushed; `pushedDueDate` what was pushed (the read-back's check, decision 230); `remoteVersion` the provider's etag or changeKey after Islestone's own write (the read-back's "this is our own write"); `lastError` a code (`UNCONFIRMED`, `PROVIDER_ERROR`, `NOT_FOUND`, `REFUSED` for a change the read-back did not take), never a provider body. `REMOVED_IN_CALENDAR` is an event the member deleted in their calendar (3.3c).

## The push

One function, `generateDesiredEvents` (`src/app-layer/usecases/calendar-sync-run.ts`), decides what leaves, under the member re-resolved when the run happens (`resolveMemberContext`; a member who left or is no longer active, or a workspace where sync is no longer allowed, gets the teardown, never a system context):

- **Items** (decision 226): their open items (Backlog, Unstarted, Started) assigned to them, due from 30 days ago to 365 days ahead (today in `NOTIFICATIONS_TZ`), in live, visible, unarchived projects; earliest due first, at most 1,000 (`WorkItemRepository.listAssignedForCalendarSync`, plan-checked); above it the member's section says "1,000 of 1,240 items are in your calendar", the count being the read's.
- **Milestones** (decision 227): the dated milestones in the same window of the live, visible, unarchived projects where the member has at least one open assigned item (dated or not), at most 200, for a member who holds `projects.view`; read-only (the read-back reverts a moved one).
- **What an event carries** (decision 229): the item's title or the milestone's name exactly, and one absolute `appLink` as the whole description (`/t/<slug>/work-items/<KEY>`, `/t/<slug>/projects/<projectKey>/milestones/<id>`); an all-day event on the due date (Google `start.date`/`end.date` exclusive; Graph `isAllDay` with midnight in UTC), private, free, no location, attendees or reminder overrides; a private extended property with the client id (Google `extendedProperties.private.islestone`; Graph a single-value extended property, and `transactionId`). Nothing else of the workspace leaves.
- **The diff** is the pure, property-tested `reconcileSyncedEvents` (`src/app-layer/domain/calendar-sync.ts`, ported from inflect-compliance): insert what has no row, patch a changed or failed row, delete a row no longer wanted (deletes are driven by the rows). An unchanged calendar makes no provider call. An event deleted in the calendar is not pushed again until its due date changes or the member resyncs (decision 232).
- **Duplicates** (H3): the client id is deterministic per (workspace, connection, kind, id). An insert is recorded BEFORE it is sent (no remote id, `UNCONFIRMED`) and confirmed straight after; a row with no remote id is looked up by its client id before it is inserted (or deleted) again, so a crash between the provider's write and the confirmation leaves one event. Google keeps a deleted event's id reserved: a 409 finds it and restores it (`status: confirmed`).
- **Failures**: an event's failure is recorded on its row (`FAILED`, or `PENDING_DELETE` for a delete, whose row is kept) and the run goes on. A refused credential (`400 invalid_grant` and its kin on refresh, 401 or 403 on a call) is terminal: REVOKED, the token destroyed, the remaining rows `PENDING_DELETE`, one `CALENDAR_CONNECTION_REVOKED` as the workspace system user, `NonRetryableJobError`, no further call. A throttle (429, or Google's 403 with a rate reason) is not.
- **Cadence** (decision 233): `calendar-sync-dispatch` every 15 minutes, and `enqueueCalendarSync` / `enqueueCalendarSyncForItems` after a commit that changes what a calendar shows (a create with an assignee, a title or due-date edit, an assignment, a state change, a delete or restore, a bulk change, a reschedule, a move; a milestone's change or delete and a project's archive, delete or restore queue the workspace's connections, at most 500). They never throw and touch Redis only when a connection is concerned. Job ids are colon-free and bucketed (a minute, a quarter hour). See [`docs/jobs.md`](jobs.md).

## The read-back (3.3c; decisions 225, 227, 230, 231, 232 and 235)

Each connection's run (`runCalendarSync`) reads the calendar back FIRST (`readBackCalendar`, `src/app-layer/usecases/calendar-read-back.ts`), then pushes, so a change Islestone does not take is put back by the same run, and the push never overwrites a day it has not read.

- **What is read** (`src/app-layer/integrations/providers/calendar/reader.ts`): the Islestone calendar only, and of it only the events Islestone recorded (matched by remote id; an event the member added by hand is ignored, never imported), and of each only whether it was deleted, its version and when it ends. Google: `events.list` with the stored `syncToken` (deleted events included), page by page through `nextPageToken`; the last page's `nextSyncToken` is stored as `syncCursor`; a `410 Gone` resets to a full listing. Microsoft: `calendarView` over the push window (a day wider at each end), times asked in UTC, every `@odata.nextLink` followed only on `https://graph.microsoft.com` through `safeFetch` (`provider-next-link.ts`); a recorded event the window does not hold is asked for by id (at most 50 a run) before anything is decided. At most 25 pages a listing; a listing cut short decides only what it read.
- **Islestone's own write is never a change**: an event whose version equals the row's `remoteVersion` is skipped. An event whose day is still the pushed day changed something else (a title, the link, attendees): not read (decision 225).
- **The day** (decision 231; `eventLastDay` in `src/app-layer/domain/calendar-read-back.ts`, pure): an all-day event's last day (its exclusive end minus one); a timed or multi-day event's end in the event's own zone (Google `end.timeZone`, Graph `originalEndTimeZone`, IANA or a Windows name mapped through `WINDOWS_TIME_ZONES`), an end at exactly midnight belonging to the day before; a zone neither names falls back to the offset the provider wrote the time in.
- **A moved day on an item event** is applied as the member, re-resolved, through `updateWorkItem` with `{ via: 'calendar', provider }`: `work_items.edit`, the field set (`FIELD_HIDDEN`), `START_AFTER_DUE`, `PROJECT_ARCHIVED`, `ITEM_CLOSED` (refused for a calendar change only), the item's `version` (a conflict read once more). Audited `WORK_ITEM_UPDATED` naming `dueDate` with `via: 'calendar'` and the provider in `detailsJson` (and the dates in `metadata`); the due bell and the watcher fan-out run as for any edit. **Only while the item's due date still equals the row's `pushedDueDate`** (decision 230). The row then records the new day and the event's version; its content hash is left, so the push rewrites the event in Islestone's shape (all-day, its title and link).
- **Not applied** (a stale change, a refused edit, and every moved milestone event, decision 227): the row is marked `FAILED` / `REFUSED`, so this run's push puts Islestone's date back, and the member gets one in-app `CALENDAR_CHANGE_NOT_APPLIED` (`src/app-layer/notifications/calendar-change.ts`): "Your calendar change to ISL-12 was not applied: the due date changed in Islestone" (or "you cannot edit this item", "this item's type does not show a due date", "the item is closed", "the new due date is before its start date", "its project is archived"), "Milestones cannot be moved from your calendar: Launch". It names the item's key or the milestone's name, never text; in-app only, never email (`IN_APP_ONLY_TYPES`, the CHECK `NotificationPreference_in_app_only_types`), under the member's preference; deduplicated per event and changed day (`clientEventId` and the day in the dedupe key), not per run. An item gone from the member's sight is not notified: its event leaves the calendar at the push.
- **A read-only feed** (decision 235): a member who holds no `work_items.edit` (a Viewer, or a custom role without it) gets nothing read back: no change applied, nobody notified, no inbound metric. The calendar is still listed, so a moved event is put back by the push and a deleted one is not pushed again.
- **A deleted event** (decision 232): the item or milestone is untouched; the row moves to `REMOVED_IN_CALENDAR` and the event is not pushed again until its due date changes or the member chooses "Resync my calendar". A row whose delete Islestone was retrying is simply dropped.
- **The whole Islestone calendar deleted** (a listing or an insert answering 404, `CalendarGoneError`): the connection moves to `CALENDAR_REMOVED` (the token kept, the calendar's id and cursor cleared), its rows are dropped, audited `CALENDAR_REMOVED_AT_PROVIDER` as the system user, and nothing runs until the member chooses "Recreate" or "Disconnect".
- **"Resync my calendar" and "Recreate"**: `POST /api/t/:slug/calendar-sync/connections?provider=… { action: 'resync' | 'recreate' }` (`work_items.view`, the caller's own connection, `actOnCalendarConnection`). Resync (a CONNECTED connection) drops the rows of events deleted in the calendar and clears the cursor, audited `CALENDAR_RESYNCED`; Recreate (a CALENDAR_REMOVED connection) puts it back to CONNECTED with no calendar, audited `CALENDAR_RECREATED`, and the run creates a new "Islestone" calendar. Each queues a run; a connection in another state is 409 `CALENDAR_CONNECTION_STATE`.
- **The metric** `calendar.sync.inbound` (`applied`, `refused`, `stale`, `milestone_reverted`, by provider; never a workspace or a person); `calendar.sync.outcome` gains `calendar_removed`.
- **The cursor** is written only when it changed and the listing named an event (or none was stored): every connection write is audited by the Prisma audit extension, so an unchanged calendar writes nothing.

## Disconnecting and teardown (decision 239)

- A member disconnects (`DELETE …/calendar-sync/connections?provider=…`, through the undo toast): the connection moves to DISCONNECTING (audited `CALENDAR_DISCONNECTED`) and `calendar-teardown` deletes every recorded event remotely first, then the Islestone calendar only if it is empty, then the token with the connection, after its rows. A failed remote delete keeps its row and the teardown retries (the job, then the dispatcher every quarter hour).
- `removeTenantMember` moves the member's connections to DISCONNECTING in its transaction (audited `CALENDAR_CONNECTIONS_TORN_DOWN_ON_MEMBER_REMOVAL`) and queues the teardowns after the commit; the teardown runs as the workspace system user and touches nothing but events Islestone created. A deactivated member is torn down by their next run.
- The switch turned off and Microsoft's consent withdrawn tear down every affected connection the same way.
- A token the provider refuses cannot delete anything: the teardown then finishes locally (the token destroyed, the rows and the connection deleted) and the member's copy says to remove the Islestone calendar themselves. A calendar already deleted at the provider finishes locally too: its events went with it.
- **Islestone never calls Google's revoke endpoint** (decision 236): with one app for sign-in and calendar, revoking a token may withdraw the person's whole grant. Tokens are destroyed locally; the copy tells the member how to remove Islestone from their Google account, and that it also affects signing in with Google. `tests/guards/calendar-sync-egress.test.ts` refuses the endpoint anywhere in `src/`.
- **A rule for 8.3**: a workspace or user deletion must run the teardown before its cascade (the connection's key from its events refuses a user delete while events remain).

## Egress

Four fixed origins through one module (`provider-http.ts`: the URL checked against the list, a plain `fetch` with no redirects, a timeout, a capped body read): `oauth2.googleapis.com`, `www.googleapis.com`, `login.microsoftonline.com`, `graph.microsoft.com`. One server-supplied URL is followed: Graph's `@odata.nextLink`, only when it is https on exactly `https://graph.microsoft.com` under `/v1.0/`, only through `safeFetch` (`provider-next-link.ts`, an SSRF sink row in `tests/guards/ssrf-egress-coverage.test.ts`), read by the same `interpretProviderResponse`. Google pages by token on Islestone's own URL. Browsers only navigate to `accounts.google.com` and `login.microsoftonline.com`. Google and Microsoft are sub-processors ([`docs/sub-processors.md`](sub-processors.md)). Logs carry the provider, the HTTP status and a code, never a token, a code, a title, a link or a provider body.
